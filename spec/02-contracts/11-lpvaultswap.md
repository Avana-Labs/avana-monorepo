# 2.11 LPVaultSwap

> **Status:** In review · **Owner:** Avana Labs · **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-12
>
> **Requirement prefix:** `REQ-SWAP-NNN`, `REQ-FLASH-NNN`, `REQ-HELPER-NNN` (flash liquidation helper) · **Formula prefix:** `SPEC-LIQ-NNN`

## Purpose

LPVaultSwap coordinates every liquidation of an Avana account. There is one LPVaultSwap per chain, at a fixed address, and it serves every Hub, CDP Spoke and market of that chain; every cap, Coverage Spoke and swap route it uses is keyed by the market's Hub, Spoke, market or debt reserve (`Q-348`). For one borrower account (one PositionProxy, which holds exactly one collateral market) and one debt reserve per call it:

1. checks the caller against the address freeze only, and the price states of the seized market and the repaid debt reserve;
2. pulls up to `debtToCover` of the debt asset itself from the caller and measures what arrived; it takes no flash loan in a liquidation (`Q-333`, `Q-404`);
3. calls the CDP Spoke's standard `liquidationCall` with `receiveShares = false` and the measured funding as Aave's bound; the seized vault tokens arrive at LPVaultSwap, the only address the vault token's transfer rule admits for a seizure;
4. has the AaveAdapter burn the seized vault tokens (`burnSeized`) and then Collateralization debit the borrower's records and unwind exactly the seized units through the market's AMM integration, which returns `n` token amounts;
5. swaps each released token into the debt asset over the market's swap routes, every leg with an oracle-derived minimum output in the debt asset, and enforces the recovery floor (closing a gap with the caller's own debt asset when the caller allows it);
6. pays out in the debt asset: Avana's fee (20 % of the oracle gain, never above the realized surplus), the borrower surplus, and the rest to the caller, which includes the funding Aave did not use and the recovered principal (`Q-337`).

GHO appears only when the debt reserve is GHO. No liquidation path mints GHO: a GHO debt is funded with GHO the caller holds or borrows from the chain's Aave V3 Pool through the flash liquidation helper, like any other debt asset. There is no purchase of the debt asset with GHO and no sale of a leftover back to GHO: the caller funds in the debt asset, so whatever Aave does not pull returns to the caller in the asset it funded.

`liquidate(params)` is the one entry point, open to any address that is not frozen, with either route mode (`PrimaryThenBackup` or `BackupOnly`); there is no second entry point and no keeper check (`Q-404`). A caller that holds no debt asset uses the flash liquidation helper ([Flash liquidation helper](#flash-liquidation-helper)), a public, immutable periphery contract outside the core: it flash-borrows the debt asset from the chain's Aave V3 Pool, calls `liquidate`, and repays the Pool from the payout. LPVaultSwap sees the helper as an ordinary caller. There is no in-kind settlement (`Q-401`) and no settlement budget (`Q-397`): the caller sizes `debtToCover` for the repayment Aave's dust rule requires, and because Aave pulls the debt out of the caller's own funding, a recovery a few units below the debt repaid lowers only the caller's payout. A payout to `feeRecipient`, the borrower, or a residue or dust leg to the caller that its recipient cannot receive goes to the payout escrow (`PayoutEscrow`), credited to that recipient, and never reverts the liquidation ([2.18](18-payout-escrow.md), `Q-214`, `Q-389`); the caller's payout itself is paid directly to `msg.sender` or the call reverts, never escrowed. The keeper list is a registry of committed operators who liquidate every account below health factor 1, whatever its size and whether or not the bonus covers the gas; it gates no call and is read for `keeperFloor`, the borrowing halt at zero committed operators and monitoring (`Q-381`, `Q-395`, `Q-400`, `Q-404`). LPVaultSwap also settles, for any caller, the pending-settlement balance Collateralization keeps for a seizure an earlier implementation never settled (`Q-201`), sending the proceeds to the Coverage Spoke of the market's (Hub, settlement asset) (`Q-338`); and runs the swap side of a borrower's repay with collateral (`deleverage`, `Q-255`), the only LPVaultSwap path that takes a flash loan, through the core flash source. It reports nothing to the circuit breakers: seizure volume and route failures are monitoring alerts read from its events, not on-chain counters (`Q-399`).

Liquidation is never paused by an Avana switch. LPVaultSwap never reads the MarketRegistry's `PauseState`, its pause reason or any circuit breaker: `liquidate` and `settlePending` run in None, Frozen, `Paused(General)` and `Paused(MintPathSuspect)` alike, on every Hub, with any breaker tripped (`Q-241`, `Q-252`, `Q-399`). The address freeze applies only to the caller of `liquidate` (`REQ-SWAP-105`) and to a new committed operator in `addKeeper` (`REQ-SWAP-107`); an address that calls through the public helper reaches LPVaultSwap as the helper, so a freeze cannot stop it from liquidating. What stops a liquidation is the account itself (health factor, price states, grace), Aave (a halted Spoke, a paused reserve, a halted Hub asset, `REQ-SWAP-055`, `Q-346`) or the settlement legs (no usable swap route, recovery floor), because a liquidation that cannot run lets losses grow while every exit stays open.

It is not responsible for:

- sizing the repayment or the seizure (Aave V4 `LiquidationLogic` does that, with its target health factor, dynamic bonus and dust rule);
- burning vault tokens (the AaveAdapter is the only burner, [2.6](06-aave-adapter.md)), debiting backing records, reconciling unsettled seizures or holding the pending-settlement balance (Collateralization, [2.3](03-collateralization.md)), or calling an AMM protocol to remove liquidity (the LPVault of the market's AMM integration);
- computing prices or price states ([2.10 LP Oracle](10-lp-oracle.md), the DebtOracle);
- storing markets, debt reserves, swap routes or the pause state (MarketRegistry, [2.8](08-market-registry.md)) or risk parameters (ParameterStore, [2.9](09-parameter-store.md));
- reporting or covering deficits (Aave reports them inside `liquidationCall`; the Coverage Spoke of each (Hub, debt asset) covers them, [2.14](14-first-loss-reserve.md)); LPVaultSwap only adds pending-settlement proceeds to a Coverage Spoke through its public `fund`;
- executing swap-route legs ([2.12 Settlement Routes](12-settlement-routes.md));
- holding settlement funds of its own: no settlement budget exists ([2.13](13-settlement-budget.md), `Q-397`);
- holding payouts their recipient could not receive ([2.18 Payout Escrow](18-payout-escrow.md));
- funding a liquidation: the caller funds it, from its own balance or through a flash liquidation helper ([Flash liquidation helper](#flash-liquidation-helper));
- lending flash liquidity for `deleverage` (the `IFlashSource` implementation and the Aave V3 Pool behind it, [Flash source](#flash-source)).

LPVaultSwap is a UUPS proxy (ERC-1967) behind a fixed address per chain. The address is an immutable of every vault token, Collateralization, the AaveAdapter, the PayoutEscrow, the swap-route contracts, the flash source and the flash liquidation helpers, and it is the only seizure recipient the vault token admits, so it never changes.

### Notation

Every settlement amount of a call is in base units of the debt asset `T` of the repaid reserve `r` (`dd` decimals, "debt units"); a pending settlement uses the asset of the market's pending-settlement reserve. Prices are in Aave's 8-decimal USD format. Basis points (bps) use 10⁴ = 100 %. `⌊·⌋` rounds down, `⌈·⌉` rounds up; every product that can exceed 256 bits uses OpenZeppelin `Math.mulDiv` with the stated rounding.

The debt reserve is USDC (`dd = 6`, `Pdebt = 100,000,000`); the vault token has `dv = 18` and `Pc = 210,000,000` ($2.10); the tokens are USDC at $1.00 and USDT at $0.9995, both 6 decimals; the account's bonus is `b = 10,500` and `lf = 0`. ## Inputs

- From any address that is not frozen, directly or through a flash liquidation helper: `liquidate(params)` with the borrower's PositionProxy, the collateral market, the debt reserve, `debtToCover`, a route mode, optional per-token unwind minimums `minAmounts`, a `deadline`, a minimum payout `minPayout` and `maxShortfall`; plus `debtToCover` of the debt asset pulled by `transferFrom`, measured as `Kf`.
- From any address: `settlePending(marketId, debtReserveId)`.
- From the caller of a liquidation with `maxShortfall > 0`: up to `maxShortfall` of the debt asset, pulled after the swaps to close a gap below the recovery floor (`SPEC-LIQ-019`).
- From the AaveAdapter, while it holds the lock for a borrower's `repayWithCollateral`: `deleverage(request)` and the tokens Collateralization releases from the borrower's record (`REQ-SWAP-106`).
- From the flash source, inside `deleverage` only: `F` of the debt asset and the `onFlashLoan` callback ([Flash source](#flash-source)).
- From the CDP Spoke (via the Hub): `c` vault-token units of the seized collateral.
- From the market's LPVault, on Collateralization's instruction: the `n` token amounts the unwind of exactly the seized units returns, principal only (fees stay with the position); from Collateralization in `settlePending`: the tokens of the pending-settlement balance.
- From the swap routes: the debt asset and any unconsumed input or intermediate tokens.
- Reads: MarketRegistry market, debt-reserve and swap-route records, the address-freeze set; ParameterStore parameters; LPOracle and DebtOracle price states (after `poke()`); AaveOracle prices and sources; Spoke account data, bonus and reserve configuration; Hub share and asset totals; the vault token's proxy registry; the lock; in `deleverage`, the flash source's liquidity and premium quotes.
- From governance (the chain's AvanaTimelock as `APPLICATION_ADMIN`): changes to the committed-operator registry and upgrades. The flash source `deleverage` uses is recorded on the MarketRegistry (`setFlashSource`, normal delay, `REQ-FLASH-006`).

## Outputs

- Borrower debt repaid on the CDP Spoke in the debt reserve's asset and vault collateral seized; a deficit reported by Aave, per debt reserve in its own asset on its funding Hub, when the call exhausts the account's collateral.
- Seized vault tokens burned from LPVaultSwap by the AaveAdapter, records debited by Collateralization, exactly the seized units removed by the LPVault.
- Avana's fee `A` to `feeRecipient`; the borrower surplus `B` to the borrower's wallet; `keeperOut` to `msg.sender`. A payout of `A`, `B`, a route residue or a dust leg whose transfer fails is deposited in the payout escrow for its recipient (`REQ-SWAP-076`); `keeperOut` is transferred directly or the call reverts. All payouts are in the debt asset except route residues and dust legs, which are returned in the token they were released as.
- In `settlePending`: stranded vault tokens burned; the matching pending tokens swapped into the market's pending-settlement asset and added to the Coverage Spoke of the market's (Hub, that asset) through `fund`; route residue to `feeRecipient`.
- In `deleverage`: the borrower's debt repaid, the record's units withdrawn, burned and unwound, the flash principal and premium `F + φ` returned to the flash source, and the debt asset left after the flash repayment sent to the borrower's wallet.
- Events listed in [Events](#events).
- No balance or allowance left in LPVaultSwap after any call, apart from the vault tokens of an unsettled seizure awaiting `settlePending` and the `carry` of pending-settlement proceeds worth less than one Hub share (`REQ-SWAP-070`).

## Storage

ERC-7201 namespaced storage at `avana.storage.LPVaultSwap`; the implementation constructor calls `_disableInitializers`. The implementation holds the roles, the committed-operator registry, the views and the dispatch; the bodies of `liquidate`, `settlePending`, and `deleverage` with `onFlashLoan` live in three immutable logic contracts (`LPVaultSwapLiquidationLogic`, `LPVaultSwapPendingLogic`, `LPVaultSwapDeleverageLogic`) that the implementation runs by delegatecall in the proxy's storage, with the calldata unchanged and the return or revert data passed back unchanged. Each logic contract is an implementation immutable built with the same immutables as the implementation; the implementation's constructor reverts with `InvalidLogic` unless each reports its kind and `keccak256(abi.encode(immutables))`, and with `ZeroAddress` for a zero address. A body reached other than through the proxy (the implementation's own address or a logic contract's) reverts with `NotDelegated`. A change of any body is therefore a new implementation behind `upgradeToAndCall` at the normal delay. Why: the settlement steps every path shares (price rules, legs, residues, delivery) make one implementation larger than EIP-170 allows; a split by entry point keeps each deployed contract inside it without a library link or a second proxy.

|---|---|---|

| `marketId` | `uint256` | The proxy's collateral market |
| `debtReserveId` | `uint256` | Debt reserve to repay, approved on the CDP Spoke |

## Permissions

| Function | Who | Delay | Cancel |
|---|---|---|---|
| `liquidate` | Any address that is not frozen (`REQ-SWAP-105`); the registry is not read | None | None |
| `settlePending` | Any address | None | None |
| `onFlashLoan` | The current `flashSource`, with an active deleverage context | None | None |
| `deleverage` | AaveAdapter, with the lock held by the AaveAdapter (`NotAdapter`) | None | None |

## Entry checks

- `REQ-SWAP-001` `liquidate` MUST NOT check `msg.sender` against the committed-operator registry or any role: any address may call it, and its only caller check is the address freeze (`REQ-SWAP-105`). The caller is always `msg.sender`: LPVaultSwap pulls its funding from that address and pays `keeperOut` to it; no caller or recipient address is taken from calldata, so a front-runner copying the calldata must fund the call itself and gains only what the original caller would have. Through a helper, `msg.sender` is the helper. `settlePending` has no caller check either. Why: every check that protects Avana and the borrower (health factor, price and grace gates, Hub share check, recovery floor, per-leg minimums, fee, surplus) runs on every call, so restricting the caller adds no protection and could only leave an account unliquidated (`Q-404`). (Source: Q-116, Q-119, Q-213, Q-242, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-002` `liquidate` and `settlePending` MUST acquire the Lock before any state-changing external call and release it as their last step, reverting with `LockHeld(holder)` if it is held. The caller pull, the route calls, the AaveAdapter and Collateralization calls and the shortfall pull run only while LPVaultSwap holds the Lock, except inside `deleverage`, where the AaveAdapter holds it and LPVaultSwap's flash callback and route calls accept it as the holder (`REQ-SWAP-106`). `liquidate` takes no flash loan, and one account per call keeps every reentry path closed; a helper's flash callback wraps the whole `liquidate` call and holds no Lock of its own. The Lock is transient (EIP-1153) and released at the end of each call, so it never serializes liquidations across transactions (`SPEC-LIQ-021`). (Source: Q-119, Q-148, Q-223, Q-251, Q-255, Q-344)
- `REQ-SWAP-003` LPVaultSwap MUST NOT read the MarketRegistry's `PauseState`, its pause reason or any circuit breaker: `liquidate` and `settlePending` run in None, Frozen, `Paused(General)` and `Paused(MintPathSuspect)` on every Hub, with any breaker tripped, a market in `Offboarding`, configuration drift, an exhausted credit line or a frozen Aave reserve. Only LPVaultSwap's entry checks, the price states of the seized market and the repaid reserve (`REQ-SWAP-008`, `REQ-SWAP-091`) and Aave-side halts or pauses (`REQ-SWAP-055`) stop it. Why: Frozen, Paused and the breakers stop new exposure; liquidation removes exposure, and stopping it would only let an unhealthy account's loss grow into a deficit. Example: the Emergency Guardian pauses the Stable LP Hub on Base with `Paused(MintPathSuspect)` at 14:00; a `liquidate` call of an account at HF 0.97 at 14:05 executes exactly as it would in None. (Source: Q-227, Q-118, Q-140, Q-241, Q-242, Q-252, Q-270)
- `REQ-SWAP-004` `liquidate` MUST revert with `UnknownMarket` unless `params.marketId` is a market in the MarketRegistry whose record names the Hub, CDP Spoke, collateral reserve, vault token and AMM integration used for the call, and these MUST match the vault token's immutables (`hub()`, `spoke()`, `marketId()`). It MUST revert with `UnknownProxy` unless `vaultToken.isValidProxy(params.proxy)`, the registry the AaveAdapter writes when it first supplies the proxy, which binds one wallet, one CDP Spoke and one market. Markets in `Registered` or `Offboarding` status can still be liquidated. (Source: Q-116, Q-224, Q-225, Q-227)
- `REQ-SWAP-005` `liquidate` MUST revert with `InvalidAmount` if `debtToCover == 0`, if `debtToCover` or `maxShortfall` exceeds `type(uint128).max`, or if `minAmounts` is neither empty nor of length `n`. Caller inputs are bounds, never exact-match values: Aave caps the repayment at `Kf` and LPVaultSwap measures `Kf`, `D` and `c` by balance delta, so a 1-wei repay or a new pledge between preview and inclusion changes the amounts, not the outcome. Aave V4's dust rule can raise the repayment needed to finish a liquidation (a partial repayment that would leave debt or collateral below the reserve's dust threshold must repay the whole debt), so the caller sizes `debtToCover` for that amount up front (`Q-397`); the part Aave does not pull returns to it inside `keeperOut`. Keepers size `debtToCover` as a full close quoted by the Lens as `⌈total × (10⁴ + LENS_DUST_MARGIN_BPS) / 10⁴⌉`, or at least `LENS_DUST_MARGIN_BPS` below Aave's dust boundary, and set `minPayout` to the funding plus the previewed net payout less at least 1 % of that net payout, `minPayout ≤ X + ⌊0.99 × (keeperOut_preview − X)⌋`; on Ethereum they submit through private order flow (`Q-345`). Why: a borrower who repays 1 wei, or repays enough to leave the debt just under the dust threshold, can otherwise make a keeper's exactly sized call revert every block at the cost of a small repay. Example: the Lens previews a whole debt of 1,950 USDC with a 500 bps margin, `debtToCover = ⌈1,950 × 1.05⌉ = 2,047.5` USDC; a 1-wei repay lands first, Aave pulls `D = 1,949.999999` USDC, and the 97.500001 USDC it did not pull returns to the caller inside `keeperOut`. (Source: Q-063, Q-119, Q-133, Q-251, Q-268, Q-345, Q-397, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-006` `liquidate` MUST revert with `NotLiquidatable` if `HF ≥ 1e18`, with `HF` read from the Spoke after the pokes of `REQ-SWAP-008` and `REQ-SWAP-091`: a poke can move the price Aave reads within the block (the LPOracle verifies token decimals only in `poke()`, `REQ-ORACLE-107`, so clearing a decimals mark turns the served accepted price into the fresh price), and the health factor this check, the grace check (`REQ-SWAP-009`) and the liquidation bonus use must be the one Aave applies in `liquidationCall`. Aave's own check inside `liquidationCall` still applies; LPVaultSwap never recomputes the health factor or the liquidation amounts. (Source: Q-116, Q-128, Q-232)
- `REQ-SWAP-007` `liquidate` MUST revert with `SharesNotEqualAssets` unless `Hub.getAddedAssets(vaultAssetId) == Hub.getAddedShares(vaultAssetId)` on the market's Hub. (Source: Q-189, Q-103)
- `REQ-SWAP-008` Collateral price rule (`Q-247`). Before funding, LPVaultSwap MUST call `poke()` on the market's LPOracle, so the first observation of a failure, a disagreement or a sequencer restart and the grace latch are stored before they are read (`Q-199`). It MUST revert with `StalePrice` if the market's price: (a) is reported `liquidationPaused`, which includes the chain's sequencer reporting down (`REQ-ORACLE-099`); (b) is in state Cached or Expired; (c) has an input source older than `liquidationPriceMaxAge` for that feed (the feed's own heartbeat plus its margin); or (d) has a token whose primary and alternate routes disagree, within `disagreementPauseMax` of the first stored observation; after that bound the market is treated as Expired for liquidation until the routes agree, a standby route is switched in or governance replaces the feed (`Q-208`, `Q-248`). LPVaultSwap gates seizure on the LPOracle the MarketRegistry records for the market whichever source the AaveOracle uses: a different AaveOracle source never reverts the call, the seized value uses the smaller of the two collateral prices (`SPEC-LIQ-004`), and the RiskModule's drift check reports the mismatch, which stops new activity, never liquidation (`REQ-ORACLE-101`, `Q-327`). Each PositionProxy holds exactly one collateral market, so no other market's price can shield or expose the account. Why: the seizure must be priced at a current, agreed price; a market that cannot be priced is valued at its stress price by its own LPOracle and waits. Example: the market's USDT feed has an 86,400 s heartbeat and a 1,800 s margin, so `liquidationPriceMaxAge = 88,200` s; a call 88,300 s after the last update reverts with `StalePrice`. Checked by: TEST-226, TEST-230, PROP-074. (Source: Q-086, Q-121, Q-128, Q-199, Q-208, Q-247, Q-248)
- `REQ-ORACLE-109` LPVaultSwap MUST call `poke()` on the LPOracle of the collateral market of the account it liquidates before it reads that market's price state (`REQ-SWAP-008`), so the first observation of a disagreement or failure and the grace latch are stored by every successful liquidation. (Source: Q-051, Q-129, Q-199, Q-209, Q-229)
- `REQ-SWAP-009` Grace latch. If the market's `getPriceState()` reports `inGrace` (a feed recovery, `recoveredAt ≤ block.timestamp < recoveredAt + graceDuration`, or on an L2 the sequencer grace after a restart, until `startedAt + sequencerGracePeriod`, `REQ-ORACLE-099`), `liquidate` MUST revert with `GraceHealthFactor` unless `HF < graceHealthFactor`. `settlePending` ignores grace and is refused only while `liquidationPaused` (`REQ-SWAP-066`). Example: Base's sequencer restarts at `t` with `sequencerGracePeriod = 3,600` s; at `t + 1,000` an account at HF 0.97 with `graceHealthFactor = 0.95` reverts, and one at HF 0.93 is liquidated. (Source: Q-088, Q-129, Q-154, Q-324, Q-357)
- `REQ-SWAP-010` No in-kind mode exists (`Q-401`): `RouteMode` has only `PrimaryThenBackup` and `BackupOnly`, and calldata with any other value reverts on ABI decoding. LPVaultSwap reads no in-kind window and no caller or governance action can make a liquidation pay out the released tokens instead of the debt asset. (Source: Q-133, Q-163, Q-227, Q-401)

## Funding

- `REQ-SWAP-011` LPVaultSwap MUST pull the caller's funding with `T.transferFrom(msg.sender, address(this), X)` before any other transfer and MUST measure the rise of its `T` balance as `Kf`, reverting with `FundingMismatch` unless `0 < Kf ≤ X`; `Kf = X` unless the asset charges a transfer fee, and every later step uses `Kf` (`REQ-SWAP-108`). Callers always fund in the debt asset of the reserve they repay. The caller is LPVaultSwap's only source of the debt asset in a liquidation. Example: `debtToCover = 2,047.5` USDC pulls 2,047.5 USDC, `Kf = 2,047,500,000`; Aave later pulls `D = 1,950` USDC and the 97.5 USDC left returns inside `keeperOut`. (Source: Q-063, Q-124, Q-333, Q-390, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-012` `liquidate` MUST NOT call the flash source or any lender, and MUST NOT measure a flash loan: no `F`, `φ` or flash transfer appears in a liquidation. A caller that needs the debt asset borrows it before calling, for example through the flash liquidation helper, whose flash callback wraps the whole `liquidate` call ([Flash liquidation helper](#flash-liquidation-helper)). Why: keeping the loan outside the core removes the flash callback, its context binding, the repayment loop and the premium terms from the audited liquidation code, and leaves the caller, who chooses the loan size, with every cost of it (`Q-404`). (Source: Q-063, Q-119, Q-334, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-013` `onFlashLoan(asset, amount, premium, data)` runs only inside `deleverage`. It MUST revert with `InvalidFlashCallback` unless `msg.sender == MarketRegistry.flashSource()`, the Lock is held by `AAVE_ADAPTER` with the deleverage context active (`REQ-SWAP-106`), `data` equals the hash of that context, `asset == T` and `amount == F` of that context, and LPVaultSwap's `T` balance rose since the call to `flashLoan` by a measured `Fr` with `0 < Fr ≤ amount` (`Fr = amount` unless the asset charges a transfer fee, `REQ-SWAP-108`; such a fee then stops the `deleverage` at Aave V4's repayment, `REQ-SWAP-123`); and with `FlashFeeTooHigh` if `premium × 10⁴ > maxFlashFeeBps × amount`. Before returning `keccak256("IFlashReceiver.onFlashLoan")` it MUST bring the flash source's `T` balance up to `amount + premium` by the transfers of `REQ-SWAP-122`, so no allowance is granted to it, and emit `FlashLoanUsed`. Every later step uses `Fr`, never `amount`, as the flash principal received. A call to `onFlashLoan` while LPVaultSwap itself holds the Lock (inside `liquidate`) reverts. (Source: Q-045, Q-119, Q-135, Q-255, Q-335, Q-390, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-014` Funding availability. A caller without the debt asset liquidates through the public flash liquidation helper or its own helper; when the Aave V3 Pool behind it is short, paused or charges above the caller's bound, the helper reverts before LPVaultSwap is called and the caller funds the debt itself or uses a smaller `debtToCover`. Each committed operator holds, on each chain, debt-asset inventory of at least the largest single `debtToCover` the caps of that chain's markets allow (`Q-336`). LPVaultSwap has no other funding source and no Hub draw capacity. Example: the Base V3 Pool holds about 0.13M idle GHO; the public helper refuses a 400,000 GHO loan before any state change, and Avana's keeper repeats the liquidation calling `liquidate` directly with 400,000 GHO of its own. (Source: Q-008, Q-063, Q-118, Q-336, Q-404)

## Flash source

`IFlashSource` is the chain's adapter to one flash lender, used only by `deleverage` (a liquidation never calls it, `REQ-SWAP-012`; liquidation callers borrow through the [flash liquidation helper](#flash-liquidation-helper), which calls the same Aave V3 Pool directly for every debt asset, GHO included). Its only implementation, `AaveV3FlashSource`, calls the chain's Aave V3 Pool `flashLoan(receiver, [asset], [amount], [0], receiver, params, 0)` with one asset and interest-rate mode 0, borrowing the debt asset itself. Aave V4 has no flash loan; the V3 Pool is live on Ethereum and Base, holds deep idle liquidity of the stable debt assets the Aave DAO may approve (for example about 200M USDC and 33M GHO on Ethereum, about 18M USDC on Base), and its `flashLoan` (unlike `flashLoanSimple`) waives the premium for an address holding the Pool's `FLASH_BORROWER` role. A frozen V3 reserve can still be flash-borrowed; a paused or deactivated reserve, `flashLoanEnabled = false`, a premium increase or a drain of idle liquidity stops it, and the checks below turn each of these into a clean revert before the loan (`Q-334`, `Q-335`).

```solidity
interface IFlashSource {
    function flashLoan(address asset, uint256 amount, address spokeHub, bytes calldata context)
        external returns (uint256 premium);
    function maxFlashLoan(address asset) external view returns (uint256 available); // 0 if paused, inactive or flash-disabled
    function flashPremium(address asset, uint256 amount) external view returns (uint256 premium);
    function LENDER() external view returns (address lender);               // pool or Hub the loan draws from
    function LP_VAULT_SWAP() external view returns (address lpVaultSwap);   // the only borrower
    function PARAMETER_STORE() external view returns (address parameterStore); // holds maxFlashFeeBps
}
```

- `REQ-FLASH-001` `AaveV3FlashSource` MUST hold the immutables `LENDER` (the chain's Aave V3 Pool), `LP_VAULT_SWAP` and `PARAMETER_STORE`, MUST have no owner, setter, sweep or arbitrary-call function, MUST end every call with no allowance, and MUST never forward to LPVaultSwap a balance it held before the loan (`REQ-FLASH-003`). Its constructor reverts with `ZeroAddress` if any of the three is `address(0)`. `flashLoan(asset, amount, spokeHub, context)` MUST, in order, revert with `NotLPVaultSwap` unless `msg.sender == LP_VAULT_SWAP`, with `FlashLoanActive` while a loan of this source is open (a call from inside `onFlashLoan`), with `FlashSourceIsHub` if `LENDER() == spokeHub`, with `InsufficientFlashLiquidity(available)` if `amount > maxFlashLoan(asset)`, and with `FlashFeeTooHigh(premium, maxPremium)` unless `flashPremium(asset, amount) × 10⁴ ≤ maxFlashFeeBps × amount` (exact, no division; `maxPremium = ⌊maxFlashFeeBps × amount / 10⁴⌋`); then open a transient context `(asset, amount, keccak256(context))` with a snapshot of its own balance of `asset`, call `LENDER.flashLoan(address(this), [asset], [amount], [0], address(this), context, 0)`, revert with `InvalidFlashCallback` unless the Pool ran the callback of `REQ-FLASH-003` for this context, read the premium the callback recorded, clear the context, set the Pool's allowance back to 0 if the Pool pulled less than it was approved for, emit `FlashLoanServed(asset, amount, premium)` and return the premium. The context lives in transient storage, so it is empty at the start of every transaction and a reverted loan leaves nothing behind. `amount = 0` passes these checks and the source skips its own transfer of zero; the Aave Pool transfers zero amounts itself, so with a token that reverts on a zero transfer such a loan reverts inside the Pool. LPVaultSwap never requests `F = 0` (`deleverage` reverts `DeleverageTooSmall` first, `REQ-SWAP-106`). `LENDER()` names the pool or Hub whose liquidity the loan draws, never an intermediate contract: the V3 Pool for `AaveV3FlashSource`, and for any later source that borrows through a V4 Spoke, the Hub behind that Spoke. LPVaultSwap refuses the source on every `deleverage` call, and `setFlashSource` refuses it, when `LENDER()` is any Hub registered on the chain, that is when `MarketRegistry.isRecordedHub(LENDER())` is true (`REQ-FLASH-005`, `REQ-FLASH-006`, `Q-386`, `Q-388`). `maxFlashLoan(asset)` returns 0 unless the reserve is active, not paused and flash-enabled, and otherwise `min(IERC20(asset).balanceOf(aToken), getVirtualUnderlyingBalance(asset))`. The Pool lends only its virtual underlying balance (V3.1+ virtual accounting: the loan lowers it with checked arithmetic, and a direct transfer to the aToken does not raise it), and only what the aToken holds can be transferred out, so the smaller of the two is the real ceiling and a loan above it is refused by the source with `InsufficientFlashLiquidity`, never by a Pool revert. Example: 18,000,000 USDC supplied and 50,000 USDC sent straight to the aToken: `maxFlashLoan = 18,000,000` USDC. `flashPremium(asset, amount)` returns the source's quote of the premium the Pool charges it for `amount` at the pinned Pool version: 0 while the source holds `FLASH_BORROWER`, read as `isFlashBorrower(address(this))` on the ACLManager that `LENDER.ADDRESSES_PROVIDER().getACLManager()` names (the lookup the Pool makes in `flashLoan`), else the ceiling `⌈amount × FLASHLOAN_PREMIUM_TOTAL / 10⁴⌉`, computed with a full-width `mulDiv` so no amount overflows; it is non-decreasing in `amount`. V3.4 rounds half-up (`percentMul`, `⌊(amount × FLASHLOAN_PREMIUM_TOTAL + 5,000) / 10⁴⌋`), which is never above the quote, so the source's ceiling quote is the binding gate of the pre-loan check and the callback re-checks the premium actually charged. Example: 1,950 USDC at 5 bps without the role: `⌈1,950,000,000 × 5 / 10⁴⌉ = 975,000`; with the role: 0. (Source: Q-334, Q-373)
- `REQ-FLASH-002` Before calling `flashLoan`, LPVaultSwap MUST revert with `FlashLiquidityShort(available, F)` if `F > flashSource.maxFlashLoan(T)` and with `FlashFeeTooHigh` if `flashSource.flashPremium(T, F) × 10⁴ > maxFlashFeeBps × F` (`SPEC-LIQ-015`; the exact form, with no division and no rounding, that the flash source, the ParameterStore and the Lens also use). Why: utilization of the V3 reserves runs at 76–95 %, so idle liquidity moves with every borrow in the same block; reading it and the premium at each call makes a short or repriced source fail before any collateral is touched, and the borrower retries `repayWithCollateral` with fewer units or repays from its own funds. (Source: Q-335, Q-135)
- `REQ-FLASH-003` `AaveV3FlashSource.executeOperation(assets, amounts, premiums, initiator, params)` MUST revert with `InvalidFlashCallback` unless `msg.sender == POOL`, `initiator == address(this)`, `assets`, `amounts` and `premiums` each have length 1, a context opened by `flashLoan` is active and its callback has not run yet, and `assets[0]`, `amounts[0]` and the hash of `params` match that context. It then re-checks the premium the Pool charges, `premiums[0] × 10⁴ ≤ maxFlashFeeBps × amounts[0]` (else `FlashFeeTooHigh(premiums[0], maxPremium)`), records the premium and marks the callback as run before any transfer, transfers `r₁`, the rise of its balance of the asset over the snapshot `flashLoan` took before calling the Pool (what the Pool delivered; `r₁ = amounts[0]` unless the asset charges a transfer fee; 0 if the balance did not rise, and then skipped), to `LP_VAULT_SWAP`, calls `LP_VAULT_SWAP.onFlashLoan(assets[0], amounts[0], premiums[0], params)`, requires the magic value `keccak256("IFlashReceiver.onFlashLoan")` (else `InvalidFlashCallback`) and its own balance of the asset to be at least `owed = amounts[0] + premiums[0]` (else `FlashRepayShort(balance, owed)`), `forceApprove`s `POOL` for exactly `owed` (the Pool pulls it when `executeOperation` returns) and returns `true`. The premium re-check makes the source itself refuse a premium above the cap, whichever receiver it serves; LPVaultSwap repeats it (`REQ-FLASH-004`). The source never transfers a fixed amount it may not hold: with a transfer fee it forwards what it received, and LPVaultSwap tops its balance up to `owed` by balance delta (`REQ-SWAP-122`). A balance the source held before the loan, for example a donation, is never forwarded: it stays in the source and, because LPVaultSwap tops the source up to `owed` by balance delta, counts toward the repayment of the next loan, which lowers LPVaultSwap's transfer by the same amount. LPVaultSwap therefore always receives `0 < Fr ≤ amounts[0]` (`REQ-SWAP-013`), and a donation can neither block the flash path nor reach LPVaultSwap. Why: authenticating the Pool, the initiator and the context stops a third party from calling `executeOperation` with a forged loan, and the second check in `REQ-SWAP-013` stops a third party from calling LPVaultSwap's callback; forwarding the measured delivery instead of `amounts[0]` means the source never transfers an amount it does not hold when the Pool's transfer arrives short (`Q-390`; a transfer fee on the debt asset then stops the `deleverage` at Aave V4's repayment, `REQ-SWAP-123`), and measuring it against the snapshot keeps a donation out of `Fr`, which LPVaultSwap refuses above `amounts[0]`; with no sweep, a forwarded donation would block every later flash loan of that asset. Example: with USDT's fee at 10 bps, a 5,000 USDT loan arrives at the source as 4,995 USDT, the source forwards 4,995 and LPVaultSwap receives `Fr = 4,990.005` USDT; a 1-unit USDC donation stays in the source, the next 1,000 USDC loan forwards exactly 1,000 USDC, and LPVaultSwap's repayment is 1 unit smaller. (Source: Q-045, Q-335, Q-390)
- `REQ-FLASH-004` LPVaultSwap's `onFlashLoan` checks are `REQ-SWAP-013`; the premium it accepts is the one the Pool reports in the callback, re-checked against `maxFlashFeeBps`, so a premium raised between the quote and the loan in the same block fails closed. (Source: Q-135, Q-335)
- `REQ-FLASH-005` On every `deleverage` call LPVaultSwap MUST revert with `FlashSourceIsHub()` if `MarketRegistry.isRecordedHub(flashSource.LENDER())` is true, that is, if the pool or Hub the source borrows from is any Hub recorded on the chain (any tier; the market's home Hub, the reserve's funding Hub or another market's Hub), and it passes the CDP Spoke's home Hub as `spokeHub`, so the flash source itself also refuses a loan from that Hub (`REQ-FLASH-001`). The check compares the source's reported liquidity owner with the chain's Hub list, never the source's own address or an intermediate Spoke's. Why: a flash loan drawn from a Hub that funds the repaid reserve, or from any Hub that shares liquidity with it, would repay that Hub's debt with that Hub's own liquidity, inside a Hub that has no reentrancy guard, and a halt of that Hub would stop both the funding and the repayment at once; a source that borrows through a Spoke has a Spoke address, so an address equality against one Hub would admit it. The launch source is the Aave V3 Pool, which is no recorded Hub; the check binds every later implementation. Example: a later source that flash-borrows through a Spoke of the Stable LP Hub on Base reports `LENDER()` = that Hub; the Hub is recorded on Base, so `setFlashSource` refuses it, and if it were installed by a Hub recorded afterwards, every `deleverage` on Base would revert `FlashSourceIsHub()` until governance replaced the source, while borrowers repay from their own funds. Liquidation is unaffected: it takes no flash loan, and the helper's Pool is checked against the Hub list at the helper's deployment (`REQ-HELPER-002`). Checked by: MATH-ST-110. (Source: Q-335, Q-388)
- `REQ-FLASH-006` The chain's flash source is recorded on the MarketRegistry: `MarketRegistry.setFlashSource(newSource)` is callable only by `APPLICATION_ADMIN` (timelock, normal delay, cancellable by every `CANCELLER_ROLE` holder) and reverts with `InvalidRoute` unless `newSource.LP_VAULT_SWAP()` is this LPVaultSwap and `isRecordedHub(newSource.LENDER())` is false; `recordHub(hub)` reverts with `InvalidRoute` if `hub == flashSource().LENDER()` ([2.8](08-market-registry.md)). LPVaultSwap has no setter and no storage for it: it reads `MarketRegistry.flashSource()` on every `deleverage` call and repeats the Hub check of `REQ-FLASH-005` per call. While a replacement waits its delay, borrowers whose `repayWithCollateral` the current source cannot serve repay from their own funds; liquidation does not depend on the source. Why: a replaceable adapter lets Avana move to a later flash facility, for example one on a V4 Hub that Avana does not record on that chain, without touching settlement code; the normal delay gives reviewers time to simulate the new source on a fork; the setter check and the per-call check together refuse a Hub-backed source whichever is recorded first. (Source: Q-336, Q-379, Q-388)
- `REQ-FLASH-007` The deployment payload of each chain requests, by AIP on that chain's Aave V3 market, the `FLASH_BORROWER` role for the chain's `AaveV3FlashSource` address and for the chain's public flash liquidation helper. Until it is granted, `deleverage` and helper loans pay the Pool premium (5 bps), which `maxFlashFeeBps` (launch 10 bps) admits because the cap sits strictly above the rate (`SPEC-LIQ-015`); once both grants are in place the premium is 0 and governance may lower the cap to 0 with `tightenMaxFlashFeeBps(0)`; the helper's grant waives the premium on every asset it borrows, GHO included, because the Pool lends them all (`REQ-HELPER-015`). A replacement source or helper needs its own grant. (Source: Q-334, Q-404)
- `REQ-FLASH-008` `enableDebtReserve` on a chain sizes the reserve's `drawCap` so that the largest single liquidation the market caps allow (`debtToCover` at Aave's close factor on the largest account the caps allow) fits within half of what the chain's public flash liquidation helper can borrow of `T`, read as the helper's `maxFlashLoan(T)` at listing: the Aave V3 Pool's idle liquidity of `T` (the flash source's `maxFlashLoan(T)`), for GHO as for every other asset (`REQ-HELPER-015`); monitoring alerts when the helper's `maxFlashLoan(T)` falls below twice that size, and the committed operators' inventory of `REQ-SWAP-014` covers the gap. Example: on Base the idle USDC is about 18M; a USDC debt reserve whose largest single liquidation is 2M USDC passes with 4.5× headroom, while a GHO debt reserve on Base, with about 0.13M idle GHO in the Pool, is capped at a largest liquidation of 65,000 GHO or is liquidated from the operators' own GHO; on Ethereum a GHO debt reserve is sized against the Core Pool's idle GHO in the same way, so with about 33M GHO idle a largest liquidation of up to 16.5M GHO passes. (Source: Q-336)
- `REQ-FLASH-009` The flash source MUST NOT be named by any caller: `deleverage` reads only `MarketRegistry.flashSource()`, and no calldata of LPVaultSwap names a lender, a receiver or a callback target. (Source: Q-335)
- `REQ-FLASH-010` Neither the core flash source nor the flash liquidation helper uses the GHO flash minter: `deleverage` and the helper flash-borrow a GHO debt from the chain's V3 Pool GHO reserve like any other asset (on Ethereum the V3 Core GHO reserve is supplied by its direct minter and is flash-enabled; on Base the Pool lends Base's bridged GHO) (`REQ-HELPER-015`). Why: one lender per chain keeps one set of quotes, callback checks and failure modes for every asset, and the Core GHO reserve already holds deep idle GHO, so a second lender would add code and a dependency without adding liquidity the liquidations need. (Source: Q-334, Q-404)

## Liquidation call

- `REQ-SWAP-015` Before `liquidationCall`, LPVaultSwap MUST call `Collateralization.beginSeizure(proxy, vaultToken)` so Collateralization snapshots the proxy's supplied shares and the vault token's total supply under the Lock. (Source: Q-160, Q-165, Q-189, Q-224)
- `REQ-SWAP-016` LPVaultSwap MUST `forceApprove` the CDP Spoke for exactly `Kf` of `T`, call the standard Aave V4 `Spoke.liquidationCall(collateralReserveId, debtReserveId, proxy, Kf, false)` and set the allowance to 0; `Kf = X` unless an issuer fee applies (`REQ-SWAP-108`). It MUST NOT pass `receiveShares = true`, choose the repay amount, or reimplement Aave's target-HF, bonus or dust logic; Aave sizes `D` and `c`, and LPVaultSwap reconciles by delta. The CDP Spoke is a standard, unmodified Aave V4 Spoke: the listing AIP sets `receiveSharesEnabled = false` and `liquidationFee = 0` on every Avana collateral reserve and no reinvestment controller, and Avana's drift check enforces them. (Source: Q-060, Q-063, Q-068, Q-116, Q-119, Q-222, Q-271)
- `REQ-SWAP-017` `D` and `c` MUST be measured as balance deltas across `liquidationCall`. `liquidate` MUST revert with `NothingSeized` if `c == 0`, so LPVaultSwap never repays debt without seizing collateral. `D ≤ Kf` always holds, because Aave pulls `D` out of the `Kf` LPVaultSwap approved. The debt asset Aave did not pull, `Kf − D`, stays in LPVaultSwap's balance and returns to the caller inside `keeperOut`; it needs no swap because it is already the settlement asset. (Source: Q-124, Q-127, Q-131, Q-144, Q-333, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-018` When the seizure exhausts the account's collateral, Aave records the deficit inside `liquidationCall`, for every debt reserve of the account in its own asset on its funding Hub; LPVaultSwap MUST NOT report, adjust or cover deficits itself, and the settlement still meets the recovery floor. If the settlement reverts, the deficit report reverts with it. (Source: Q-070, Q-117, Q-134, Q-166, Q-236)
- `REQ-SWAP-019` Every LPVaultSwap measurement MUST be a per-call delta or a per-call snapshot. The vault token admits a seizure transfer only from the CDP Spoke's Hub to LPVaultSwap, so another address's `liquidationCall` on an Avana account reverts on the token transfer and cannot change the account between calls. (Source: Q-144, Q-116, Q-222)

## Unwind and swaps

- `REQ-SWAP-020` After `liquidationCall`, LPVaultSwap MUST call `AaveAdapter.burnSeized(vaultToken, c)` and then `Collateralization.settleSeizure(proxy, vaultToken, c, minAmounts)`, which returns the `n` amounts, in the same call. Collateralization checks that the proxy's share drop, the token's supply drop and `c` are equal, debits that account's records in the market in list order from the head (at most 16 records per account per market; `SPEC-BACK-006`), and has the market's LPVault remove exactly `c` units through the AMM integration's unwind, which returns `n` token amounts to LPVaultSwap, principal only, with no per-token minimum of Avana's own; `minAmounts` is the caller's optional bound (empty means 0 for every token) and can only make the call revert. `settlePending` passes none and `deleverage` passes the request's minimums. If the AaveAdapter or Collateralization reverts, the whole liquidation reverts. (Source: Q-061, Q-131, Q-160, Q-165, Q-170, Q-189, Q-224, Q-240)
- `REQ-SWAP-021` The token amounts `a₁…aₙ` used for swaps and dust legs MUST be the balance deltas of the market's `n` tokens across `settleSeizure`; uncollected fees never enter them. A token that is the debt asset `T` itself is not swapped: its delta counts in `R` directly. (Source: Q-061, Q-011, Q-124)
- `REQ-SWAP-022` In `PrimaryThenBackup` mode, for each released token `i ≠ T` with `aᵢ > 0` that is not a dust leg (`REQ-SWAP-121`), LPVaultSwap MUST call the market's primary swap route from that token into `T` if it is usable, as `swap(aᵢ, minOutᵢ)` with `minOutᵢ` from `SPEC-LIQ-018`, inside `try/catch`; if it is not usable or reverts (including `MinOutNotMet`), it MUST reset the allowance to 0 and call the backup with the same `minOutᵢ`. A route is usable iff `MarketRegistry.getRoute(marketId, token, T, kind)` returns `usable = true`. `BackupOnly` calls only the backup. Before calling a route LPVaultSwap reads its `intermediates()` to track their residues (`REQ-SWAP-024`); a route whose read reverts counts as a route that reverted (the primary emits `RouteFailed` and the backup is tried). If no usable route swaps a token, the call MUST revert with `NoRouteAvailable`. Route rules are in [2.12](12-settlement-routes.md). (Source: Q-062, Q-084, Q-151, Q-187, Q-227, Q-250, Q-339)
- `REQ-SWAP-023` Before each route call LPVaultSwap MUST `forceApprove` that route for exactly the input amount and set the allowance to 0 after the call, whether it succeeded or reverted. (Source: Q-119, Q-124, Q-342)
- `REQ-SWAP-024` Input or intermediate tokens a route returns unswapped count as zero toward `R` and toward the recovery floor; each non-zero residue MUST be paid to `msg.sender` by the delivery of `REQ-SWAP-076` (escrowed for `msg.sender` if the transfer fails) before `liquidate` returns. The residue of a token is LPVaultSwap's balance of it after the last route call less its balance at the start of the recovery window (just before `burnSeized`, for each of the market's tokens) or just before the first route call that names it among its `intermediates()` (for any other token); the debt asset is never a residue, because what arrives in it is recovery. A dust leg is the residue of its token, since it is never swapped (`REQ-SWAP-121`); together they leave LPVaultSwap's balance of every market and intermediate token unchanged over the call (`REQ-SWAP-042`). The aggregate floor therefore values a residue paid to the caller at zero: a route that returns part of its input must still bring `R + shortfall` to `minRec` in the debt asset alone, and each leg's `minOutᵢ` is set on the whole input `aᵢ`, so a residue never stands in for recovery. A caller can still move value from `R` into a residue it receives, within each leg's `Vlegᵢ − minOutᵢ`; that lowers `S`, and with it `A` and `B`, exactly as a sandwich of its own route does (`SPEC-LIQ-009`), and never lowers the floor. Example: a USDT leg of 4,998 USDT with `minOut = 4,946.030395` USDC returns 4,946.1 USDC and 0.5 USDT unswapped; `R` counts 4,946.1 USDC, and the 0.5 USDT goes to the caller and counts 0. (Source: Q-097, Q-124, Q-213, Q-217)
- `REQ-SWAP-025` In `PrimaryThenBackup` and `BackupOnly` modes, `liquidate` MUST revert with `RecoveryBelowFloor` if `R + shortfall < minRec` (`SPEC-LIQ-005`, `SPEC-LIQ-006`, `SPEC-LIQ-019`). No pool spot price gates a liquidation (`Q-350`). The unwind of the seized units uses zero per-token minimums of Avana's own; the value it returns is checked at oracle prices after the unwind, by this floor over the whole unwind and by each leg's oracle-derived `minOutᵢ` (`REQ-SWAP-100`). A pool price moved away from the oracle never reverts a liquidation, never switches its route mode and never adds to the caller's loss. Why: fair composition at the oracle price vector is the minimum, over every pool spot price, of the oracle value of the tokens the position holds ([2.10](10-lp-oracle.md) `SPEC-ORC-007`), so removing liquidity at a manipulated spot price returns tokens worth at least their fair value at oracle prices; a spot move changes only the token mix, and the per-leg minimums price each token at the oracle. Example: a searcher pushes the USDC/USDT pool so the unwind of the example returns 2,051.25 USDT and no USDC; at $0.9995 those are worth 2,050.224375 USDC ≥ `V = 2,047.5`, and the single USDT leg runs at its own oracle minimum. Checked by: MATH-ST-106. (Source: Q-085, Q-131, Q-159, Q-198, Q-250, Q-251)

## Settlement math

The SettlementMath library is internal to LPVaultSwap (`Q-240`). It has one goal: a liquidation either recovers, at oracle prices less the slippage Aave's net bonus can pay for, the debt asset its caller funded, or it reverts. The caller funds the repayment, so the recovery floor protects Avana's fee and the borrower, and any rounding below the debt repaid comes out of the caller's own payout. Every leg carries its own oracle-derived minimum in the debt asset, and Avana's fee is measured on the oracle value of the seizure, so the caller's routing choices cannot reduce it while the realized surplus covers it. No liquidation formula contains a flash principal, flash premium or transfer-fee term: a helper's loan and premium are the helper's own (`REQ-SWAP-012`, `Q-404`). `deleverage` keeps its flash premium (`SPEC-LIQ-020`).

- `SPEC-LIQ-001` **Net bonus.** `bNet = 10⁴ + ⌊(b − 10⁴) × (10⁴ − lf) / 10⁴⌋` bps, with `b` and `lf` read before `liquidationCall` in the same call. Units: bps where 10⁴ means no bonus. Rounding: down (smaller bonus, smaller slippage, against the caller). Bounds: `b ≤ type(uint32).max`, `lf ≤ 10⁴`. Aave's configuration keeps `b ≥ 10⁴` and `lf ≤ 10⁴`; for `b ≤ 10⁴` or `lf ≥ 10⁴` the bonus term is 0 and `bNet = 10⁴`, so `s = 0` (`SPEC-LIQ-003`) and the floor is the full oracle value less the rounding terms. Without that rule a bonus below 10⁴ combined with a fee above 10⁴ would multiply two negative terms into a positive bonus. Why: Aave pays a liquidation fee out of the bonus to its fee receiver, so only the remainder absorbs swap loss. The listing AIP sets `lf = 0` and the drift check enforces it, so `bNet = b` while the setting holds; the formula keeps the floor safe if a fee appears before the breaker stops new activity. Example: `b = 10,500`, `lf = 0` give `bNet = 10,500`; `lf = 1,000` would give `bNet = 10⁴ + ⌊500 × 9,000 / 10⁴⌋ = 10,450`. Checked by: SYM-16a–b, MATH-ST-12, MATH-ST-113. (Source: Q-190, Q-159, Q-068, Q-222)
- `SPEC-LIQ-002` **No cost term.** A liquidation has no cost paid outside `R`: LPVaultSwap takes no flash loan (`REQ-SWAP-012`), so the effective slippage of `SPEC-LIQ-003` carries no premium term (the former `costsBps` is 0 on every call). A helper's premium is paid by the helper from the payout it receives, before its caller is paid (`REQ-HELPER-007`); swap-route fees are taken before `R` is measured and are covered by the slippage allowance. Units: bps. Why: the caller chooses whether and how much to borrow, so the cost of borrowing belongs to the caller, never to Avana's fee, the borrower surplus or the floor. Example: a helper that flash-borrows `F = 1,950` USDC at the 5 bps Pool premium pays `φ = ⌈1,950·10⁶ × 5 / 10⁴⌉ = 975,000` (0.975 USDC) out of the 2,020.8 USDC payout; LPVaultSwap computes the same `s`, `minRec`, `A` and `kr` as for a caller funding from its own balance. `deleverage` keeps its premium (`SPEC-LIQ-020`). Checked by: MATH-ST-13, MATH-ST-101, HYP-29. (Source: Q-159, Q-135, Q-190, Q-334, Q-404)
- `SPEC-LIQ-003` **Effective slippage.** `s = min(slippageAllowanceBps, ⌊10⁴ × (bNet − 10⁴) / bNet⌋)` if `bNet > 10⁴`, else `s = 0`. Units: bps of the seized oracle value. Rounding: down; rounding to nearest is not allowed (MATH-ST-11). Bounds: `10⁴ × bNet < 2⁵⁰`. It guarantees `bNet × (10⁴ − s) ≥ 10⁸`, so the slippage term of the floor alone covers the debt repaid `D` up to one vault unit plus 1 debt unit; the rounding terms of `SPEC-LIQ-005` widen that gap by at most `U + ε`, which comes out of the caller's payout (`SPEC-LIQ-010`). Why: a subtractive bound (`bonus − slippage`) overstates the room at large bonuses; the multiplicative form is the largest slippage for which the discounted seizure still covers `D`. Example: `s = min(100, ⌊10⁴ × (10,500 − 10,000) / 10,500⌋) = min(100, 476) = 100` bps; check `10,500 × 9,900 = 103,950,000 ≥ 10⁸`. A bonus of 10,050 would cap it at `⌊10⁴ × 50 / 10,050⌋ = 49` bps (`10,050 × 9,951 = 100,007,550 ≥ 10⁸`). Checked by: MATH-ST-06, MATH-ST-07, MATH-ST-09, MATH-ST-10, MATH-ST-11, SYM-41a–b, SYM-44a–b, SYM-47, HYP-29, HYP-38. (Source: Q-159, Q-190, Q-127, Q-085, Q-198)
- `SPEC-LIQ-004` **Seized oracle value.** `V = mulDiv(c × Pc, 10^dd, 10^dv × Pdebt)`, rounded down. Units: debt units; `c` in vault-token base units, `Pc` and `Pdebt` 8-decimal USD. Both prices are read in the same call as `liquidationCall`. `Pc` is the smaller of the AaveOracle price of the collateral reserve and the market's LPOracle `latestAnswer()`, and `Pdebt` the larger of the AaveOracle price and the DebtOracle's (GHO: Aave's fixed $1.00); while the AaveOracle sources are the recorded oracles, as the drift check enforces, each pair is one read and equals the prices Aave used for the seizure (`REQ-SWAP-008`, `REQ-ORACLE-101`). Bounds: `c < 2¹²⁰`, `Pc < 2⁶⁴`, `10^dd ≤ 10¹⁸`; the numerator is below 2²⁴⁴. Every valuation of this page (`V`, `Vlegᵢ`, `Ulegᵢ`, `U`, `V_P`) reverts with `InvalidDecimals(d)` if a token's decimals exceed 36, then with `ZeroCollateralPrice` if the valued price (`Pc` or a leg price `pᵢ`) is 0, then with `ZeroDebtPrice` if the quote price (`Pdebt` or `Pp`) is 0. A zero collateral price would value the seizure at 0, drop the floor, Avana's fee and the keeper reward to 0 and turn every leg into a dust leg handed to the caller; a zero debt price has no finite value. Why: valuing the seizure at the prices Aave used means the floor measures swap loss only, never a price difference between Avana and Aave. Example: Aave repays `D = 1,950` USDC at a 5 % bonus and seizes `c = 975·10¹⁸` units; `V = mulDiv(975·10¹⁸ × 210,000,000, 10⁶, 10¹⁸ × 100,000,000) = 2,047,500,000` (2,047.5 USDC). Checked by: SYM-10a–c, SYM-31, MATH-ST-07, MATH-ST-09, MATH-ST-101. (Source: Q-085, Q-034, Q-100, Q-333)
- `SPEC-LIQ-005` **Recovery floor.** `U = Σᵢ ⌈pᵢ × 10^dd / (10^dᵢ × Pdebt)⌉` over the `n` released tokens (one raw unit of each token valued in debt units, at least 1; a token that is `T` counts 1), and `minRec = max(0, ⌈V × (10⁴ − s) / 10⁴⌉ − U − ε)`, with `ε = floorRoundingAllowance(T)`. The floor is tested on `R + shortfall`; `minRec` depends on no caller input. Units: debt units; `ε` is set per debt reserve worth about $1 (10⁶ for USDC, 10¹⁸ for GHO), so the rounding gap a caller may have to pay is bounded by a few dollars (`Q-267`). Rounding: the slippage term up (against the caller); each unit value up so it covers the one-unit unwind and swap truncation per token. Overflow: `V < 2²⁰⁰`; the subtraction saturates at 0. Why (`Q-198`): the unwind (amounts floored, then reduced by 1 raw unit) and every swap truncate, so even a perfect route can return up to one raw unit per token less than the oracle value, and fixed per-call rounding does not shrink with the size. Without the subtracted terms a dust liquidation would revert on every attempt and its deficit would never be recorded (FINDING-048, HYP-53). Any amount by which the recovery ends below `D` comes out of the caller's payout, because the caller funded `D` (`SPEC-LIQ-010`, `SPEC-SET-001`). Example (regular): `U = 1 + 1 = 2` (one USDC unit and one USDT unit at $0.9995 each round up to 1), the slippage term is `⌈2,047,500,000 × 9,900 / 10⁴⌉ = 2,027,025,000`, and `minRec = 2,027,025,000 − 2 − 1,000,000 = 2,026,024,998` (2,026.024998 USDC). Example (dust): `D = 3` USDC seizes `c = 1.5·10¹⁸`, `V = 3,150,000`, slippage term 3,118,500, `minRec = 2,118,498`; routes returning `R = 2.95` USDC pass, and the caller, which funded `Kf = 3` USDC, receives `keeperOut = 3 + 2.95 − 3 = 2.95` USDC, a 0.05 USDC loss (`SPEC-LIQ-010`). Checked by: SYM-40, MATH-ST-01, MATH-ST-06, MATH-ST-07, MATH-ST-101, QNT-27, HYP-29, HYP-53, SIM-06C, SIM-08B. (Source: Q-085, Q-159, Q-198, Q-251, Q-267, Q-333, Q-397)
- `SPEC-LIQ-006` **Recovered amount.** `R = T balance after the last route call − T balance immediately before LPVaultSwap calls burnSeized`. The window contains only the burn, the Collateralization release (including a released token that is `T`) and the swap-route calls; the caller funding `Kf`, the leftover `Kf − D`, the shortfall top-up and every payout are outside it, so prefunding or an oversized `debtToCover` cannot raise `R`. Units: debt units. Why: the floor must compare the debt asset produced by the seized collateral alone. Example: after Aave pulls `D = 1,950` USDC the balance is 0; the USDC leg releases 1,030.25 USDC and the USDT leg swaps to 1,010.05 USDC, so `R = 2,040,300,000`. A 5 USDC donation made before the call appears in both readings and leaves `R` unchanged. Checked by: MATH-ST-01, MATH-ST-02, MATH-ST-03, MATH-ST-04, QNT-27. (Source: Q-085, Q-124, Q-148, Q-333)
- `SPEC-LIQ-007` **No flash premium on the debt.** The term `φD` does not exist in a liquidation (`Q-404`): the oracle gain `G`, the surplus `S`, the fee `A`, the reward `kr` and the borrower surplus `B` are computed from `D` alone (`SPEC-LIQ-008`, `SPEC-LIQ-009`, `SPEC-LIQ-022`). A helper pays the premium on its whole loan, including any part of the loan the debt did not need, from the payout it receives (`REQ-HELPER-007`). Units: debt units. Why: the caller chooses whether to borrow and how much; charging Avana or the borrower for a loan, or for an oversized one, would let a caller drain them by over-borrowing, and removing the term removes that question from the core. Example: the Lens margin `X = 2,047.5` USDC borrowed whole through the public helper at 5 bps carries `φ = ⌈2,047.5·10⁶ × 5 / 10⁴⌉ = 1,023,750` (1.02375 USDC); LPVaultSwap pays the helper `keeperOut = 2,118.3` USDC, the helper repays `2,048.52375` USDC and pays its caller 69.77625 USDC, so the premium on the unused 97.5 USDC, 0.04875 USDC, falls on the caller alone. Checked by: SYM-45, MATH-ST-23, MATH-ST-51, MATH-ST-69, TEST-246. (Source: Q-190, Q-135, Q-267, Q-334, Q-404)
- `SPEC-LIQ-008` **Realized surplus.** `S = max(0, R_f − D)`, with `R_f = max(R, minRec) = R + max(0, minRec − R)`, the recovery plus the floor part of the caller's top-up. Units: debt units. Rounding: exact. Why: Avana's fee and the borrower surplus are charged only on gain the seizure realized after repaying the debt, never on caller capital; the floor top-up stands in for recovery the routes should have produced. Example: `S = 2,040.3 − 1,950 = 90.3` USDC; in the dust example `R = 2.95` USDC is above `minRec`, so `R_f = 2.95`, `S = max(0, 2.95 − 3) = 0`, and the caller's 0.05 USDC loss is not surplus. Checked by: MATH-ST-15, MATH-ST-16, MATH-ST-20, SYM-46a–d, SYM-49, HYP-30. (Source: Q-190, Q-097, Q-135, Q-333)
- `SPEC-LIQ-009` **Avana fee.** `A = min(⌊G × avanaFeeShareBps / 10⁴⌋, S)` with the oracle gain `G = max(0, V − D)`. Units: debt units. Rounding: down. Bounds: `avanaFeeShareBps ≤ 5,000` (launch 2,000); `V < 2²⁰⁰`. `A ≤ S`, so the fee never makes a liquidation revert, and while `S` covers it `A` depends only on the oracle value of the seizure and the debt repaid, never on how the caller swapped. The share is best-effort where the floor sits below `D + ⌊G × avanaFeeShareBps / 10⁴⌋`: when `s` is at the bonus cap of `SPEC-LIQ-003` (a bonus close to the listing minimum of `SPEC-LIQ-016`), or when `U + ε` is large against the gain (a liquidation of a few tens of dollars). A caller that routes its swaps at the floor, for example by moving the pool before and after its own call, then lowers `S`, and with it `A`, and keeps the difference through its own trades. Example: `b = 10,100`, `lf = 0`, `D = 1,000,000` USDC give `s = min(100, ⌊10⁴ × 100 / 10,100⌋) = 99`, `V = 1,010,000` USDC and `minRec = ⌈1,010,000 × 9,901 / 10⁴⌉ − 0.000002 − 1 = 999,999.999998` USDC against `D = 1,000,000`; routes returning `0.9995 V = 1,009,495` USDC pay `A = 2,000` and `kr = 7,495` USDC, while routes at the floor pay `A = 0` and `kr = 0` and the caller receives `Kf − 0.000002` USDC, and the caller's trades around the call take up to about 10,000 USDC less their pool fees. Avana accepts this: the share is exact on every market whose bonus keeps the floor above the fee (in the example below `minRec = 2,026.024998 ≥ D + A = 1,969.5` USDC), and the borrower's loss is fixed by Aave's bonus either way. Avana charges no fee on borrow interest (`Q-231`); this share is its only on-chain revenue, paid in the debt asset repaid. Why: measured on realized proceeds, the fee shrank whenever the caller swapped badly, so a caller that sandwiched its own swaps kept Avana's share; measured on the oracle gain, the fee is set by the bonus Aave paid. Example: `G = 2,047.5 − 1,950 = 97.5` USDC and `S = 90.3`, so `A = min(⌊97.5 × 0.2⌋, 90.3) = 19.5` USDC; a caller that sandwiched its own routes down to `R = 2,027` USDC (above `minRec`) has `S = 77` and still pays `A = 19.5`. Dust: `S = 0`, so `A = 0`. Checked by: MATH-ST-14, MATH-ST-16, MATH-ST-20, MATH-ST-101, SYM-46a–d, HYP-30, TEST-240, PROP-069. (Source: Q-097, Q-190, Q-196, Q-231, Q-256)
- `SPEC-LIQ-010` **Caller payout and conservation.** `keeperOut = (Kf + R') − (D + A + B)`, with `R' = R + shortfall` and `B` from `SPEC-LIQ-022`. Units: debt units. LPVaultSwap's `T` change over the call is exactly 0: `Kf + R + shortfall = D + A + B + keeperOut`. No settlement budget term and no flash term exist (`Q-397`, `Q-404`). The subtraction is never negative: `D ≤ Kf` because Aave pulls `D` out of the `Kf` it was approved for (`REQ-SWAP-017`), and `A + B ≤ S = max(0, R_f − D) ≤ max(0, R' − D)`, so `keeperOut ≥ min(Kf, Kf − D + R') ≥ 0`. The call still checks it and reverts with `InsufficientProceeds` if the first sum is smaller, a defensive check against a faulty balance measurement. When `R' ≥ D`, `keeperOut = Kf + kr`: the caller gets back all of its funding (the unused `Kf − D` and the recovered principal `D`) plus the keeper reward. When `R' < D`, `keeperOut = Kf − (D − R')`: the caller bears the difference, which the floor bounds by one vault unit's value plus `U + ε` and Aave's rounding of `D` (`INV-038`). `Kf`, `D` and `R` are measured amounts (`REQ-SWAP-108`), so the identity holds for whatever arrived; a transfer fee on `T` itself never reaches it, because Aave V4's Hub refuses the fee-reduced repayment inside `liquidationCall` (`InsufficientTransferred`) and the call reverts until the fee returns to 0 (`REQ-SWAP-123`). Bounds: every term below 2¹²⁹. Why: an exact balance identity means LPVaultSwap never keeps or creates the debt asset, and the caller is paid only what remains after Avana's fee and the borrower surplus. Example: `keeperOut = (1,950 + 2,040.3) − (1,950 + 19.5 + 0) = 2,020.8` USDC `= Kf + kr = 1,950 + 70.8`; `T` in `1,950 + 2,040.3 + 0 = 3,990.3` equals `T` out `1,950 + 19.5 + 0 + 2,020.8 = 3,990.3`. - `SPEC-LIQ-019` **Caller shortfall top-up.** With the call's `maxShortfall`, every leg runs at `sLeg = min(9,999, s + min(maxShortfallBps, ⌊maxShortfall × 10⁴ / V⌋))` (the added term is 0 when `maxShortfall = 0` or `V = 0`). - `SPEC-LIQ-021` **Liquidation throughput.** For a market with `N` liquidation calls queued after a price drop (accounts liquidatable × calls per account) and liquidatable value `L` (USD): `callsPerBlock = ⌊share × blockGasLimit / gasPerCall⌋`, capped at `⌊txGasCap / gasPerCall⌋` per transaction times the keepers' transactions per block, `depthPerBlock` = the USD the market's weakest swap route absorbs within `slippageAllowanceBps` per block (route depth at that allowance plus arbitrage refill per block), and the drain time `T_drain = blockTime × max(⌈N / callsPerBlock⌉, ⌈L / depthPerBlock⌉, ⌈Lflash / flashPerBlock⌉)`, where the last term is the part funded through flash liquidation helpers over the Aave V3 Pool's idle liquidity of the debt asset per block. `listCollateralMarket` and every raise of `marketUsdCap` require `T_drain ≤ liquidationDrainMaxSeconds` for the stress case of a 20 % drop at the 95th-percentile keeper gas share. Units: gas, calls, USD, seconds. Rounding: calls per block down, block counts up (against liveness). The Lock does not appear: it is transient and released at the end of each call, so separate transactions never contend for it and sequential calls in one keeper transaction each acquire and release it. Why: throughput is bounded by gas, route depth and flash liquidity, not by a lock, so a market's cap must be sized so that its own liquidations clear in time. - `SPEC-LIQ-022` **Keeper reward and borrower surplus.** With `S` from `SPEC-LIQ-008`, `G = max(0, V − D)` and `A` from `SPEC-LIQ-009`: the keeper reward is `kr = min(S, G) − A` and the borrower surplus is `B = S − A − kr = max(0, S − G)`. Units: debt units. Rounding: exact (both are differences of already-rounded terms). Then `A + kr + B = S`, `kr ≤ G − A`, `B = 0` whenever `S ≤ G` (in particular whenever the floor part of the top-up is positive), and when `S > G`, `B = R_f − max(V, D)`, the recovery above the oracle value of the seizure. `B` is paid to the borrower's wallet even when the same call leaves the account with a deficit (collateral exhausted and debt remaining): the deficit is the debt Aave could not cover at oracle prices and is covered under 2.14, while `B` is what the collateral produced above those prices; Avana does not net one against the other. `B` exists only when the caller's routes beat the oracle value; a caller that routes at or below it pays none. Why: Aave sizes the seizure at the oracle price, so the bonus it pays is `G`; the keeper's reward is bounded by that bonus after Avana's share, and value the collateral produced above its oracle value (a pool trading away from the oracle, a route better than the oracle) belongs to the borrower whose collateral produced it (`Q-337`). Checked by: MATH-ST-96, MATH-ST-97, MATH-ST-101, MATH-ST-104, SYM-180a–c. (Source: Q-337)

## Waterfall

- `REQ-SWAP-026` In `PrimaryThenBackup` and `BackupOnly` modes, after the last route call LPVaultSwap MUST, in this order: (1) compute `R_f = max(R, minRec)`, `S`, `G`, `A`, `kr` and `B`; (2) compute the settlement gap `g` and `shortfall` by `SPEC-LIQ-019` (reverting with `RecoveryBelowFloor`, or with the defensive `InsufficientProceeds`) and pull `shortfall` when positive; (3) compute `keeperOut` by `SPEC-LIQ-010` with `R' = R + shortfall`, reverting with `InsufficientProceeds` if it would be negative (defensive, `SPEC-LIQ-010`), then apply the minimum-payout check of `REQ-SWAP-075`; (4) pay `A` to `feeRecipient`; (5) pay `B` to the borrower's wallet (`AaveAdapter.walletOf(proxy)`; the proxy is logic-free, `Q-349`) and emit `BorrowerSurplusPaid`; (6) transfer `keeperOut` to `msg.sender` with a plain `safeTransfer`, so a failed transfer reverts the whole call; (7) pay route residues and dust legs to `msg.sender`; steps (4), (5) and (7) each use the gas-limited delivery of `REQ-SWAP-076`, which deposits the amount in the PayoutEscrow for its recipient when the transfer fails; (8) emit `LiquidationSettled`; (9) call `Collateralization.markDebtFree(proxy)` while still holding the Lock, which clears the account's indebted mark when Aave reports no borrow left (its last debt repaid, or the rest reported as deficit) and otherwise changes nothing ([2.3](03-collateralization.md) `REQ-COLL-040`), so a liquidation to zero debt needs no keeper `refreshDebtMark` before `closeMarket`; no breaker report is made (`REQ-SWAP-104`). Zero amounts are skipped (`REQ-SWAP-077`). Avana's fee is paid first, then the borrower surplus, then the caller; the First-Loss reserve funds nothing in a liquidation (`Q-071`). Why `keeperOut` is never escrowed: it contains the caller's returned funding, and a flash-funded caller repays its loan from it in the same transaction, so an escrowed payout would leave the loan unpaid and the transaction would revert anyway; reverting at once is the honest outcome, and the caller, who chose `msg.sender`, controls whether that address can receive the debt asset. Example: the routed example pays 19.5 USDC to `feeRecipient`, nothing to the borrower, and 2,020.8 USDC to the caller. (Source: Q-071, Q-097, Q-119, Q-127, Q-135, Q-190, Q-216, Q-241, Q-251, Q-267, Q-337, Q-389, Q-397, Q-399, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-027` The borrower keeps the collateral Aave did not seize and receives the borrower surplus `B` of `SPEC-LIQ-022` in the debt asset. No other borrower payout exists. (Source: Q-060, Q-061, Q-133, Q-337, Q-401)
- `REQ-SWAP-028` Avana's fee MUST be paid in the same call, in the debt asset, to the `feeRecipient` read from the ParameterStore, through the delivery of `REQ-SWAP-076`; LPVaultSwap MUST NOT accumulate fees. If `feeRecipient` is `address(0)`, the call MUST revert with `InvalidFeeRecipient`. If the transfer to `feeRecipient` fails (a token that blocks it, a recipient contract that reverts), the fee is deposited in the payout escrow credited to `feeRecipient` and the liquidation completes. (Source: Q-097, Q-124, Q-231, Q-389)
- `REQ-SWAP-029` No settlement budget exists (`Q-397`). No Avana contract pays any part of a liquidation: the caller funds `D` out of `Kf`, a recovery below `D` lowers only `keeperOut` (`SPEC-LIQ-010`), and the settlement gap `g` of `SPEC-LIQ-019` never exceeds the floor gap the caller tops up; `deleverage` sizes its repayment within the recovered value (`SPEC-LIQ-020`) and reverts with `DeleverageShortfall` otherwise. Why: the rounding a liquidation can leave below `D` is a few units of the debt asset, bounded by one vault unit plus `U + ε` and Aave's rounding of `D` (`INV-038`); the caller already holds the debt asset it funded and controls the sizing that produces the loss. Example: the dust liquidation's 0.05 USDC loss comes out of its caller's payout of 2.95 USDC on a funding of 3 USDC. (Source: Q-117, Q-127, Q-144, Q-190, Q-213, Q-267, Q-397)

## Payout delivery

- `REQ-SWAP-030` No in-kind settlement exists (`Q-401`). Every liquidation runs the routed path: entry checks, caller funding, snapshot, `liquidationCall`, burn, unwind, swaps at the per-leg minimums, the floor with the caller's top-up and the waterfall. A future path in which a caller funds the repayment up front and takes the recovered underlying tokens of a specific market would be a separate governance-authorized change, not part of this contract. (Source: Q-084, Q-133, Q-163, Q-190, Q-213, Q-401)
- `REQ-SWAP-031` The MarketRegistry holds no in-kind window, trigger or expiry, and LPVaultSwap has no `reportRecoveryShortfall` or `probeLiquidation` (`Q-401`). A market whose token's issuer has blocklisted LPVaultSwap or paused the token cannot be liquidated against that token's routes until the token recovers or governance replaces the routes; guardians freeze new borrowing on the market meanwhile and the token-issuer playbook applies ([3.6](../03-operations/06-emergency-operations.md), `Q-342`). (Source: Q-133, Q-163, Q-227, Q-251, Q-342, Q-401)
- `REQ-SWAP-076` Payout delivery. Every escrowable payout LPVaultSwap makes is delivered by this procedure: Avana's fee `A` to `feeRecipient`; each route residue and each dust leg to `msg.sender`; the borrower surplus `B` to the borrower's wallet; and the route residue of `settlePending` to `feeRecipient`. The caller's payout `keeperOut` is not escrowable: it is transferred to `msg.sender` with a plain `safeTransfer`, and a failure reverts the call (`REQ-SWAP-026`). (`deleverage` pays `assetOut` to the borrower who called it, by a plain transfer, since its Lock is held by the AaveAdapter.) For each non-zero payout of `amount` of `token` to `recipient`, LPVaultSwap MUST: (1) read its own balance of `token`; (2) revert with `InsufficientGas(gasleft(), required)` unless, immediately before the transfer, `gasleft() ≥ required = ⌈PAYOUT_TRANSFER_GAS × 64 / 63⌉ + POST_CALL_GAS`, with LPVaultSwap's own implementation constant `POST_CALL_GAS = 10,000`, the gas it keeps after the transfer returns for the balance read and, on a failure, the escrow deposit's setup (AMMN-GAS-04); (3) call `token.transfer(recipient, amount)` as a low-level call forwarding exactly `PAYOUT_TRANSFER_GAS` and copying no return data, and read its own balance again; (4) take the fall of its own balance, at most `amount`, as the amount that left (`sent`; 0 when the call reverted), whatever the call returned; (5) if `sent < amount`, `forceApprove` the payout escrow (`PAYOUT_ESCROW`) for exactly the undelivered `amount − sent`, call `PAYOUT_ESCROW.deposit(token, recipient, amount − sent)`, reset the allowance and report the payout as escrowed in its event. A transfer that reverts, returns `false`, runs out of gas, or returns `true` but moves less than `amount` out of LPVaultSwap is therefore escrowed for exactly what did not leave, and one that moves `amount` is delivered whatever it returns; a fee the token takes on the way to the recipient lowers what the recipient receives, never what LPVaultSwap escrows. A failed escrowable transfer never reverts the liquidation or the pending settlement; only a revert of the escrow deposit does, which is a token blocking the escrow itself. Copying no return data keeps a token that returns a large revert or return payload from consuming the gas the deposit needs. The credited recipient claims later from the escrow, to any address it names ([2.18](18-payout-escrow.md)). Why: a recipient whose address a token blocks (USDC and USDT blocklists, a blocklisted treasury Safe as `feeRecipient`), or a contract that reverts on receipt, could otherwise make every liquidation that owes it a payout revert, and a blocklisted `feeRecipient` would stop every routed liquidation with `S > 0` and every `settlePending` residue in that asset on the chain until a normal-delay `setFeeRecipient` executed (N2a). Example: `required = ⌈100,000 × 64 / 63⌉ + 10,000 = 101,588 + 10,000 = 111,588` gas per payout; Circle blocklists the treasury Safe that is `feeRecipient`; in the routed liquidation of the examples the transfer of `A = 19.5` USDC reverts inside its 100,000 gas, LPVaultSwap deposits 19,500,000 units in the payout escrow credited to the Safe, pays `keeperOut = 2,020.8` USDC to the caller and completes; had the caller's own address been blocklisted, the transfer of `keeperOut` would revert and the whole liquidation with it; the Safe later claims the credit to a new treasury address. Checked by: FV-LIQ-05, MATH-ST-28, MATH-ST-83, TEST-229. (Source: Q-214, Q-217, Q-133, Q-337, Q-389, Q-401)

## Committed operators

The keeper list on each chain's LPVaultSwap is a registry of committed operators. Each registered operator commits to liquidate every account below health factor 1 on that chain, whatever its size and whether or not the bonus covers the gas. The registry gates no call: `liquidate` is open to every address that is not frozen (`REQ-SWAP-001`). It feeds `keeperFloor`, the RiskModule's borrowing halt at zero active committed operators, and monitoring of how fast each account below HF 1 is liquidated. The function names (`addKeeper`, `removeKeeper`, `isApprovedKeeper`, `activeKeeperCount`, `getKeepers`, `KEEPER_FLOOR`, `MAX_KEEPERS`) and the events `KeeperAdded` / `KeeperRemoved` keep their names, so the timelock's delay classes and the RiskModule's read stay unchanged; "keeper" in them means committed operator.

- `REQ-SWAP-032` The committed-operator registry lives on each chain's LPVaultSwap and covers every Hub on that chain. At launch each chain's registry holds Avana's own keeper; others, such as Aave Labs or a risk provider, are added by `addKeeper`, up to `MAX_KEEPERS = 32` (`Q-400`). A registered operator has no power any other caller lacks: every caller uses `liquidate` with both route modes and every check. Avana's keeper liquidates every account, whatever its size, and repays whatever Aave allows per call; liquidation never depends on a keeper's profit: there is no minimum debt and no minimum repayment on any chain (`Q-381`, `Q-395`). The 16-record limit per account per market exists so that one liquidation of the largest account fits one transaction: `G(16) = 1,310,000 + 240,000 × 16 = 5,150,000 ≤ 16,777,216` gas ([1.5](../01-architecture/05-parameters.md) `SPEC-CHAIN-001`). (Source: Q-053, Q-082, Q-147, Q-155, Q-213, Q-348, Q-381, Q-395, Q-400, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-033` `addKeeper(operator)` registers a committed operator. It MUST be callable only by `APPLICATION_ADMIN` (the chain's timelock, normal delay, cancellable only by `DAO_CANCELLER_ROLE`, `Q-263`, because it restores liquidation capacity); it MUST revert with `InvalidKeeper` for `address(0)` or a registered operator, with `AddressIsFrozen(operator)` for a frozen address and with `TooManyKeepers(MAX_KEEPERS)` if 32 operators are already registered. The registered address is the operator's own: its key or Safe that calls `liquidate` or its per-operator helper (`REQ-HELPER-011`); the public helper is never registered, because it commits to nothing. Why: `activeKeeperCount` and `getKeepers` iterate the list, so a bound keeps them within a fixed gas cost. Example: the Ethereum governance Safe schedules `addKeeper(0x3b9f…e210)` for a risk provider on 2 March at 12:00 UTC; it executes on 7 March at 12:00 UTC, and from that block the operator counts toward `keeperFloor`; its `liquidate` calls were accepted before that block too, like anyone's. (Source: Q-048, Q-155, Q-228, Q-242, Q-263, Q-400, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-034` `KEEPER_FLOOR` is `keeperFloor = 1` (Avana's keeper at launch), counted per chain over active committed operators (`activeKeeperCount()`, `REQ-SWAP-107`). New borrowing halts only when zero active committed operators remain on a chain: the RiskModule then blocks new borrowing and withdraw-with-debt on every Hub of that chain (`REQ-SWAP-039`). Liquidation itself never halts on it: `liquidate` stays open to every address. Why: with no committed operator, no party is bound to liquidate accounts whose bonus does not cover the gas, so new debt must stop while the existing debt stays liquidatable by anyone. (Source: Q-139, Q-155, Q-227, Q-242, Q-400, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-035` `removeKeeper(operator)` MUST be callable only by `APPLICATION_ADMIN` at the short delay (12 hours), and MUST revert with `InvalidKeeper` if `operator` is not registered and with `KeeperFloor(activeAfter, KEEPER_FLOOR)` if `activeAfter < KEEPER_FLOOR`. Example: with 2 active committed operators, removing one leaves 1 and succeeds; removing the last reverts. (Source: Q-048, Q-139, Q-155, Q-228, Q-242)
- `REQ-SWAP-036` No operator suspension exists: an operator is registered or removed at the short delay. Registration grants no power over liquidation, so a misbehaving operator can do only what any caller can: liquidate accounts Aave deems liquidatable, through Aave's sizing, held to the floor and the per-leg minimums. (Source: Q-227, Q-228, Q-397, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-037` No operator or account quarantine exists; a key that should not act is removed from its Safe, and a guardian can freeze its address (`REQ-SWAP-107`). (Source: Q-227, Q-228)
- `REQ-SWAP-038` A removed operator returns to the registry only through `addKeeper` at the normal delay; removal never stops its `liquidate` calls, which stay open to it like to any address that is not frozen. (Source: Q-213, Q-228, Q-404)

## Governance

- `REQ-SWAP-039` No Avana switch halts liquidation (`REQ-SWAP-003`). Liquidation of an account is unavailable only when its price gates fail, an Aave-side halt or pause applies (`REQ-SWAP-055`), its Hub shares differ from assets, or no usable settlement path exists for its market (no usable swap route of some token into the debt asset, `Q-401`). In each case the RiskModule blocks new borrowing and withdraw-with-debt for accounts that use the affected market or debt reserve (`LiquidationHalted`, or its price-gate, Aave-halt and shares-equal-assets errors, [2.7](07-risk-module.md)), so no account can add risk that cannot be liquidated. While zero active committed operators remain on the chain (`REQ-SWAP-034`) it blocks them on every Hub of the chain, although liquidation stays open to every address, because no party is then bound to clear accounts whose bonus does not cover the gas. Repay and debt-free withdrawal stay open where Aave permits. (Source: Q-118, Q-164, Q-189, Q-213, Q-227, Q-229, Q-242, Q-346, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-040` LPVaultSwap MUST be a UUPS proxy (ERC-1967) whose `_authorizeUpgrade` requires `APPLICATION_ADMIN`, held only by the chain's AvanaTimelock (normal delay, cancellable by any `CANCELLER_ROLE` holder, 14-day grace period). `initialize(timelock, initialKeepers)` grants both admin roles to the timelock only, registers the launch committed operators, and `_grantRole` MUST revert with `AdminRoleOnlyTimelock(role, account)` for any other account. CI diffs the ERC-7201 storage layout before every upgrade payload; reviewers check the implementation's bytecode hash and simulate the payload on a fork. An upgrade keeps the address, which is the only seizure recipient the vault tokens admit. (Source: Q-043, Q-090, Q-119, Q-226, Q-228, Q-259)

## Restrictions

- `REQ-SWAP-041` One account (one PositionProxy, one market) and one debt reserve per call; no batching inside a call and no flash loan in `liquidate`. An account with several debt reserves is liquidated by sequential calls; keepers batch accounts with separate transactions or a multicall of separate calls, each acquiring and releasing the Lock. (Source: Q-098, Q-119, Q-166, Q-193, Q-194, Q-234)
- `REQ-SWAP-042` LPVaultSwap MUST end every `liquidate` call with no change in its balance of the debt asset, the vault token or any of the market's tokens, and every allowance it granted at 0; `settlePending` ends with no change in its balance of the market's tokens, its balance of the pending asset changed by exactly the change of `carry[Tp]` (`REQ-SWAP-070`), and its vault-token balance lower by exactly the `x` units burned; `deleverage` ends with no change in any balance. Its code MUST use before/after deltas, never `balanceOf(this)` as proceeds; donated dust is ignored. (Source: Q-097, Q-119, Q-124, Q-201, Q-214, Q-255)
- `REQ-SWAP-043` LPVaultSwap MUST have no rescue, sweep or arbitrary-call function. Inside a liquidation, tokens leave only to the CDP Spoke (through `liquidationCall`), the swap routes, `feeRecipient`, `msg.sender`, the borrower's bound wallet and the payout escrow, and vault tokens leave only by `burnSeized`; no flash source or lender receives anything. Inside `deleverage`, tokens leave only to the flash source, the swap routes, the AaveAdapter and the borrower's wallet. In `settlePending`, to the market's routes, the Coverage Spoke through `fund`, `feeRecipient` and the payout escrow. Every escrowable payout to `feeRecipient`, `msg.sender` or the borrower that fails goes to the payout escrow (`REQ-SWAP-076`); the caller's payout `keeperOut` does not. (Source: Q-067, Q-104, Q-124, Q-201, Q-213, Q-214, Q-224, Q-255)
- `REQ-SWAP-044` Seized vault tokens MUST leave LPVaultSwap only by `AaveAdapter.burnSeized`, in the same call they were received or, for stranded tokens, inside `settlePending`. LPVaultSwap MUST NOT transfer vault tokens or supply them to a Hub or Spoke. (Source: Q-165, Q-201, Q-224)
- `REQ-SWAP-045` LPVaultSwap MUST call `beginSeizure`, `burnSeized` and `settleSeizure` only for the proxy and vault token of the active liquidation, and `burnSeized` and `Collateralization.settlePending` only for the vault token of the market named in `settlePending`. (Source: Q-125, Q-160, Q-201, Q-224)
- `REQ-SWAP-046` A caller MUST NOT supply a route address, a flash lender, a funding source, a recipient, an override of the recovery floor or any calldata executed by LPVaultSwap; only `routeMode` selects among the recorded routes. `deadline` and `minPayout` can only make the call revert; `maxShortfall` adds the caller's own funds and lowers each leg minimum by at most `maxShortfallBps`. No caller input changes `D`, `c`, `minRec`, `A`, `B`, a route or a recipient. (Source: Q-062, Q-119, Q-151, Q-216, Q-251, Q-335)
- `REQ-SWAP-047` LPVaultSwap MUST read every parameter from the ParameterStore and every market, debt reserve and route from the MarketRegistry during the call and MUST NOT cache any of them. (Source: Q-119, Q-139, Q-240)
- `REQ-SWAP-077` LPVaultSwap MUST skip, not attempt, every transfer, `transferFrom`, route call and escrow deposit whose amount is zero: `A`, `B`, `keeperOut`, each route input, residue and dust leg, the shortfall pull, `assetOut` of `deleverage` and the Coverage Spoke funding of `settlePending` (the caller pull is never zero, because `X > 0`, `REQ-SWAP-005`). It never asks the AaveAdapter to burn, or Collateralization to settle, zero vault-token units: `liquidate` reverts with `NothingSeized` before `burnSeized` when `c = 0` (`REQ-SWAP-017`), `settlePending` reverts with `NothingPending` before the Lock when `x = 0` (`REQ-SWAP-066`), and `deleverage` reverts with `DeleverageTooSmall` before the flash loan when the units size a zero repayment (`SPEC-LIQ-020`). Why: some tokens revert on a zero-amount transfer, and the vault token's `burn` of zero is refused by the AaveAdapter (`ZeroAmount`). (Source: Q-217, Q-124)

## Debt reserves

- `REQ-SWAP-087` LPVaultSwap MUST revert with `UnknownDebtReserve(debtReserveId)` unless `MarketRegistry.isApprovedDebtReserve(spoke, debtReserveId)` is true and the reserve's record names an asset. It reads the reserve's record (asset, decimals, funding Hub, Hub asset ID, price source, Coverage Spoke). A reserve the MarketRegistry has removed can no longer be liquidated, which is why removal requires zero drawn debt. Aave's `borrowable` flag is not part of the test, for `liquidate` and for `deleverage` alike: Aave V4 itself repays and liquidates a reserve whose new borrowing it turned off (its liquidation validation reads paused, supplied, drawn, HF, the collateral flag and receive-shares, never `borrowable`), and turning borrowing off is Aave's routine first response to a debt-asset incident. A borrowable flag that differs from the registry's set is configuration drift (`REQ-HUB-085`, `REQ-RISK-063`), which stops new borrowing and never liquidation (`REQ-SWAP-003`, `EMG-047`); since only LPVaultSwap may receive seized vault tokens, refusing it here would leave every account owing that reserve with no liquidation path at all (`ATTACK-200`). Example: the Aave DAO calls `updateBorrowable(cdpSpoke, usdcReserveId, false)`; Bob, 10,000 vault units at $2.10 against 18,000 USDC, is liquidated in the same block exactly as before the change. (Source: Q-233, Q-237)
- `REQ-SWAP-088` Debt asset funding. The repayment is funded only by the caller's `Kf` of the debt asset itself (`REQ-SWAP-011`); LPVaultSwap never buys the debt asset with another token and never sells a leftover back. No GHO is involved unless the debt asset is GHO (`Q-333`). Why: funding in the asset that is repaid removes a swap leg, its slippage and its MEV exposure, and removes any need for GHO depth on chains where GHO is thin. Example: a USDC liquidation of `D = 1,950` USDC is funded with 1,950 USDC, from the caller's balance or a helper's flash loan of USDC; no GHO moves. (Source: Q-333, Q-334, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-089` Leftover. The debt asset LPVaultSwap holds after `liquidationCall` above what it held before funding, `Kf − D`, MUST be returned in `keeperOut` (`SPEC-LIQ-017`); LPVaultSwap MUST NOT swap it. (Source: Q-333, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-090` `debtToCover` bounds the repayment for every debt reserve: Aave pulls `D ≤ Kf ≤ X` (`REQ-SWAP-011`, `REQ-SWAP-016`). (Source: Q-333, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-091` Debt price rule for the repaid reserve (`Q-247`, `Q-248`). For `r`, LPVaultSwap MUST revert with `DebtPriceStale(r)` unless `r` is GHO (Aave's fixed GHO source) or all of the following hold for the DebtOracle the MarketRegistry records for `r`, whichever source the AaveOracle uses (a different source is reported by the drift check and never reverts the call, `REQ-ORACLE-101`): after LPVaultSwap calls its `poke()`, that DebtOracle reports state Live (including the sequencer gate on an L2); and every input it used is no older than `liquidationPriceMaxAge` for that feed. The account's other debt reserves are not gated: Aave values them at their served price, which once Expired is the last accepted price raised by `debtStressRiseBps`, so a failing debt price elsewhere only lowers the health factor. Why: a stale price for `r` could size the repayment and the seizure at a price no current report supports. Example: the USDC feed has an 86,400 s heartbeat and a 1,800 s margin; an account owing USDC and GHO whose USDC DebtOracle is Expired is liquidated against its GHO debt at once, while a call naming the USDC reserve reverts with `DebtPriceStale`. Checked by: TEST-226, TEST-230, TEST-231. (Source: Q-238, Q-234, Q-182, Q-247, Q-248, Q-274)
- `REQ-ORACLE-110` LPVaultSwap MUST call `poke()` on the DebtOracle of the reserve it repays before it reads that DebtOracle's state, whose state alone gates the repayment (`INV-030`, `REQ-SWAP-091`); the account's other debt reserves enter its health factor at their served prices, which a failing debt price only raises, and the first observation of a failure there is stored by the next RiskModule or keeper poke. (Source: Q-129, Q-199, Q-247, Q-248)
- `REQ-SWAP-092` Seized-unit burn. LPVaultSwap MUST call `AaveAdapter.burnSeized(vaultToken, units)` while it holds the Lock, for exactly the `c` units of the current `liquidationCall` or the `x` units of `settlePending`. The AaveAdapter is the vault token's only burner; Collateralization then checks that the supply fell by exactly the units it debits. (Source: Q-224, Q-201)
- `REQ-SWAP-093` One debt reserve per call. An account owing several debt reserves is liquidated by sequential calls, each naming its `debtReserveId`; a committed operator's commitment requires continuing until the health factor is at least 1 or a deficit is recorded. (Source: Q-234, Q-193)
- `REQ-SWAP-094` Any debt asset settles the same way: the repayment comes from the caller's funding in the debt asset, and every payout is in the debt asset; no in-kind refund or token share exists (`Q-401`). Example: `Kf = 1,000` USDC and `D = 950` USDC: the leftover 50 USDC is part of `keeperOut` (`SPEC-LIQ-010`). (Source: Q-133, Q-333, Q-401)

## One entry point, deadline and minimum payout

- `REQ-SWAP-072` `liquidatePermissionless` does not exist: `liquidate(params)` is the only liquidation entry point, callable by any address that is not frozen, with `PrimaryThenBackup` or `BackupOnly` (`Q-404`). Every check runs on every call, whoever the caller: the deadline, the caller freeze, the Lock, the market, proxy, amount and debt-reserve checks, the health factor, the LPOracle `poke()` and the price, grace and sequencer gates, the Hub share check, the caller funding, the liquidation call, burn, unwind, swaps at the per-leg minimums, the recovery floor with the optional shortfall top-up, the waterfall with Avana's fee and the borrower surplus, and `minPayout`. Why: these checks fix everything a caller could exploit (oracle recovery floor, fixed routes, payout to `msg.sender`), so a second, restricted entry point protected nothing; `BackupOnly` only selects an approved route held to the same floor and leg minimums; one path means one set of code, tests and monitoring. Example: an address with no registration, holding 1,950 USDC, liquidates the account of the examples with `debtToCover = 1,950` USDC, `routeMode = BackupOnly` and `minPayout = 2,019` USDC; the backup routes return 2,040.3 USDC, `A = 19.5` USDC goes to `feeRecipient` and the caller receives `keeperOut = 2,020.8` USDC, a net 70.8 USDC. (Source: Q-213, Q-063, Q-097, Q-190, Q-242, Q-251, Q-256, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-073` Every caller settles the same way (`Q-397`): LPVaultSwap pulls `Kf` from `msg.sender`, draws on no protocol balance, and conservation `Kf + R + shortfall = D + A + B + keeperOut` holds (`SPEC-LIQ-010`). A caller funding from its own balance bears a recovery below `D` in `keeperOut`; a flash-funded caller covers a payout below its loan through its helper's top-up bound (`REQ-HELPER-007`). Example: the dust liquidation funded with `Kf = 3` USDC pays `keeperOut = 2.95` USDC, a 0.05 USDC loss the caller accepted through `minPayout ≤ 2.95` USDC; through the public helper with `F = 3` USDC at 5 bps (`φ = 0.0015`) and a top-up bound of 0.06 USDC, the helper pulls 0.0515 USDC from its caller, repays 3.0015 USDC and pays its caller 0. (Source: Q-213, Q-117, Q-127, Q-190, Q-397, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-074` `liquidate` MUST revert with `DeadlineExpired(deadline)` if `block.timestamp > params.deadline`, as its first check. Example: a keeper signs with `deadline = t + 36` on Ethereum (three blocks) or `t + 6` on Base; included later, the call reverts and changes nothing. (Source: Q-216, Q-131)
- `REQ-SWAP-075` LPVaultSwap MUST revert with `PayoutTooLow(keeperOut, minPayout)` if `keeperOut < params.minPayout`. `keeperOut` includes the caller's returned funding, so the caller sets `minPayout` to its funding plus the smallest net payout it accepts (`Q-345`). Example: a caller funding 1,950 USDC with a previewed net payout of 70.8 USDC sets `minPayout = 1,950 + 69 = 2,019` USDC; the regular example pays 2,020.8 and succeeds; routes returning `R = 2,035` USDC give `S = 85`, `A = 19.5`, `kr = 65.5`, `keeperOut = 2,015.5` and the call reverts with `PayoutTooLow(2,015,500,000, 2,019,000,000)`. (Source: Q-216, Q-097, Q-190, Q-256, Q-345)

## Pending settlement

An upgraded LPVaultSwap implementation could seize collateral and never settle it. Collateralization repairs the borrower's records on the next withdrawal or liquidation settlement for that account (`Q-201`), moving the matching tokens into a pending-settlement balance per vault token. Once an honest implementation is back, any address can call `settlePending`: the AaveAdapter burns the stranded vault tokens, Collateralization releases the matching tokens, LPVaultSwap swaps them into the market's pending-settlement asset and funds the Coverage Spoke of the market's (Hub, that asset), which absorbs the losses such an incident causes (`Q-211`, `Q-338`).

- `REQ-SWAP-066` `settlePending(marketId, debtReserveId)` MUST be callable by any address and MUST NOT read the pause state, any breaker or the address freeze. Before it acquires the Lock it MUST revert with `UnknownMarket`, with `InvalidFeeRecipient` if `feeRecipient` is `address(0)` and with `LockHeld(holder)`; then read `p` (pending units), `h` (LPVaultSwap's vault-token balance), `x = min(h, p)`, reverting with `NothingPending` if `x == 0`. These are view reads made while the Lock is free (`getPending` reverts with `Locked` while another member holds it), and Collateralization re-checks `x` against the pending balance and the token's supply under the Lock, so a stale read can only revert ([2.16](16-lock.md) `REQ-LOCK-007`). It MUST revert with `UnknownDebtReserve(debtReserveId)` unless `debtReserveId == MarketRegistry.settlementReserve(marketId)` (the market's pending-settlement reserve `rp`, the first debt reserve enabled on its CDP Spoke unless governance changed it at the normal delay), and reads its asset `Tp`, funding Hub `hF` and Coverage Spoke `cov = coverageSpokeOf(hF, Tp)`. Under the Lock it MUST: (1) `poke()` the market's LPOracle and revert with `StalePrice` if `REQ-SWAP-008` (a) to (d) fail (so `settlePending` is refused while the sequencer is down, and grace does not apply), and apply `REQ-SWAP-091` to `rp`; (2) record its balances, call `burnSeized(vaultToken, x)` and `Collateralization.settlePending(vaultToken, x)`, and take `aᵢ` as balance deltas; (3) swap each token `≠ Tp` with a positive amount over the market's routes into `Tp` as `PrimaryThenBackup` does, with `minOutᵢ` at `sLeg = s_P`; (4) measure `R` in `Tp` and revert with `RecoveryBelowFloor` if `R < minPending`; (5) fund `cov` per `REQ-SWAP-070`; (6) pay any route residue to `feeRecipient` by the delivery of `REQ-SWAP-076`; (7) release the Lock and emit `PendingSettlementForwarded`. Why: the unsettled seizure already repaid the borrower's debt and no flash loan is open, so nothing in the waterfall is owed; sending the proceeds to the Coverage Spoke of the market's own Hub, paying the caller nothing and holding the swaps to the floor means no caller gains from triggering the call. Example: a hostile implementation seized 40 vault tokens of a Base Slipstream USDC/USDT market; the market's pending-settlement reserve is USDC, so the released 41.2 USDC and 42.6 USDT become 83.77 USDC (USDC counted in full, USDT swapped above its minimum), and `fund` adds them to the Stable LP Hub's USDC Coverage Spoke on Base. Checked by: QNT-T09, QNT-03, QNT-37. (Source: Q-201, Q-211, Q-124, Q-148, Q-224, Q-227, Q-236, Q-241, Q-250, Q-257, Q-338)
- `REQ-SWAP-067` `settleSeizure` reconciles the account-market before it settles the current seizure (`Q-201`): an earlier gap is debited and moved to the pending-settlement balance, never paid to LPVaultSwap inside `liquidate`, so `aᵢ` and `R` measure only the `c` units of the current seizure. (Source: Q-201, Q-165, Q-189, Q-240)
- `REQ-SWAP-070` `settlePending` funds `Rt = R + carry[Tp]`, the recovery plus any amount an earlier settlement of the same asset carried. If `Rt` is worth at least one Hub share of `Tp` on the funding Hub (`Hub.previewAddByAssets(hubAssetId, Rt) > 0`), it MUST `forceApprove` the Coverage Spoke `cov` of the market's (Hub, `Tp`) for exactly `Rt`, call `cov.fund(Rt)`, reset the allowance, set `carry[Tp] = 0` and revert with `FundingMismatch` unless its `Tp` balance fell by exactly `Rt`. Otherwise it MUST skip `fund`, keep `Rt` in its balance and record `carry[Tp] = Rt`, so the next `settlePending` that settles into `Tp` adds it. LPVaultSwap MUST NOT send funds to a Coverage Spoke by plain transfer, which it does not count. If `fund` reverts, the whole `settlePending` reverts and the tokens stay pending. Why: a Coverage Spoke's `fund` of less than one share's value mints no shares and reverts on the Hub, which would block the settlement of a few units forever; carrying the amount loses nothing and the next settlement funds it. Example: a settlement that recovers 1 wei of GHO while one Hub share is worth 1.05 wei records `carry[GHO] = 1`; the next settlement recovering 599.9 GHO funds 599.9 GHO plus 1 wei. (Source: Q-211, Q-101, Q-124, Q-142, Q-236, Q-338)

## Per-leg minimums, breakers and freezes

- `REQ-SWAP-100` Every route call LPVaultSwap makes MUST carry an oracle-derived minimum: each leg in `liquidate`, `settlePending` and `deleverage` passes `minOutᵢ ≥ 1` from `SPEC-LIQ-018`; the backup receives the same minimum as the primary; no call runs a leg without one. Removing liquidity is not a swap and keeps zero minimums. Checked by: TEST-232, PROP-068. (Source: Q-250, Q-255, Q-256)
- `REQ-SWAP-121` **Dust legs.** A released token `i ≠ T` with `aᵢ > 0` and `Vlegᵢ ≤ Ulegᵢ` (`SPEC-LIQ-018`) is a dust leg. In `liquidate`, `settlePending` and `deleverage` LPVaultSwap MUST NOT call a route for a dust leg; it counts as 0 toward `R`, is left out of `n'`, and MUST be transferred whole to the address that receives route residues in that call (`msg.sender` in `liquidate`, `feeRecipient` in `settlePending`, `r.wallet` in `deleverage`), by the delivery of `REQ-SWAP-076` in `liquidate` and `settlePending`, so a dust leg the recipient cannot receive is escrowed for it. When every released token is `T` or a dust leg, the call swaps nothing and `NoRouteAvailable` cannot occur. Why: a leg worth about one raw unit of the debt asset or less can return 0 from every route, so `minOutᵢ ≥ 1` can never be met; the liquidation would revert with `NoRouteAvailable` on every attempt, and the account could not be liquidated. This happens whenever the debt asset has fewer decimals than a token priced far below it, and in any market whose pool sits near the edge of a position's range. Skipping the leg loses nothing the floor counts: its own term `⌈Vlegᵢ × (10⁴ − s) / 10⁴⌉ − Ulegᵢ` is at most 0, so `minRec` already allows for it through `U`, and the tokens handed to the caller are worth less than `Ulegᵢ + 1` debt units at the capped leg price. Example: USDC debt with `Pdebt = 70,000,000` ($0.70), a DAI leg of `aᵢ = 7,349,976,535,931` wei at the capped leg price 5,000,016 ($0.05000016): `Vlegᵢ = ⌊7,349,976,535,931 × 5,000,016 × 10⁶ / (10¹⁸ × 70,000,000)⌋ = 0` and `Ulegᵢ = ⌈5,000,016 × 10⁶ / (10¹⁸ × 70,000,000)⌉ = 1`; the DAI goes to the caller, `R = 0`, `minRec = 0`, and the call repays the debt with `keeperOut = Kf − D`. Checked by: MATH-ST-111, HYP-36. (Source: Q-250, Q-251)
- `REQ-SWAP-101` In both route modes of `liquidate`, LPVaultSwap MUST apply `SPEC-LIQ-019`; `R` stays the amount recovered from the seized collateral alone, so the top-up never counts as recovery and never changes `minRec`. Keepers answer a floor failure with a top-up sized from the floor gap, never with repeated halving, and size a helper's top-up bound from the Lens `expectedCallerLoss`; no shortfall report exists (`Q-401`). No minimum call size or minimum debt applies: Avana's keepers liquidate every liquidatable account whatever its debt, and pay their own gas (`Q-381`). Checked by: TEST-233. (Source: Q-251, Q-381)
- `REQ-SWAP-102` No recovery-shortfall report exists (`Q-399`, `Q-401`): LPVaultSwap has no `reportRecoveryShortfall` and no `probeLiquidation`. A market whose routes run below the recovery floor shows in monitoring through the keeper's failed simulations and the routes' depth checks (`REQ-ROUTE-006`); a guardian freezes new borrowing when the approved routes are confirmed unable to settle valid liquidations at the required size. Why: the report existed to open in-kind settlement automatically, which no longer exists, and a count fed by callers can be gamed. (Source: Q-251, Q-242, Q-399, Q-401)
- `REQ-SWAP-103` LPVaultSwap MUST keep no per-market shortfall or failure state across calls (no `shortfallSince`, `lastShortfallAt` or failure counter); every call is judged on its own inputs and the current oracle and route state. (Source: Q-251, Q-241, Q-399)
- `REQ-SWAP-104` LPVaultSwap MUST NOT report liquidation outcomes to the MarketRegistry and MUST NOT read any breaker (`Q-399`). Seizure volume (alert above 20 % of a market in 1 hour) and route failures (alert at 3 in 1 hour) are monitoring alerts computed from `LiquidationSettled` and `RouteFailed`, not on-chain trips; the deficit breaker reads Aave's deficit of the debt reserve directly ([2.7](07-risk-module.md)). Why: a reverted liquidation cannot persist a count, a count fed by liquidations can be gamed, and removing the report removes a gas-limited external call and its headroom check from every settlement. (Source: Q-241, Q-328, Q-399)
- `REQ-SWAP-105` `liquidate` MUST revert with `AddressIsFrozen(msg.sender)` if the caller is frozen, checked right after the deadline. The borrower's wallet, the proxy and every contract-to-contract call are never checked, and a frozen wallet's positions are liquidated like any other. The freeze binds `msg.sender` only: a frozen address that calls through the public flash liquidation helper reaches LPVaultSwap as the helper, so the freeze does not stop it, and the public helper is in the MarketRegistry's protected set ([2.8](08-market-registry.md) `REQ-MREG-020`), because a freeze of it would stop every caller that uses it. A freeze therefore stops an address's direct calls and removes a frozen committed operator from `keeperFloor` (`REQ-SWAP-107`); it cannot stop an address from liquidating, which is the intended outcome, because every check that protects Avana and the borrower runs whoever calls. Example: a guardian freezes address Z after an incident; Z's direct `liquidate` reverts `AddressIsFrozen(Z)`, and the same liquidation sent by Z through the public helper settles, paying Z through the helper. Checked by: TEST-229, PROP-066. (Source: Q-242, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-107` `activeKeeperCount()` MUST return the number of registered committed operators not frozen. `addKeeper` refuses a frozen address and `removeKeeper` applies the floor `KEEPER_FLOOR` (1) to active committed operators. `freezeAddress` checks no floor ([2.8](08-market-registry.md) `REQ-MREG-021`): a guardian can always freeze a compromised operator, including the last active one. New borrowing halts only when 0 active committed operators remain on the chain (`REQ-SWAP-034`, `REQ-SWAP-039`), and liquidation stays open to every address. Example: with 2 registered operators, `removeKeeper` of one succeeds and of the second reverts `KeeperFloor(0, 1)`; freezing the remaining operator succeeds, `activeKeeperCount()` becomes 0 and every `preBorrowCheck` on the chain reverts until an operator is registered or unfrozen, while `liquidate` keeps settling for every caller. Checked by: TEST-229, PROP-071. (Source: Q-242, Q-139, Q-155, Q-400)
- `REQ-SWAP-108` Balance-delta accounting (`Q-271`, `Q-390`). Every amount LPVaultSwap receives MUST be measured as the rise of its own balance, and every later step MUST use the measured amount: the caller funding `Kf` (`REQ-SWAP-011`), the vault tokens `c` and debt `D` across `liquidationCall` (`REQ-SWAP-017`), the tokens released by Collateralization, the debt asset a route returns, the shortfall pull and, in `deleverage`, the flash principal `Fr` (`REQ-SWAP-013`). The `deleverage` flash repayment is measured as the fall of its own balance, `P`, across the transfers of `REQ-SWAP-122`, and each escrowable payout reports the recipient's balance rise (`REQ-SWAP-076`). LPVaultSwap approves the Spoke for exactly the measured `Kf` and passes `Kf` as Aave's bound (`REQ-SWAP-016`), and settles with the measured terms of `SPEC-LIQ-010`. Every oracle limit still compares against full oracle value. Measurement covers a token that delivers less than it was asked for, the rounding of a rebasing token, a pool token whose issuer switched on a transfer fee (its seized amounts and route inputs) and payouts to recipients; it does not make a fee-charged repayment of the debt asset settle. A transfer fee on `T` itself stops every repayment of `T` inside Aave V4: the Hub requires its balance to cover the amount it books and reverts `InsufficientTransferred` when the Spoke delivers less, so `liquidationCall` reverts and the liquidation with it until the fee returns to 0 (`REQ-SWAP-123`). Why: exact-amount checks of LPVaultSwap's own would revert on a token that legitimately delivers a unit less, and booking what arrived means LPVaultSwap never counts tokens it does not hold, whatever a token does. Example: in a USDC-debt liquidation of a USDC/USDT market with Tether's fee at 10 bps, LPVaultSwap sends 6,211.3 seized USDT to a Curve USDT → USDC route, the route swaps the 6,205.0887 USDT that arrived ([2.12](12-settlement-routes.md) `REQ-ROUTE-008`), LPVaultSwap measures `R` as the rise of its own USDC balance, and the conservation identity of `SPEC-LIQ-010` holds with the measured terms; the USDC repayment into the Hub carries no fee and settles. Checked by: MATH-ST-112, TEST-221. (Source: Q-271, Q-124, Q-241, Q-390, Q-397, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-122` Flash repayment by balance delta, in `deleverage` only (a liquidation takes no flash loan, `REQ-SWAP-012`). Inside `onFlashLoan`, with `owed = amount + premium`, LPVaultSwap MUST repeat at most `MAX_REPAY_TRANSFERS = 4` times: read `b = T.balanceOf(flashSource)`, stop if `b ≥ owed`, else transfer `need = owed − b` of `T` to the flash source. It MUST then revert with `FlashRepayShort(b, owed)` unless the source's balance is at least `owed`. `P`, the fall of its own `T` balance across the loop, is never booked as a number: `assetOut` is measured as the rise of LPVaultSwap's `T` balance across the whole `flashLoan` call, so it is net of `P` and of any fee the transfers lost. Units: debt units. Rounding: none; each transfer is of an exact integer amount and the token's fee is subtracted from what the source receives. Without a transfer fee the loop makes one transfer of `F + φ` and `P = F + φ`. With USDT's fee, each transfer of `x` delivers `x − min(⌊x × ρ / 10⁴⌋, maxFee)`, and USDT's contract bounds `ρ ≤ 19` bps and `maxFee < 50` USDT, so the residual after transfer `k` is the fee on transfer `k`: at most `50·10⁶`, then `⌊50·10⁶ × 19 / 10⁴⌋ = 95,000`, then `⌊95,000 × 19 / 10⁴⌋ = 180`, then `⌊180 × 19 / 10⁴⌋ = 0` raw units, so four transfers always reach `owed` and three do not. A token whose fee does not shrink this way fails the final check, the `deleverage` call reverts, and the borrower repays from its own funds. A transfer fee on `T` that is on throughout the call never reaches this loop: Aave V4's Hub refuses the fee-reduced repayment of `REQ-SWAP-106` step (4) first (`InsufficientTransferred`, `REQ-SWAP-123`); the loop is defence in depth for any transfer to the source that delivers less than was sent. Why: the flash source must hold `owed` when the Pool pulls it; topping up by measured balance makes the repayment exact for any fee-bearing transfer without knowing the token's fee function, and leaves the source with no balance after the Pool's pull. Example: USDT at 10 bps, `owed = 5,002.5` USDT: transfer 1 of 5,002,500,000 raw delivers 4,997,497,500; transfer 2 of 5,002,500 delivers 4,997,498; transfer 3 of 5,002 delivers 4,997; transfer 4 of 5 delivers 5; the source holds 5,002,500,000 and `P = 5,007,507,507` raw (5,007.507507 USDT). Checked by: MATH-ST-111. (Source: Q-335, Q-390)
- `REQ-SWAP-123` Transfer fee on a debt asset. A debt asset is enabled only while its transfer fee is zero: `MarketRegistry.enableDebtReserve` reverts with `TransferFeeActive(asset)` unless the asset's issuer probe reads a fee of 0 at listing, or the asset has no fee switch ([2.8](08-market-registry.md)). If the issuer later switches a fee on, the issuer breaker of [2.7](07-risk-module.md) `SPEC-BRK-003` trips for every debt reserve of that asset: new borrows of it stop. While the fee is above 0, no repayment of the asset settles on Aave V4, for Avana or any other Aave user: the Hub's `add`, `restore` and `reclaim` each require its token balance to be at least the liquidity it books and revert `InsufficientTransferred(missing)` otherwise, and a fee-charged transfer into the Hub always arrives short. So `liquidate` (inside `liquidationCall`), the borrower's repay, repay with collateral (`deleverage`, at `deleverageRepay`) and `settlePending` into that asset revert until the fee returns to 0: the first three inside Aave, and `settlePending` at the Coverage Spoke's `fund`, which requires the Hub's balance to rise by exactly the amount funded (`TransferAmountMismatch`, [2.14](14-first-loss-reserve.md) `REQ-FLR-010`) before the Hub's own check. Avana adds no path around Aave: no side transfer, no top-up into the Hub, no repayment in another asset. Keepers and monitoring alert on the probe ([3.6](../03-operations/06-emergency-operations.md) P-20), and liquidations resume at the next block after the issuer sets the fee back to 0, with no Avana action; the `ISSUER` freeze on new borrows stays until an authorized reset ([2.7](07-risk-module.md) `SPEC-BRK-008`). Balance-delta measurement (`REQ-SWAP-108`, `REQ-SWAP-122`, `REQ-FLASH-003`) stays as defence in depth. A transfer fee on a pool token, which a route swaps into `T`, and on payouts LPVaultSwap sends, is a different case: those transfers never enter the Hub and settle by balance delta (`REQ-SWAP-108`, [2.12](12-settlement-routes.md) `REQ-ROUTE-008`). Why: Aave's own accounting refuses a short repayment, and a side path would bypass that accounting; listing only fee-free debt assets and stopping new borrows the moment a fee appears keeps the debt that cannot be repaid from growing, which is the same position every Aave V4 user of the asset is in. Example: Tether sets USDT's `basisPointsRate = 10` on Ethereum (it is 0 today). The next RiskModule check reads it and every borrow of USDT on the chain reverts `DebtBreakerTripped`. A keeper's `liquidate` with `debtToCover = 1,950` USDT delivers `Kf = 1,948.05` USDT; LPVaultSwap passes it to `liquidationCall`, the Spoke's transfer of `D = 1,948.05` USDT reaches the Hub as 1,946.101950 USDT, and the Hub reverts `InsufficientTransferred(1,948,050)` raw units; the account is unchanged. When Tether sets the rate back to 0, the same call settles in the next block. Checked by: MATH-ST-112, TEST-048, TEST-221. (Source: Q-271, Q-390)
- `REQ-SWAP-118` Liquidation throughput (`Q-344`). Callers MAY batch separate `liquidate` calls in one transaction through a multicall; each call acquires and releases the Lock, so warm-state savings apply and no call waits on another. The cap sizing of `SPEC-LIQ-021` is checked at `listCollateralMarket` and at every raise of `marketUsdCap`, and monitoring recomputes `T_drain` from measured route depth, flash liquidity and gas daily. Example: 12 sequential calls of 1.3M gas each fit in one transaction under the 16.78M per-transaction cap. Checked by: MATH-ST-102. (Source: Q-344)
- `REQ-SWAP-119` Aave-side halts (`Q-346`). An Aave halt of a CDP Spoke or a pause of the vault-token or debt reserve blocks repay, withdraw and liquidation of that market, and no Avana contract can override it. The listing AIP asks the Aave DAO to contain a CDP Spoke with a reserve freeze and drawCap 0, never `haltSpoke` or `pauseReserve` while the market has debt. The RiskModule's drift check reports a halted CDP Spoke or paused Avana reserve as drift (stopping new activity on the Hub's other markets that share it), monitoring pages on every such flag, and the keepers' playbook re-runs the Lens on every account of the market and liquidates each eligible account in the first block after the unpause, through the public helper where the Aave V3 Pool's liquidity allows and from the keepers' own funds otherwise. (Source: Q-346, Q-140, Q-202)

## Repay with collateral

LPVaultSwap runs the swap side of the AaveAdapter's `repayWithCollateral` ([2.6](06-aave-adapter.md), `Q-255`). The call preserves backing (every unit burned equals the record debit), solvency (debt is repaid before any collateral leaves, the flash loan and premium are covered by the swaps or the call reverts, and no bonus, fee or keeper payout applies), the health-factor floor (the AaveAdapter requires the health factor after the call to be at least its value before and at least the borrower's `minHealthFactor`) and conservation.

- `REQ-SWAP-106` `deleverage(r)` MUST revert with `NotAdapter` unless `msg.sender == AAVE_ADAPTER` and the Lock is held by `AAVE_ADAPTER`. It runs under `Paused(General)` and every breaker, because it only reduces debt and never lowers the health factor; the AaveAdapter refuses it only under `Paused(MintPathSuspect)`, and runs it for a frozen wallet, because it only reduces that wallet's debt ([2.6](06-aave-adapter.md) `REQ-ADAPTER-080`, `REQ-ADAPTER-082`, `Q-356`). In order it MUST: (1) check the market (`UnknownMarket`, also when the request's `vaultToken` or `spoke` is not the market record's), the debt reserve (`UnknownDebtReserve`), the price rules of `REQ-SWAP-008` (a) to (d) and `REQ-SWAP-091`, and Hub shares == assets; (2) size `x` per `SPEC-LIQ-020` (`DeleverageTooSmall`); (3) open the transient context, bound to the hash of the encoded sizing context it passes through the flash source, and flash-borrow `F = x` of the debt asset through the flash source with the checks of `REQ-FLASH-002` and `REQ-FLASH-005`, running the rest inside `onFlashLoan`; if the source is short it reverts with `FlashLiquidityShort` and the borrower retries with a smaller `units`; (4) `forceApprove` the AaveAdapter for exactly `Fr`, the principal received (`Fr = x` unless the asset charges a transfer fee, `REQ-SWAP-013`), call `deleverageRepay(proxy, r, Fr)` and reset the allowance; with a transfer fee on the debt asset, Aave V4's Hub refuses this repayment (`InsufficientTransferred`) and `deleverage` reverts until the fee returns to 0 (`REQ-SWAP-123`); (5) call `deleverageRelease(proxy, position, units, mins)`, with `mins = r.minAmounts`, or `n` zeros when it is empty (the normalised array the Adapter's session also hashes, [2.6](06-aave-adapter.md) `REQ-ADAPTER-080`), in which the units are withdrawn, burned and unwound to LPVaultSwap; take `aᵢ` as balance deltas; (6) swap each token `≠ T` at `minOutᵢ` with `sLeg = slippageAllowanceBps` and measure `R`; (7) revert with `DeleverageShortfall(R, x + φ)` if `R < x + φ`, repay the flash source by balance delta per `REQ-SWAP-122` until it holds `x + φ`, then send each route residue and dust leg to `r.wallet` by a plain transfer (`REQ-SWAP-024`, `REQ-SWAP-121`); after `flashLoan` returns, send `assetOut`, the rise of LPVaultSwap's `T` balance across the `flashLoan` call (which includes any part of `Fr` the AaveAdapter returned unused), to `r.wallet`; (8) clear the context and emit `Deleveraged`; `onFlashLoan` emits `FlashLoanUsed`. Why: a borrower near liquidation who holds no spare debt asset could otherwise only wait for a keeper and pay the bonus. Example: the 1,000-unit repayment of `SPEC-LIQ-020` flash-borrows 2,075.924073 USDC, repays it, unwinds the liquidity, swaps the USDT, repays `x + φ` with the half-up premium the Pool charges (`φ = 1,037,962`) and sends 19.237965 USDC to the wallet. Checked by: TEST-239, PROP-072. (Source: Q-255, Q-250, Q-256, Q-271, Q-347, Q-356)

Preserved invariants (zero residual balances, received = burned = debited, vault-token supply at most credited units + pending units, only LPVaultSwap receives seized vault tokens, every swap leg has an oracle-derived minimum, Avana's fee bounded by the surplus, `A + kr + B = S`, repay with collateral never lowering the health factor) are stated in [4.1 Global Invariants](../04-security/01-global-invariants.md).

## Liquidation access

LPVaultSwap is the single entry for every liquidation of an Avana account; [3.5 Liquidation access](../03-operations/05-liquidation.md#liquidation-access) states the access model step by step.

- `REQ-SWAP-084` Liquidation exclusivity comes from the vault token, not from the Spoke. The CDP Spoke is a standard Aave V4 Spoke whose `liquidationCall` anyone may call; the vault token's transfer rule admits only proxy to Hub on supply, Hub to proxy on withdrawal (the AaveAdapter then burns from the proxy) and Hub (on the CDP Spoke's seizure) to the chain's LPVaultSwap address ([2.4](04-vault-tokens.md) `REQ-VTOKEN-006`), and a registered proxy never calls `liquidationCall` because the AaveAdapter's call builder has no such selector (`REQ-PROXY-008`), so any other liquidator's `liquidationCall` reverts on the token transfer, and `receiveShares` is disabled on every Avana collateral reserve so a share transfer cannot bypass it. An LPVaultSwap upgrade keeps the address and so keeps the exclusivity; moving LPVaultSwap to a new address would need new vault tokens and is not supported. Drift of `receiveSharesEnabled`, `liquidationFee` or the reinvestment controller trips a breaker, which stops new activity, never liquidation. (Source: Q-049, Q-116, Q-222, Q-226)
- `REQ-SWAP-085` `liquidate` MUST be the only liquidation entry point, open with both route modes to every address that is not frozen; the committed-operator registry gates no part of it (`REQ-SWAP-001`, `REQ-SWAP-032` to `REQ-SWAP-038`). No Avana contract reimburses gas: Avana's keeper pays it as an operating cost, together with any loss of a recovery below the debt it funded (`SPEC-LIQ-010`, `Q-381`, `Q-397`). An operator the Aave DAO or a provider it chooses runs is registered as a committed operator like any other, at the normal delay. (Source: Q-053, Q-082, Q-155, Q-222, Q-228, Q-242, Q-263, Q-381, Q-404)
- `REQ-SWAP-086` Any address with no role, the Aave DAO executor included, MAY call `liquidate` directly or through the public flash liquidation helper, at once and with no Avana action. A liquidator other than LPVaultSwap cannot receive Avana vault tokens (`REQ-SWAP-084`), so the Aave DAO liquidates Avana accounts through `liquidate` like anyone else. (Source: Q-213, Q-222, Q-224, Q-404)

## Flash liquidation helper

The flash liquidation helper lets a caller that holds no debt asset liquidate. It is a periphery contract outside the audited core, at `contracts/src/periphery/flash-liquidator/`: public, immutable, with no owner, admin, upgrade, setter or sweep, and holding no funds or allowances between transactions. One public instance per chain borrows every debt asset, GHO included, from that chain's Aave V3 Pool (the Ethereum Core Pool; on Base `0xA238Dd80C259a72e81d7e4664a9801593F98d1c5`), and anyone can use it. An operator may deploy its own instance of the operator variant, bound to its signer and its funding Safe. LPVaultSwap treats every helper as an ordinary caller: `msg.sender` is the helper, which funds `liquidate`, pays any floor shortfall and receives `keeperOut`; no check or formula of LPVaultSwap depends on the loan (`REQ-SWAP-012`). In this section `F` is the helper's loan, `φ` the Pool's premium on it, `X = params.debtToCover`, `T` the debt asset, `D` the debt LPVaultSwap repaid in the call and the lender the Pool, the helper's only lender (`REQ-HELPER-015`).

```solidity
struct FlashLiquidationRequest {
    LiquidationParams params; // passed unchanged to LPVaultSwap.liquidate
    uint256 flashAmount;      // F: 0 < F <= params.debtToCover (public), 0 <= F <= params.debtToCover (operator)
    uint256 maxPremium;       // most premium accepted on F
    uint256 maxTopUp;         // most debt asset pulled after liquidate to repay F + premium
    uint256 minPayout;        // smallest net payout after the repayment
}

interface IFlashLiquidator {
    function flashLiquidate(FlashLiquidationRequest calldata request) external returns (uint256 payout);
    function executeOperation(address[] calldata assets, uint256[] calldata amounts, uint256[] calldata premiums,
        address initiator, bytes calldata params) external returns (bool);              // Aave V3 Pool callback, the only one
    function claimEscrowed(address token) external returns (uint256 amount);
    function maxFlashLoan(address asset) external view returns (uint256 available);
    function flashPremium(address asset, uint256 amount) external view returns (uint256 premium);
    function POOL() external view returns (address pool);                                // the only lender
    function LP_VAULT_SWAP() external view returns (address lpVaultSwap);
    function MARKET_REGISTRY() external view returns (address registry);
    function PARAMETER_STORE() external view returns (address store);
}

interface IOperatorFlashLiquidator is IFlashLiquidator {
    function SIGNER() external view returns (address signer);
    function FUNDING_SAFE() external view returns (address safe);
    function MAX_TOP_UP_BPS() external view returns (uint256 bps);
}
```

- `REQ-HELPER-001` The helper MUST hold the immutables `POOL` (the chain's Aave V3 Pool, its only lender, `REQ-HELPER-015`), `LP_VAULT_SWAP`, `MARKET_REGISTRY` and `PARAMETER_STORE`, and the operator variant also `SIGNER`, `FUNDING_SAFE` and `MAX_TOP_UP_BPS`. It MUST have no owner, admin role, upgrade path, setter, sweep, rescue or arbitrary-call function, and MUST end every transaction with no allowance and no balance of the debt asset, the market's tokens or their routes' intermediates beyond what it held before the transaction. Example: a WETH → USDC hop of an X → WETH → USDC route fills only partly at its price limit while the leg still meets its minimum; LPVaultSwap returns the 0.4 WETH left as a residue to the helper, which forwards it to its caller in the same call. `claimEscrowed` (`REQ-HELPER-010`, `REQ-HELPER-011`) moves only the helper's own PayoutEscrow credit, to a recipient the variant fixes, never a balance the helper holds. Its constructor reverts with `ZeroAddress` for any zero address and, in the operator variant, with `InvalidTopUpBps` unless `MAX_TOP_UP_BPS ≤ 10⁴`. It has no lender callback other than the Pool's `executeOperation`: an ERC-3156 `onFlashLoan` call reaches no function and reverts. Why: an immutable contract with nothing to administer and nothing to hold has no key to steal and no balance to drain, so it needs no governance and can be used by anyone. (Source: Q-358, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-002` Source rule. The constructor MUST revert with `FlashSourceIsHub` if `MarketRegistry.isRecordedHub(POOL)` is true, and `flashLiquidate` repeats the check on every call that takes a loan. The helper borrows only from `POOL` (`REQ-HELPER-015`), never from an address in calldata. Why: a loan drawn from a Hub that funds the repaid reserve would repay that Hub's debt with its own liquidity inside a Hub that has no reentrancy guard (`REQ-FLASH-005`); the Aave V3 Pool is no Avana Hub, and the per-call check keeps a later Hub recording from turning the helper into one. (Source: Q-335, Q-388, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-003` Request checks. `flashLiquidate(request)` MUST, in order, revert with `ContextActive` while a context of this helper is open (no nesting and no reentry); in the operator variant with `NotSigner` unless `msg.sender == SIGNER`; with `InvalidFlashAmount` unless `0 < F ≤ X` in the public helper or `0 ≤ F ≤ X` in the operator variant, and both `X` and `params.maxShortfall` are at most `2¹²⁸ − 1` (LPVaultSwap's own bound, `REQ-SWAP-005`, so the prefund of `REQ-HELPER-004` cannot overflow); read `T` as the asset of `params.debtReserveId` on the Spoke of `params.marketId` from `MARKET_REGISTRY`; and, when `F > 0`, revert with `FlashSourceIsHub` if `MarketRegistry.isRecordedHub(POOL)` (`REQ-HELPER-002`, `REQ-HELPER-015`), revert with `InsufficientFlashLiquidity(available)` if `F > maxFlashLoan(T)`, and revert with `FlashFeeTooHigh` unless `flashPremium(T, F) × 10⁴ ≤ maxFlashFeeBps × F`, the pre-loan bound of `SPEC-LIQ-015` on the quote. Every one of these checks runs before the Pool is called. An unknown debt reserve reads `T = address(0)`, which the Pool does not lend, so a call with `F > 0` reverts with `InsufficientFlashLiquidity(0)` and one with `F = 0` reverts at its first read of `T`. With `F = 0` (operator variant only) the Pool is not read, quoted or called, so a paused or empty Pool reserve or a changed Pool does not stop the operator: the Safe funds the whole liquidation (`REQ-HELPER-011`). The public helper keeps `F > 0`: a public caller that funds the whole repayment calls `liquidate` itself, with nothing for the helper to add. (Source: Q-063, Q-135, Q-335, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-004` Bound context. Before calling the Pool, the helper MUST record a single-use context in transient storage: the lender (`POOL`), itself as initiator, `T`, `F`, `maxPremium`, `maxTopUp`, the hash of the encoded `params`, its payer (`msg.sender` for the public helper, `FUNDING_SAFE` for the operator variant) and its payout recipient (`msg.sender` for the public helper, `FUNDING_SAFE` for the operator variant), with an active flag set and a used flag clear. It then pulls the prefund `X − F + params.maxShortfall` of `T` from the payer by `transferFrom` when it is positive: the inventory `X − F` and the caller's budget for a `SPEC-LIQ-019` floor shortfall, whose unused part returns in the payout; what arrived is accounted by the whole-call balance rise of `REQ-HELPER-008`. With `F > 0` it borrows `F` from the Pool: `POOL.flashLoan(address(this), [T], [F], [0], address(this), abi.encode(params), 0)`, where `flashLoan` is used, not `flashLoanSimple`, because only `flashLoan` waives the premium for an address holding `FLASH_BORROWER`. After the Pool returns it MUST revert with `InvalidFlashCallback` unless the used flag is set. With `F = 0` (operator variant) it records `address(0)` as the lender, sets the used flag, and runs the liquidation of `REQ-HELPER-006` itself with no loan: the prefund `X + params.maxShortfall` funds it, nothing is owed, so no top-up is pulled and the Pool is not approved; the Pool's callback cannot bind, because the used flag is set before LPVaultSwap is called and the recorded lender is `address(0)`, not `POOL`. Funds flow with `F = 0`: the Safe pays `X + maxShortfall`; LPVaultSwap pulls `X + shortfall` and pays `keeperOut`; the helper returns `payout = keeperOut + u` to the Safe, so the Safe's net outlay is `X + shortfall − keeperOut = D + A + B − R` (`SPEC-LIQ-010`, the shortfall cancelling), the same quantity the outlay bound of `REQ-HELPER-011` checks with a loan, less the premium. Example: Bob's liquidation (`D = X = 1,950` USDC, `R = 2,040.3`, `A = 19.5` USDC) with `F = 0`: the Safe pays 1,950 USDC and receives `keeperOut = 2,020.8` USDC, a net 70.8 USDC gain; with `R = 1,900` and `A = 0` the net outlay is 50 USDC above the 19.5 USDC bound and the call reverts with `OutlayExceeded(50, 19.5)`. The context stays open through the payout of `REQ-HELPER-008` and is cleared as the call's last step, so a `flashLiquidate` re-entered from a token transfer during the payout reverts with `ContextActive` and a replayed callback with `InvalidFlashCallback`. The context lives in transient storage, so it is empty at the start of every transaction and a reverted loan leaves nothing behind. (Source: Q-045, Q-063, Q-334, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-005` Callback binding. `executeOperation(assets, amounts, premiums, initiator, params)` MUST revert with `InvalidFlashCallback` unless a context is active and its used flag is clear, the context's lender is `POOL` and `msg.sender == POOL`, `initiator == address(this)`, `assets`, `amounts` and `premiums` each have length 1, `assets[0]` and `amounts[0]` equal the context's `T` and `F`, and `keccak256(params)` equals the context's hash. With `φ = premiums[0]` the premium the Pool reports, the callback MUST revert with `PremiumTooHigh(φ, maxPremium)` if `φ > maxPremium` and with `FlashFeeTooHigh` unless `φ × 10⁴ ≤ maxFlashFeeBps × F` (the ParameterStore's `maxFlashFeeBps`, read in the call, `SPEC-LIQ-015`). It MUST set the used flag before any external call, so a second callback in the same transaction fails. `executeOperation` is the helper's only callback: it has no ERC-3156 `onFlashLoan` or any other lender's callback (`REQ-HELPER-015`). Why: binding the lender, the initiator, the asset, the amount, the premium bound and a single-use context, not only the data hash, stops a third party from calling the callback with a forged loan, a context with no loan from being bound by a callback, and a replayed callback, and stops a Pool that changed its premium from charging more than the caller accepted. (Source: Q-045, Q-135, Q-335, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-006` Liquidation inside the callback. The callback MUST `forceApprove` LPVaultSwap for exactly `X + params.maxShortfall` of `T`, call `LP_VAULT_SWAP.liquidate(params)`, read the allowance left as the unused shortfall budget `u`, reset the allowance to 0, and measure `keeperOut` as the rise of its own `T` balance across that call plus what LPVaultSwap pulled in it, read as the allowance it consumed (`X + maxShortfall − u = X + shortfall`): LPVaultSwap pulls `X` (`REQ-SWAP-011`) and then, when the routes recover below the floor, exactly `shortfall` (`SPEC-LIQ-019`), so the balance falls by `X + shortfall` and rises by `keeperOut`. It reads `D` as the `debtRepaid` of the returned record, the debt LPVaultSwap measured by balance delta across `liquidationCall` (`REQ-SWAP-017`), capped at `X`; `D` sizes only the operator variant's bounds (`REQ-HELPER-011`). Example: `X = 2,047.5` USDC and `keeperOut = 2,118.3` USDC raise the balance by 70.8 USDC across the call, and the helper measures `70.8 + 2,047.5 = 2,118.3`. The floor case of `SPEC-LIQ-019`, `X = F = 12,000` USDC with `maxShortfall = 200` USDC: the helper holds and approves 12,200 USDC, LPVaultSwap pulls 12,000 USDC and then the 123.373998 USDC floor gap and pays `keeperOut = 12,297.373998` USDC, and `u = 76.626002` USDC stays in the helper. LPVaultSwap runs every check for the helper as caller, including the address freeze on the helper itself, which the public helper always passes because the MarketRegistry refuses to freeze it ([2.8](08-market-registry.md) `REQ-MREG-020`); it pulls `Kf` from the helper and pays `keeperOut` directly to it or reverts (`REQ-SWAP-026`). A frozen address calling the public helper is not stopped, because LPVaultSwap sees the helper (`REQ-SWAP-105`). (Source: Q-045, Q-119, Q-242, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-007` Repayment. With `owed = F + φ` and `held = keeperOut + u`, if `held < owed` the callback MUST pull `topUp = owed − held` of `T` from the payer, reverting with `TopUpExceeded(topUp, bound)` if `topUp > bound`, where `bound = maxTopUp` for the public helper and `bound = min(maxTopUp, ⌊D × MAX_TOP_UP_BPS / 10⁴⌋)` for the operator variant (`REQ-HELPER-011`); the unused shortfall budget pays toward the loan before any top-up, since both come from the same payer; what arrived is accounted by the whole-call balance rise of `REQ-HELPER-008`. It then `forceApprove`s the Pool for exactly `owed` and returns `true`; the Pool pulls `owed`. A revert here reverts the whole liquidation. The premium on the whole loan, including a loan larger than the debt Aave used, is paid from the payout the helper received, never by Avana's fee or the borrower surplus. A transfer fee on `T` stops a helper-funded liquidation as it stops every liquidation of `T` (`REQ-SWAP-123`): the helper receives less than `X` from its payer and the Pool, so LPVaultSwap's pull of exactly `X` fails, and a caller that funds `liquidate` itself meets Aave V4's refusal of the fee-reduced repayment (`InsufficientTransferred`); the whole transaction reverts, so no loan or premium is owed, until the fee returns to 0. A payout that arrives short, from any token that delivers less than was sent, lowers the payout, and a balance the helper held before the call never pays for it (`REQ-HELPER-008`). Units: debt units. Example: the dust liquidation with `X = F = 3` USDC at 5 bps: `owed = 3.0015`, `keeperOut = 2.95`, so `topUp = 0.0515` USDC, allowed with `maxTopUp ≥ 0.0515`; with `maxTopUp = 0` the helper reverts and the caller resubmits from its own funds. (Source: Q-060, Q-063, Q-119, Q-190, Q-397, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-008` Caller payout. After the loan returns and the context is checked, the helper MUST compute `payout` as the rise of its `T` balance over the whole `flashLiquidate` call, which equals `keeperOut + u + topUp − F − φ` (the prefund `X − F + maxShortfall`, the loan and LPVaultSwap's pull of `X + shortfall` leave `u`), reverting with `PayoutTooLow(0, minPayout)` if the balance ended below its start (possible only with a transfer fee on `T` and a balance the helper held before the call); revert with `PayoutTooLow(payout, minPayout)` if `payout < request.minPayout`; in the operator variant apply the outlay bound of `REQ-HELPER-011`; withdraw any allowance the lender left unpulled; transfer `payout` of `T` to the payout recipient; transfer to the payout recipient the rise of its balance of each watched token: the market's tokens and the `intermediates()` of each pool token's primary and backup route into `T` (route residues, dust legs, and the intermediate a partly filled multi-hop route returned, all of which LPVaultSwap pays it, `REQ-SWAP-024`), each read before the call; claim to the payout recipient every PayoutEscrow credit of a watched token that was 0 when the call started and is not now (`REQ-HELPER-010`); reset every allowance; and return `payout`. It pays only after `flashLoan` returns, so nothing leaves the helper while the loan is open. A payout or a market token the recipient cannot receive reverts the whole call: the helper escrows nothing for its recipient, who chose the address. `request.minPayout` bounds `payout`, which is net of the loan and its premium (`Q-345`) and includes the returned inventory `X − F` when `F < X` and the unused shortfall budget `u`; a caller that wants a net result of at least `n` sets `minPayout = X − F + maxShortfall + n`, and the Lens preview reports the payout the same way. `params.minPayout` stays the bound LPVaultSwap checks on `keeperOut`, and a helper's caller may leave it at 0, because the helper's own check binds. Example (`LIQ-069`): `keeperOut = 2,020.8` USDC reaches the helper, it repays `1,950 + 0.975 = 1,950.975` USDC and pays its caller `payout = 69.825` USDC, which passes `minPayout = 69` USDC. With `X = 2,047.5` and `F = 1,950` USDC, `keeperOut = 2,118.3` USDC and `payout = 167.325` USDC, of which 97.5 USDC is the caller's own inventory returned: a net minimum of 69 USDC is `minPayout = 166.5` USDC. (Source: Q-063, Q-097, Q-216, Q-345, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-009` Quotes, the same for every debt asset, GHO included: `maxFlashLoan(asset)` MUST return 0 unless the Pool's reserve of `asset` is active, not paused and flash-enabled, and otherwise `min(IERC20(asset).balanceOf(aToken), getVirtualUnderlyingBalance(asset))`, as the core flash source does (`REQ-FLASH-001`); `flashPremium(asset, amount)` MUST return 0 while the helper holds `FLASH_BORROWER` on the Pool's ACLManager, else the ceiling `⌈amount × FLASHLOAN_PREMIUM_TOTAL / 10⁴⌉`, which bounds the Pool's half-up charge from above. Example: 1,950 USDC at 5 bps without the role quotes `975,000`; with it, 0; 1,950 GHO at the same 5 bps quotes `0.975` GHO. (Source: Q-334, Q-335, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-010` Public instance. The public helper takes its payer and payout recipient from `msg.sender`, so a caller can pull from and pay only itself; anyone can call it. Its address is named in each chain's deployment manifest and is the MarketRegistry's implementation immutable `FLASH_LIQUIDATOR`, so `freezeAddress` refuses it (`ProtectedAddress`, [2.8](08-market-registry.md) `REQ-MREG-020`, `REQ-CONFIG-005`); the deployment check confirms that the helper's code is at that address with the chain's registry as `MARKET_REGISTRY`; the deployment payload requests `FLASH_BORROWER` for it (`REQ-FLASH-007`), and it is never registered as a committed operator, because it commits to nothing. A residue or dust leg LPVaultSwap could not deliver to the public helper is credited to the helper in the PayoutEscrow. That happens when a market token's issuer blocks the helper's own address, which USDC and USDT can do per address while the helper's caller could still receive, and the helper measures it inside the call that caused it: a watched token's credit that was 0 when `flashLiquidate` started and is not when the loan has returned is that caller's, and the helper claims it to the caller in the same call (`PayoutEscrow.claim(token, recipient)`, allowed then because LPVaultSwap has released the Lock, [2.18](18-payout-escrow.md)); a claim the caller cannot receive leaves the credit standing, so a residue never fails the liquidation. A credit that already stood when a call started (one no caller can be matched to), or one whose claim failed, stays for `claimEscrowed(token)`, which MUST be callable by anyone and MUST claim the helper's whole credit of `token` to `feeRecipient`, read from the ParameterStore as LPVaultSwap reads it (`REQ-SWAP-028`), reverting with `InvalidFeeRecipient` while it is `address(0)`. No credit is stranded, and each caller receives its own residues. A debt asset whose issuer blocks the public helper stops only liquidations through the helper: LPVaultSwap's payment of `keeperOut` to it reverts and is never escrowed (`REQ-SWAP-026`), so every helper call of that debt asset reverts, while callers still fund `liquidate` themselves (`REQ-SWAP-011`) or through their own operator helper. Monitoring alerts when a launch debt asset or market token blocks the public helper, and the playbook redeploys it and updates `FLASH_LIQUIDATOR`. (Source: Q-213, Q-242, Q-389, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-011` Operator variant. An operator's helper MUST refuse every caller except `SIGNER` (`NotSigner`), accept `F = 0` (`REQ-HELPER-003`, `REQ-HELPER-004`: no loan, the Safe funds all of `X`), pull the inventory `X − F`, the shortfall budget `params.maxShortfall` and every top-up only from `FUNDING_SAFE`, which approves the helper for the debt asset, and pay `payout`, every market token and every claimed credit only to `FUNDING_SAFE`. With `D` the debt LPVaultSwap repaid in the call (`REQ-HELPER-006`), it MUST revert with `FlashAmountAboveNeed(F, need)` once `liquidate` returns if `F > need = ⌈D × (10⁴ + LENS_DUST_MARGIN_BPS) / 10⁴⌉`; bound each top-up by `⌊D × MAX_TOP_UP_BPS / 10⁴⌋` as well as the request's `maxTopUp` (`REQ-HELPER-007`); and, after the loan returns, revert with `OutlayExceeded(outlay − payout, bound)` if the Safe's net outlay exceeds `bound = ⌊D × MAX_TOP_UP_BPS / 10⁴⌋`, where `outlay = (X − F) + maxShortfall + topUp` is everything pulled from the Safe and `payout` what returns to it. `claimEscrowed(token)` MUST be callable only by `SIGNER` and MUST claim the helper's PayoutEscrow credit of `token` to `FUNDING_SAFE`. The operator registers this helper's address, or its signer's address when it calls `liquidate` directly, as its committed operator (`REQ-SWAP-033`). Why: every input of a call (`debtToCover`, `F`, `maxShortfall`, `maxTopUp`) is the signer's, so a bound computed from them protects nothing against a compromised key: a bound of 1 % of `X` lets a signer name `X = 10,000,000` USDC against a 1,950 USDC debt and take a 4,929.2 USDC top-up, and with top-ups disabled the premium on an oversized loan is paid from the Safe's own inventory. The bounds therefore sit on the measured outcome. `D` is what the liquidation actually repaid; `need` is that debt plus the margin the Lens adds to a whole-debt quote (`REQ-SWAP-005`), so the premium the Safe can be charged is the premium on at most `1.05 × D`; and the outlay bound covers everything that can leave the Safe in one call: a top-up, a premium and a floor shortfall. Without a transfer fee the net outlay is `D + A + B − R + φ` (the `SPEC-LIQ-010` waterfall with the shortfall cancelling), so a call that gains for the Safe is never refused, and a compromised key can at worst cost the Safe `MAX_TOP_UP_BPS` of the debt each call repays, on liquidatable accounts, while the Safe's approval lasts. An operator sizes `F` at most the `D` its preview expects and funds any margin above it from inventory, which then returns in the payout. Example: `MAX_TOP_UP_BPS = 100` and Bob's liquidation repaying `D = 1,950` USDC (`R = 2,040.3`, `A = 19.5` USDC): `need = ⌈1,950 × 1.05⌉ = 2,047.5` USDC and the outlay bound is 19.5 USDC; a signer sending `X = 10,000,000` USDC with `F = 9,995,000` USDC is refused with `FlashAmountAboveNeed(9,995,000, 2,047.5)` and the Safe keeps its inventory; with `X = 2,047.5` and `F = 1,950` USDC, routes returning `R = 1,900` USDC (and `A = 0`) would cost the Safe 50.975 USDC and revert with `OutlayExceeded(50.975, 19.5)`, while `R = 1,940` USDC costs it 10.975 USDC and settles. (Source: Q-219, Q-381, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-012` The helper MUST NOT take a swap step, a route, a recipient other than its payer or the bound Safe, or executable calldata from the request: the unwind and the routes stay in the core, and `params` is passed to `liquidate` unchanged. Why: a periphery that swapped or called arbitrary targets would bring back the trust the core's fixed routes remove. (Source: Q-062, Q-335, Q-404)
- `REQ-HELPER-015` One lender. The helper MUST borrow every debt asset, GHO included, from `POOL` and from no other lender: it holds no other lender's address, has no lender callback other than the Pool's `executeOperation` (`REQ-HELPER-005`) and never mints the asset it borrows. GHO is quoted, borrowed, bounded and repaid exactly as USDC is (`REQ-HELPER-004` to `REQ-HELPER-009`): on Ethereum the V3 Core GHO reserve is supplied by GHO's direct minter and is flash-enabled, and on Base the Pool lends Base's bridged GHO (`REQ-FLASH-010`). Why one lender: the Pool already holds deep idle GHO on Ethereum, so a second lender would add a second callback, a second set of quotes and a second external dependency to an immutable contract without adding liquidity the liquidations need; when the Pool cannot lend enough GHO, the caller funds `liquidate` itself or uses a smaller `F` (`REQ-SWAP-014`). Why an immutable and not a MarketRegistry or ParameterStore record: the helper is immutable periphery with nothing to administer (`REQ-HELPER-001`); a lender read from governance storage would let a configuration change redirect every helper loan and would give the helper a governance dependency it does not otherwise have. A new Pool means a new helper, and the deployment check confirms the Pool address against the chain's manifest. Example: Ethereum, a 1,950 GHO liquidation at the Pool's 5 bps borrows 1,950 GHO from the Core Pool, which charges 0.975 GHO; the helper repays 1,950.975 GHO and pays its caller 69.825 GHO; a premium raised to 20 bps (3.9 GHO) above `maxFlashFeeBps = 10` reverts with `FlashFeeTooHigh` before the loan; with 1,000 GHO idle in the Pool's GHO reserve the call reverts with `InsufficientFlashLiquidity(1,000 GHO)`, and the caller funds the liquidation itself. (Source: Q-334, Q-335, Q-404)

| `PendingSettlementForwarded` | `settlePending` completes | `caller` (indexed), `marketId` (indexed), `vaultToken`, `units`, `amounts[]`, `asset`, `added` (`R`), `minPending`, `coverageSpoke`, `routesUsed`, `funded` (`Rt`, 0 when carried), `carried` (`carry[Tp]` after the call) |

`LiquidationSettled` carries no flash field and no `permissionless` flag: LPVaultSwap takes no loan, and every caller uses the same entry point. A flash liquidation helper emits `FlashLiquidated(caller, recipient, asset, flashAmount, premium, keeperOut, topUp, payout)` after its loan returns. The Spoke emits `LiquidationCall` (and `ReportDeficit` when applicable), the AaveAdapter `SeizedUnitsBurned`, Collateralization `SeizureSettled`, `SeizureReconciled` and `PendingSettled`, the payout escrow `RemainderEscrowed` and the Coverage Spoke `ReserveFunded`. Every liquidation is one transaction; failed attempts emit nothing. (Source: Q-076, Q-213, Q-214, Q-224, Q-241, Q-251, Q-255, Q-337)
