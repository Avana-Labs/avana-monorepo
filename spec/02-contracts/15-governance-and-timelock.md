# 2.15 Governance and Timelock

> **Status:** In review · **Owner:** Avana Labs · **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-12
>
> **Requirement prefix:** `REQ-ACCESS-NNN` · **Math prefix:** `SPEC-GOV-NNN`

## Purpose

Avana governance fixes who can change Avana, how fast, and who can stop a change. Governance is per chain (`Q-285`): Avana launches on Ethereum and Base (`Q-279`), and each chain has its own AvanaTimelock, governance Safe 4-of-7 and Emergency Guardian 3-of-5, beside the Aave Protocol Guardian and the Aave DAO's executor on that chain. No operation on one chain changes anything on another; there is no cross-chain Avana governance message. On each chain governance consists of one contract, one switch per Hub and one cross-contract table:

- **AvanaTimelock**: one non-upgradeable extension per chain of OpenZeppelin `TimelockController` (Contracts 5.1.0) that holds its own `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` and no other admin. Every privileged change to an Avana contract on that chain is an operation scheduled on it by a proposer (that chain's 4-of-7 governance multisig, or the Aave governance executor on that chain as second proposer), executed after its delay (12 hours when every call is on the short-delay list, a subset of the immutable `SHORT_ELIGIBLE` set; otherwise the normal delay, 5 days at launch and never below `MIN_NORMAL_DELAY` = 4 days) by anyone, within a 14-day grace window, unless a canceller cancels it first. On every upgradeable Avana contract the timelock holds `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` and `APPLICATION_ADMIN`, and no grant can ever give either role to another account (`REQ-ACCESS-153`); every immutable Avana contract with a privileged function checks `msg.sender == TIMELOCK`.
- **Application pause state**: one `PauseState` (`None`, `Frozen`, `Paused`) per Hub, held by the chain's MarketRegistry ([2.8](08-market-registry.md)), with a `PauseReason` (`General` or `MintPathSuspect`) while Paused. The 3-of-5 Emergency Guardian and the Aave Protocol Guardian move one Hub's state at once through `APPLICATION_PAUSE_GUARDIAN` and `APPLICATION_FREEZE_GUARDIAN` (`freeze(hub)`, `pause(hub, reason)`), or every Hub of the chain in one call (`freezeAll()`, `pauseAll(reason)`); the timelock moves it through `APPLICATION_ADMIN`. A borrow reads the state of its CDP Spoke's home Hub and of its debt reserve's funding Hub, so pausing a funding Hub stops every draw of its liquidity, cross-Hub lines included (`Q-283`). It stops new activity only: repay, fee collection, debt-free withdrawal and partial removal, zero-credit release, liquidation and pending settlement never read it, and under `Paused(General)` rescue top-ups still run.
- **Guardian incident tools**: the address freeze (`freezeAddress`, one frozen set per chain, read for every Hub of that chain), the manual trip and the reset of circuit breakers, and the switch of a token's price route to its pre-approved standby, all on the MarketRegistry. Each either stops new activity or puts a reviewed price route in service, acts at once, and never reaches an exit or liquidation; every step that loosens again (address unfreeze, switch back to the replaced route, a new price source) waits the normal delay, and only the Aave DAO can cancel the ones that restore liquidation or an address (`REQ-ACCESS-156`).
- **Restricted-function matrix**: the role and delay class of every privileged selector on every Avana contract, including the three deployment operations through which a chain gains a CDP Spoke, a collateral market or a debt reserve: `deployCdpSpoke`, `listCollateralMarket` and `enableDebtReserve` (`Q-286`).

The role table below is the single definition of Avana's governance roles; every other page uses these role names.

Outside governance: the Aave Hub and CDP Spoke (Borrow Spoke) configuration, which the Aave DAO governs through Aave's own AccessManager on each chain ([2.1](01-avana-hub-and-spokes.md)). Every CDP Spoke is a standard Aave V4 Spoke; the three settings Avana requires of every Avana collateral reserve (`receiveSharesEnabled = false`, `liquidationFee = 0`, no reinvestment controller) are set by the Aave DAO's listing payload, and Avana only checks them, at activation and through the RiskModule's drift check (`Q-281`); the roles `COVERAGE_ROLE` and `RESERVE_ADMIN_ROLE` of each Avana Umbrella (Coverage Spoke), Avana's protocol safety module, named after Aave's Umbrella whose design inspired it, which live in the Aave DAO's AccessManager ([2.14](14-first-loss-reserve.md)); the storage and logic of the keeper list (the registry of committed operators), routes and parameters (each contract owns its storage; governance fixes who may change it and with what delay); parameter values ([1.5](../01-architecture/05-parameters.md)); price states ([2.10](10-lp-oracle.md)); borrower entry points, which carry no role.

## Inputs

- `schedule` and `scheduleBatch` from the proposers: the governance multisig and the Aave governance executor (`PROPOSER_ROLE`).
- `cancel` from the cancellers: the governance multisig, the Emergency Guardian, the Aave Protocol Guardian and the Aave governance executor (`CANCELLER_ROLE`); role-change and restoring operations only from the Aave governance executor (`DAO_CANCELLER_ROLE`).
- `execute` and `executeBatch` from anyone while public execution is on; `cleanupExpiredOperation` from anyone at any time.
- `freeze(hub)`, `pause(hub, reason)`, `unfreeze(hub)`, `unpause(hub)`, `freezeAll()`, `pauseAll(reason)`, `freezeAddress`, `tripMarketBreaker`, `tripDebtBreaker`, `resetMarketBreaker`, `resetDebtBreaker` and `useStandbyRoute` on the MarketRegistry from the guardians; `cancelStandbySwitch` from the Aave governance executor.
- Reads by Avana contracts: `hasRole` (through `onlyRole`), the immutable `TIMELOCK` address, `MarketRegistry.pauseState(hub)` and `pauseReason(hub)` (AaveAdapter and Lens only), `MarketRegistry.isFrozenAddress` (AaveAdapter, ProxyManager, LPVaultSwap and Lens), the breaker views (RiskModule and Lens) and `activePriceRoutes` (LPOracles and DebtOracles).

## Outputs

- Execution of ready operations on Avana contracts, with `msg.sender = AvanaTimelock` at the target.
- The pause state and reason of each Hub, read by the AaveAdapter for pledge, re-credit, borrow, withdraw-with-debt, removal with debt and repay with collateral; the frozen-address set and the latched breakers, read on the new-activity paths listed in `REQ-ACCESS-082` and `REQ-ACCESS-166`.
- Events for every role, schedule, execution, cancellation, expiry clean-up, delay, selector-list, pause-state, pause-reason, address-freeze, breaker and standby-route change.
- No token movements. Neither the timelock nor the pause switch holds or moves tokens.

## Storage

### AvanaTimelock

OpenZeppelin `TimelockController` storage (`_timestamps[id]`, `_minDelay`, `AccessControl` roles) plus:

| Variable | Type | Description | Written by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SHORT_DELAY` | `uint256` constant | 43,200 seconds (12 hours); also what `getMinDelay()` returns | Constant |
| `MAX_DELAY` | `uint256` constant | 432,000 seconds (5 days); upper bound of `normalDelay` | Constant |
| `MIN_NORMAL_DELAY` | `uint256` constant | 345,600 seconds (4 days); lower bound of `normalDelay` (`SPEC-GOV-015`) | Constant |
| `GRACE_PERIOD` | `uint256` constant | 1,209,600 seconds (14 days); how long a ready operation stays executable | Constant |
| `_normalDelay` | `uint256` | Delay of every operation that is not entirely on the short-delay list; within `[MIN_NORMAL_DELAY, MAX_DELAY]`, launch 432,000 | Constructor; `setNormalDelay` (self) |
| `_shortEligible[target][selector]` | `mapping(address => mapping(bytes4 => bool))` | The immutable set `SHORT_ELIGIBLE` of `REQ-ACCESS-155`: every pair that may ever take the short delay | Constructor only; no function writes it afterwards |
| `_shortDelaySelector[target][selector]` | `mapping(address => mapping(bytes4 => bool))` | The short-delay list of `REQ-ACCESS-141`; always a subset of `SHORT_ELIGIBLE` | Constructor; `setShortDelaySelector` (self) |
| `_daoCancelSelector[target][selector]` | `mapping(address => mapping(bytes4 => bool))` | The immutable DAO-only cancel set of `REQ-ACCESS-156` | Constructor only; no function writes it afterwards |
| Public execution | OpenZeppelin open role: `EXECUTOR_ROLE` held by `address(0)` (`onlyRoleOrOpenRole`) | When held, anyone executes ready operations; `publicExecution()` returns `hasRole(EXECUTOR_ROLE, address(0))`; launch `true`. OpenZeppelin 5.1.0's `execute` admits any caller exactly when `address(0)` holds `EXECUTOR_ROLE` and its execution state is private, so this is the one representation an extension can keep consistent; a grant or revoke of `EXECUTOR_ROLE` for `address(0)` by any other path (`grantRole`, `revokeRole`, an `executors` entry of `address(0)`) reverts `PublicExecutionOnlyByToggle()`, so the class and cancel rule of `togglePublicExecution` (normal delay, any canceller, `REQ-ACCESS-029`) always apply | Constructor (`publicExecution_`); `togglePublicExecution` (self) |
| `_roleChangeOperation[id]` | `mapping(bytes32 => bool)` | True when every call of the scheduled operation is a role change (`REQ-ACCESS-143`); cleared on execution, cancel and clean-up | `schedule`, `scheduleBatch` |
| `_restoringOperation[id]` | `mapping(bytes32 => bool)` | True when every call of the scheduled operation is in the DAO-only cancel set (`REQ-ACCESS-156`); cleared on execution, cancel and clean-up | `schedule`, `scheduleBatch` |
| `_cleaned[id]` | `mapping(bytes32 => bool)` | True after `cleanupExpiredOperation(id)` until the same `id` is scheduled again; while true `getTimestamp(id)` returns 0, so the operation reads `Unset` to every OpenZeppelin check (OpenZeppelin's `_timestamps` is private and cannot be deleted by an extension) | `cleanupExpiredOperation`; cleared by `schedule`, `scheduleBatch` |

`_minDelay` stays `SHORT_DELAY` for life (`updateDelay` reverts), so OpenZeppelin's own floor never falls below the short delay. `SHORT_ELIGIBLE` and the DAO-only cancel set are written by the constructor and by nothing else, so adding a pair to either needs a new timelock, which every Avana contract can only adopt through an upgrade at the normal delay (`REQ-ACCESS-153`).

### Application pause state (MarketRegistry)

| Variable | Type | Description | Written by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pauseState[hub]` | `mapping(address => PauseState)` (`None`, `Frozen`, `Paused`) | The state of each Hub of the chain, `SPEC-GOV-004`; a Hub never registered reads `None` | `freeze`, `pause`, `unfreeze`, `unpause`, `freezeAll`, `pauseAll` |
| `pauseReason[hub]` | `mapping(address => PauseReason)` (`None`, `General`, `MintPathSuspect`) | The reason of the Hub's current pause; `None` outside `Paused` (`REQ-ACCESS-162`) | `pause(hub, reason)`, `pauseAll(reason)`, `unpause` |
| Frozen addresses and per-caller freeze buckets | Set of addresses; 24 one-hour counters per caller | The address freeze of `REQ-ACCESS-157`; one set per chain, applied to every Hub of the chain | `freezeAddress`, `unfreezeAddress` |
| Latched breakers | `LatchedBreaker` per market and per (CDP Spoke, debt reserve); `lastBreachAt` per market and per (CDP Spoke, debt reserve) | The latched security freezes (`DEFICIT`, `ISSUER`, `CONFIG_DRIFT`, `MANUAL`) and the last recorded breach of each temporary gate (`ORACLE`, `POOL_SHARE`, `POOL_DEPTH`) of `REQ-ACCESS-158` | `tripMarketBreaker`, `tripDebtBreaker`, `resetMarketBreaker`, `resetDebtBreaker`; the RiskModule's `recordBreach`, called from the permissionless `pokeBreakers` |
| Replaced route slot per token | `RouteSlot` | Which route of the token its standby replaces (`REQ-ACCESS-159`) | `useStandbyRoute`, `cancelStandbySwitch`, `restorePriceRoute`, `setPriceSource` |

The MarketRegistry keeps all of these in its own ERC-7201 namespace ([2.8](08-market-registry.md)); `gateRecoverySeconds` and the breaker thresholds are ParameterStore values ([2.9](09-parameter-store.md)). Avana has no in-kind settlement and so no in-kind window (`Q-401`).

### Role table

Role IDs are `bytes32` constants of `AvanaRoles` (`keccak256` of the role name) and OpenZeppelin's own role IDs. Caller checks that are not roles (the Adapter-only checks of the PositionProxy, ProxyManager, vault tokens and Collateralization, the `TIMELOCK` checks of immutable contracts, the `deleverage` flash callback check of LPVaultSwap's `IFlashSource` and the Pool callback check of each flash liquidation helper) are listed in `REQ-ACCESS-066` and on each contract's own page.

| ID | Role | Holders | Role admin | Functions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `REQ-ACCESS-001` | Timelock `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` | The AvanaTimelock itself only; the OpenZeppelin constructor `admin` argument is `address(0)` | Itself | Grants and revokes `PROPOSER_ROLE`, `CANCELLER_ROLE`, `EXECUTOR_ROLE`, `DAO_CANCELLER_ROLE` and `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` on the timelock, each as a normal-delay role-change operation. - `SPEC-GOV-001` — **Required delay of an operation.** For an operation with calls `(targetᵢ, payloadᵢ)`, `i = 1…n`, let `selᵢ` be the first four bytes of `payloadᵢ` (a payload shorter than four bytes has no selector and is never short). `requiredDelay = SHORT_DELAY` if `n ≥ 1` and `isShortDelaySelector(targetᵢ, selᵢ)` for every `i`; otherwise `requiredDelay = normalDelay`. `schedule` and `scheduleBatch` revert `DelayBelowRequired(delay, required)` when `delay < requiredDelay`; OpenZeppelin's own check `delay ≥ getMinDelay() = SHORT_DELAY` also applies. Units: seconds (`uint256`). No rounding. No overflow (selection between two stored values). (Source: Q-228)
  - Why: the delay is a property of what the operation does, read from its calldata, not something the proposer chooses. One non-listed call is enough to make the whole batch normal, so a loosening change cannot ride on a tightening batch to get the short delay. An empty batch has nothing to make it short and takes the normal delay.
  - Example: a batch `[ParameterStore.tightenMarketUsdCap(m, 4,000,000e8), MarketRegistry.disableRoute(m, USDT, GHO, 0)]` needs 43,200 s. Adding `ParameterStore.loosenTopUpSliceUsd(m, …)` makes it need 432,000 s, and scheduling it with `delay = 43,200` reverts `DelayBelowRequired(43200, 432000)`.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-01.
- `SPEC-GOV-002` — **Schedule and execution window.** Scheduling at `t_s` with `delay ≥ requiredDelay` stores `readyAt = t_s + delay` (OpenZeppelin `_timestamps[id]`). The operation is executable at time `t` iff `readyAt ≤ t < readyAt + GRACE_PERIOD`, its predecessor (if any) is done, and the caller may execute (`REQ-ACCESS-144`); before `readyAt` `execute` reverts `TimelockUnexpectedOperationState`, and from `readyAt + GRACE_PERIOD` on it reverts `OperationExpired(id)`. Units: seconds; `t` is `block.timestamp`. No rounding. Overflow: `t_s + delay + GRACE_PERIOD < 2^256` for any real timestamp and any `delay` a proposer can afford to wait. (Source: Q-228)
  - Why: scheduling publishes the exact call and its earliest execution time, which gives borrowers time to leave and cancellers time to act. The grace window stops an approved operation being executed long after conditions changed and reviewers stopped watching it.
  - Example: an upgrade scheduled at `t_s = 1,780,000,000` with `delay = 432,000` has `readyAt = 1,780,432,000`; it is executable from 1,780,432,000 up to and including 1,781,641,599, and at 1,781,641,600 (`readyAt + 1,209,600`) `execute` reverts `OperationExpired`.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-02.
- `SPEC-GOV-003` — **Normal-delay changes.** `setNormalDelay(newDelay)` requires `MIN_NORMAL_DELAY ≤ newDelay ≤ MAX_DELAY`, that is `345,600 ≤ newDelay ≤ 432,000` (else `DelayOutOfBounds(newDelay)`), and is itself an operation targeting the timelock, so it waits the current (old) normal delay. The new value applies to operations scheduled after it executes; an operation already scheduled keeps its `readyAt`. Hence, after a cut from `old` to `new`, the first operation that benefits executes no earlier than `old + new` after the cut was scheduled. Units: seconds. No rounding. No overflow (comparisons and one stored value). (Source: Q-228, Q-262)
  - Why: the normal delay can never exceed 5 days (borrowers and cancellers know the longest wait) nor fall below 4 days, the smallest delay that still holds the exit window of `SPEC-GOV-015` for a 3-day reaction; and shortening it is announced a full old delay in advance, so nobody can shorten the review window of a change that is already being prepared without that being visible for at least the old delay.
  - Example: `setNormalDelay(345,600)` scheduled at 1,780,000,000 executes at 1,780,432,000; an upgrade scheduled in that same second with `delay = 345,600` is ready at 1,780,777,600, 777,600 s (9 days) after the cut was published. `setNormalDelay(345,599)`, `setNormalDelay(259,200)` and `setNormalDelay(432,001)` revert `DelayOutOfBounds`.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-03, TEST-244, PROP-073.

### State machine and guardian bounds

- `SPEC-GOV-004` — **Pause-state transitions.** For each Hub `h` of the chain, with `s_h ∈ {None, Frozen, Paused}`, reason `ρ ∈ {None, General, MintPathSuspect}` (`ρ = None` exactly when `s ≠ Paused`) and caller roles `F` = `APPLICATION_FREEZE_GUARDIAN`, `P` = `APPLICATION_PAUSE_GUARDIAN`, `A` = `APPLICATION_ADMIN`: `freeze(h)`: `None → Frozen` iff `F ∨ A`; `pause(h, r)` with `r ∈ {General, MintPathSuspect}`: `None → Paused(r)` or `Frozen → Paused(r)` iff `P ∨ A`, and `Paused(ρ) → Paused(r)` iff `(P ∨ A) ∧ r ≠ ρ`; `unfreeze(h)`: `Frozen → None` iff `P ∨ A`; `unpause(h)`: `Paused(ρ) → None` with `ρ := None` iff `P ∨ A`. `freezeAll()` (iff `F ∨ A`) applies `freeze(h)` to every registered Hub `h` with `s_h = None` and leaves the others; `pauseAll(r)` (iff `P ∨ A`) applies `pause(h, r)` to every registered Hub with `(s_h, ρ_h) ≠ (Paused, r)` and leaves the others; when it changes no Hub, `freezeAll` reverts `AlreadyFrozen()` and `pauseAll` reverts `AlreadyPaused()`. A call on one Hub never changes another Hub's state, and no call on one chain changes another chain's. Any other call reverts: `Unauthorized(caller)` for a caller without the role; `InvalidHub(hub)` for a Hub not registered on the chain; `InvalidPauseReason()` for `pause(None)` or for `pause(ρ)` while already `Paused(ρ)`; `AlreadyFrozen()` for `freeze` in `Frozen`, `AlreadyPaused()` for `freeze` in `Paused`; `NotFrozen()` for `unfreeze` in `None`; `CannotUnfreezeWhilePaused()` for `unfreeze` in `Paused`; `NotPaused()` for `unpause` outside `Paused`. Each change of a Hub's state emits `PauseStateChanged(hub, previous, current, caller)` and each change of its reason emits `PauseReasonChanged(hub, previous, current, caller)`. Allowed actions per state are those of `REQ-ACCESS-072`, `REQ-ACCESS-073`, `REQ-ACCESS-074` (`General`) and `REQ-ACCESS-164` (`MintPathSuspect`). Units: none (enumerated state). No rounding. No overflow. (Source: Q-227, Q-252, Q-285)
  - Why: Frozen stops new exposure from new accounts and new debt while everything that reduces risk keeps working. Paused also stops borrowing, withdraw-with-debt and every pledge that grows exposure; the reason says whether the minting code itself is suspect. Under `General` a rescue top-up stays open, because adding collateral to an indebted account is the cheapest way out of liquidation and, capped at the restore amount and excluded from new borrowing, it cannot grow exposure; under `MintPathSuspect` nothing mints, because every top-up mints vault tokens. Liquidation is never stopped by any state because stopping it lets unhealthy accounts lose more value with nobody able to close them, which would turn a code incident into bad debt. Only the pause guardian or the admin can return to None, so a key that holds only the freeze role, used for fast containment, cannot undo a freeze, and unfreezing while Paused cannot silently skip the stronger state. The state is per Hub because a local incident (a stablecoin depeg in the Stable LP Hub's pools) should stop only that Hub, and `freezeAll` and `pauseAll` exist because the chain's shared contracts serve every Hub, so a code incident must be containable in one transaction.
  - Example: on Ethereum the Emergency Guardian calls `freeze(stableHub)` at 1,780,000,000 (`None → Frozen`); a wallet with no debt calling `pledge` reverts `ApplicationFrozen()`, while a keeper's `liquidate` and a borrower's `repay` succeed. The Aave Protocol Guardian then calls `pause(stableHub, General)` (`Frozen → Paused(General)`); an ordinary top-up of an account at health factor 1.30 reverts `NotRescueTopUp` and a borrow reverts `ApplicationPaused()`, while a rescue top-up of an account at health factor 1.04 (below `rescueHealthFactor` 1.10) whose charge is within its restore amount succeeds, and a liquidation succeeds. The Emergency Guardian raises the reason with `pause(stableHub, MintPathSuspect)`; the same rescue top-up now reverts `ApplicationPaused()`, a second `pause(stableHub, MintPathSuspect)` reverts `InvalidPauseReason()`, and liquidation still succeeds. A key holding only `APPLICATION_FREEZE_GUARDIAN` calling `unpause(stableHub)` reverts `Unauthorized`; `unfreeze(stableHub)` by the Emergency Guardian reverts `CannotUnfreezeWhilePaused()`; `unpause(stableHub)` by it returns the state to None and the reason to None. Throughout, a Correlated LP Hub on Ethereum stays `None` and the Base Stable LP Hub is untouched; a later `pauseAll(General)` on Ethereum changes every Ethereum Hub that is not already `Paused(General)`, and a second `pauseAll(General)` reverts `AlreadyPaused()`.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-07, MATH-GOV-08, TEST-235.
- `SPEC-GOV-005` — **Cancel rights.** For operation `id` with calls `(targetᵢ, payloadᵢ)`, `i = 1…n`, both marks are fixed at scheduling: `roleChange(id) = (n ≥ 1) ∧ ∀ i: selector(payloadᵢ) ∈ {grantRole, revokeRole, renounceRole}`, and `restoring(id) = (n ≥ 1) ∧ ∀ i: daoOnly(targetᵢ, payloadᵢ)`, where `daoOnly(t, p)` holds iff `(t, selector(p))` is in the immutable DAO-only cancel set of `REQ-ACCESS-156`. `cancel(id)` by caller `c` succeeds iff the operation is pending and `hasRole(CANCELLER_ROLE, c) ∧ (¬roleChange(id) ∧ ¬restoring(id) ∨ hasRole(DAO_CANCELLER_ROLE, c))`; otherwise it reverts `AccessControlUnauthorizedAccount` (no canceller role), `RoleChangeCancelRestricted(id)` or `RestoringCancelRestricted(id)`. Units: none (predicates). No rounding. No overflow. (Source: Q-136, Q-228, Q-263)
  - Why: the guardians and the governance multisig can stop any risky change, but none of them can stop its own removal or a replacement's appointment, because a pure role change is cancellable only by the Aave governance executor. The marking requires every call to be a role call: if one role call were enough, a hostile proposer could add a no-op `grantRole` to a malicious upgrade batch and only the Aave governance executor could cancel it, shielding the upgrade from the Emergency Guardian, the Aave Protocol Guardian and the multisig. Under every-call marking a guardian can cancel a mixed batch that also revokes it, but that only delays its removal: governance then schedules the revocation as a pure role-change operation, which no guardian can cancel. Restoring operations follow the same pattern: each of their calls only puts back configuration that was reviewed at the normal delay (a previously approved swap route, a standby recorded at listing, a keeper, an address's access), so a compromised canceller that could cancel them could keep a market unliquidatable or an address frozen for as long as its role lasted, while the Aave DAO alone stopping them costs little. A new price source (`setPriceSource`) is new configuration and keeps the ordinary rule.
  - Example: a batch `[MarketRegistry.revokeRole(APPLICATION_PAUSE_GUARDIAN, emergencyGuardian), MarketRegistry.unpause(stableHub)]` is not a role-change operation, so any of the four cancellers, the Emergency Guardian included, may cancel it. Governance then schedules `[MarketRegistry.revokeRole(APPLICATION_PAUSE_GUARDIAN, emergencyGuardian)]` alone: the Emergency Guardian's `cancel` reverts `RoleChangeCancelRestricted` and only the Aave governance executor's `cancel` succeeds. Likewise `[AaveAdapter.upgradeToAndCall(impl, ""), MarketRegistry.grantRole(APPLICATION_PAUSE_GUARDIAN, g)]` stays cancellable by every canceller. `[MarketRegistry.restoreRoute(7, USDT, GHO, 0, route), MarketRegistry.restoreRoute(7, USDC, GHO, 0, route2)]` scheduled at the short delay is restoring: the Emergency Guardian's `cancel` reverts `RestoringCancelRestricted`. Adding `ParameterStore.loosenTopUpSliceUsd(7, …)` to the batch makes it neither short nor restoring, so every canceller may cancel it; `[MarketRegistry.disableRoute(7, USDT, GHO, 0)]` is not restoring either.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-09, MATH-GOV-10, TEST-244, PROP-073.
- `SPEC-GOV-006` — **Removal of a guardian.** A guardian role is revoked by a role-change operation on the MarketRegistry, every call of which is `revokeRole`, scheduled at `t_s` with `delay ≥ normalDelay`. No guardian can cancel it (`SPEC-GOV-005`), and public execution lets anyone execute it, so the guardian's last moment of power is `readyAt = t_s + delay`, normally `t_s + normalDelay`. Returning each Hub to None is a separate step: `unpause(hub)` (or `unfreeze(hub)`) by the other holder of `APPLICATION_PAUSE_GUARDIAN` at once, or by `APPLICATION_ADMIN` in a separate operation scheduled with the revocation and ready at the same time. Units: seconds. No rounding. No overflow. (Source: Q-136, Q-227, Q-228)
  - Why: a guardian that keeps Avana frozen or paused against governance stops only new activity, because exits and liquidation never read the pause state, and its power ends within one normal delay; that bound replaces the incident clock and cooldown. The other guardian holding the same roles can also unpause at once in the meantime.
  - Example: the governance multisig schedules `[revokeRole(APPLICATION_PAUSE_GUARDIAN, g), revokeRole(APPLICATION_FREEZE_GUARDIAN, g)]` at 1,780,000,000 with `delay = 432,000`, and `[unpause(stableHub)]` as a second operation in the same block. Only the Aave governance executor can cancel the first; `g` may cancel the second, but at 1,780,432,000 anyone executes the revocation, after which `g` can neither pause nor cancel, and the other guardian or a rescheduled `unpause(stableHub)` returns the state to None. A guardian of one chain has no role on any other chain, so its removal is scheduled on each chain where it holds a role. Until then repay, debt-free withdrawal and liquidation run normally.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-02, MATH-GOV-10.
- `REQ-ACCESS-085` — `freeze(hub)`, `pause(hub, reason)`, `freezeAll()` and `pauseAll(reason)` MUST change states only per `SPEC-GOV-004`, emit `PauseStateChanged(hub, previous, current, msg.sender)` (and `PauseReasonChanged` for a new reason) for each Hub changed, and revert `AlreadyFrozen()`, `AlreadyPaused()`, `InvalidPauseReason()` or `InvalidHub(hub)` when the transition does not apply (`freezeAll` reverts `AlreadyFrozen()` and `pauseAll` reverts `AlreadyPaused()` when no Hub changes); none has an expiry, and each takes only its Hub (or none, for the chain-wide calls) and its reason. ### Keepers, routes and role holders in an incident

- `REQ-ACCESS-091` — A misbehaving keeper, route or role holder MUST be handled by: `freezeAddress` of the keeper (immediate) and `removeKeeper` (short, keeper floor `SPEC-GOV-008`); `disableRoute` (short), with a guardian breaker trip if every route of a token is confirmed unable to settle valid liquidations at the required size; a standby switch (`useStandbyRoute`, immediate) for a failing price route; and `revokeRole` of a guardian, canceller or proposer (role-change operation, normal delay, DAO-only cancel). Meanwhile the guardians may `freeze(hub)`, `pause(hub, reason)`, `freezeAll()`, `pauseAll(reason)` or trip a breaker, none of which stops liquidation. (Source: Q-155, Q-156, Q-227, Q-228, Q-241, Q-242, Q-248, Q-399, Q-401)
- `SPEC-GOV-007` — **Cancel window.** A canceller may cancel operation `id` at any `t` with `t_s ≤ t` while the operation is pending (scheduled, not executed, not cancelled), including after it expired. Execution happens at some `t_exec ≥ readyAt = t_s + delay ≥ t_s + requiredDelay`, so the cancel window `[t_s, t_exec)` lasts at least `requiredDelay`: 43,200 s for a short-delay operation, `normalDelay` for any other. Units: seconds. No rounding. No overflow. (Source: Q-228)
  - Why: the cancellers' review time is guaranteed by the schedule itself, not by anyone's reaction speed, and an expired operation can be removed by a canceller or by anyone through `cleanupExpiredOperation`.
  - Example: `MarketRegistry.disableRoute(m, USDC, GHO, 0)` scheduled at 1,780,000,000 with `delay = 43,200` can be cancelled at any time until it is executed, which is no earlier than 1,780,043,200.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-15.
- `REQ-ACCESS-092` — Registration grants no power: a misbehaving committed operator, registered or removed, frozen (calling through the public flash liquidation helper) or not, can only do what any caller can, liquidate accounts Aave deems liquidatable, through Aave's sizing, Avana's recovery floor and the per-leg oracle `minOut`, and it draws no Avana funds, because no SettlementBudget exists (`Q-397`). - `REQ-ACCESS-101` — `graceHealthFactorMin` (0.90 WAD), `graceDurationMax` (7,200 seconds) and `consistencyToleranceMinBps` (10 bps) MUST be immutable constants of the ParameterStore implementation, one value for every market, changed only by an upgrade at the normal delay; they bound properties of the price and grace mechanism that do not depend on a market's route. `slippageAllowanceMinBps` is per-market storage (`REQ-ACCESS-095`) because the cost of converting seized tokens depends on each market's swap routes and pool depth. Open records per account per market are bounded by the immutable `MAX_RECORDS_PER_ACCOUNT_MARKET` = 16 (`Q-287`), so the seizure of an account's last chunk under Aave's dust rule always fits one transaction; no setter changes it. (Source: Q-139, Q-194, Q-209, Q-210, Q-287)
- `SPEC-GOV-008` — **Keeper floor.** Let `K` be the registered committed operators (the keeper list) and `A = {k ∈ K : ¬isFrozenAddress(k)}` the active committed operators, `|A|` = `LPVaultSwap.activeKeeperCount()` at execution, and `keeperFloor = 1` (Avana's own keeper). `removeKeeper(k)` succeeds iff `k ∈ K` and `|A| − [k ∈ A] ≥ keeperFloor`, else it reverts `KeeperFloor(|A| − [k ∈ A], keeperFloor)` (or `InvalidKeeper` for `k ∉ K`); `addKeeper` reverts for a frozen address, never lowers `|A|`, and reverts once `|K| = MAX_KEEPERS` (32). `freezeAddress(k)` is never blocked by the floor: it can take `|A|` to 0, and while `|A| = 0` the RiskModule refuses new borrowing and withdraw-with-debt on every Hub of the chain (`LiquidationHalted`, [2.7](07-risk-module.md) `REQ-RISK-071`). Hence a timelock removal never leaves the chain without an active committed operator, and only a guardian freeze can, which fails closed for new debt. The registry gates no liquidation: `liquidate` is open to every address that is not frozen whatever `|A|` is (`Q-404`). `[·]` is 1 when the condition holds, else 0. Units: count (`uint256`). No rounding. No overflow (`|A| ≥ 1` whenever `k ∈ A`). (Source: Q-139, Q-155, Q-242, Q-400, Q-404)
  - Why: any address can liquidate, but only a committed operator is bound to liquidate accounts whose bonus does not cover the gas. Each chain has one LPVaultSwap serving every Hub there, so the floor is counted per chain, and an operator funded only on Ethereum cannot liquidate Base debt (`Q-289`). Avana's own keeper liquidates whatever Aave allows per call and pays its gas as an operating cost (`Q-381`, `Q-395`), so one active committed operator is enough; more can be registered at the normal delay. A guardian must be able to freeze a compromised operator at once, even the last one, so the freeze is not limited by the floor; instead new debt stops while no operator is active, and `liquidate` stays open to every address for existing debt.
  - Example: with 2 registered committed operators (Avana's keeper and one more), none frozen, `removeKeeper` of the second leaves 1 active operator and succeeds; a later `removeKeeper` of Avana's keeper reverts `KeeperFloor(0, 1)` at execution. The Emergency Guardian then freezes Avana's keeper after a key compromise: the freeze succeeds, `|A| = 0`, every `borrow` on the chain reverts `LiquidationHalted`, while repay, withdrawal and `liquidate` by any caller continue, the frozen keeper included when it calls through the public flash liquidation helper. An `addKeeper` of a fresh keeper address (normal delay, DAO-only cancel) makes the active count 1 again and borrowing reopens.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-12, TEST-229, PROP-071.
- `SPEC-GOV-009` — **Tolerance tightening.** `tightenConsistencyToleranceBps(m, v)` succeeds at execution iff `consistencyToleranceMinBps ≤ v < τ(m)`, and `tightenFeedAgreementToleranceBps(token, v)` iff `feedAgreementToleranceMinBps(token) ≤ v < T(token)`, where `τ(m)` and `T(token)` are the stored values at execution; otherwise `LivenessBound` (below the bound) or `NotStricter`. No override or second value exists: the LPOracle reads `τ(m)` and `T(token)` directly. Units: ticks (`uint24`) and bps (`uint16`). No rounding. No overflow (comparisons). (Source: Q-139, Q-184, Q-228)
  - Why: a tightening at the short delay can make the price checks stricter, never looser, and never so strict that honest prices fail and liquidation stops.
  - Example: market `m` has `consistencyToleranceBps = 50` and `consistencyToleranceMinBps = 10`. `tightenConsistencyToleranceBps(m, 30)` executes (`10 ≤ 30 < 50`); a second queued `tightenConsistencyToleranceBps(m, 40)` then reverts `NotStricter` at its execution; `tightenConsistencyToleranceBps(m, 8)` reverts `LivenessBound`. Returning to 50 is `setConsistencyToleranceBps(m, 50)` at the normal delay.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-11.
- `SPEC-GOV-010` — **No in-kind window.** Avana has no in-kind settlement (`Q-401`): `enableInKind`, `disableInKind`, `autoEnableInKind`, `inKindUntil`, `inKindExpiry`, `inKindAutoDelay` and `inKindAutoTrigger` do not exist, and every liquidation converts the seized tokens into the debt asset through approved swap routes. The set of governance-timed settlement states is empty, so `isSettlementModeTimed(m) = false` for every market `m` at every `t`. The PayoutEscrow, under its existing name, is payout escrow only: a payout `x` (borrower surplus or Avana fee in the debt asset repaid, or a route residue or dust leg in the token released; never the caller's payout, which is paid directly or the liquidation reverts) whose transfer reverts, returns `false` or exhausts `PAYOUT_TRANSFER_GAS` is credited to its payee and claimable for life, with `credited = x` exactly. Units: base units of the payout's token for `x`. No rounding. No overflow. (Source: Q-133, Q-163, Q-228, Q-401, Q-404)
  - Why: an in-kind mode handed seized pool tokens to keepers and borrowers on a governance or automatic trigger, which added a second settlement path, its own timers and a DAO-only restoring selector. Without it, a failing route is handled by the caller's shortfall top-up, by restoring or replacing the route, and by a guardian freeze of new borrowing when routes are confirmed unable to settle; the escrow stays because a token can still refuse an ordinary payout transfer.
  - Example: a USDT liquidation pays the borrower a surplus of 412.5 USDT; the borrower is blocked by USDT, the transfer reverts, and the PayoutEscrow credits 412.5 USDT to the borrower, claimable once the block is lifted. A call of `enableInKind(7)` reverts, because no such function exists.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-13.
- `SPEC-GOV-011` — **Tighten predicate and loosening steps.** For a parameter `p` with stored value `old` at execution and safer direction `s` (column "Rule" of the matrix), `tighten<P>(new)` succeeds iff (`s` = lower ∧ `new < old`) ∨ (`s` = higher ∧ `new > old`), `new` is within its 1.5 hard bounds, and `new` is within any liveness bound above; otherwise it reverts `NotStricter`, `OutOfBounds` or `LivenessBound`. A loosening of a step-limited parameter (`REQ-PSTORE-017`) additionally requires, with `key = keccak256(abi.encode(name, scope))` per parameter and scope (market, token, depositor or debt reserve): one step, that is `old − new ≤ 0.03e18` for `hardHealthFloor` (a value of 0, off, counts as 1.00e18), `new × 10⁴ ≤ old × 15,000` for `marketUsdCap`, `tokenUsdCap` and `depositorUsdCap`, the same for `topUpSliceUsd` with `old` replaced by `max(old, ⌊marketUsdCap / 10⌋)`, and `new − old ≤ 25` bps for `slippageAllowanceBps`, else `LoosenStepTooLarge(key)`; and spacing, `lastLoosenedAt[key] = 0 ∨ block.timestamp ≥ lastLoosenedAt[key] + normalDelay`, else `LoosenTooSoon(key, lastLoosenedAt[key] + normalDelay)`; a successful loosening sets `lastLoosenedAt[key] = block.timestamp`. Every predicate is evaluated when the operation executes, not when it is scheduled. Tightenings have no step or spacing limit. Units: those of `p` (WAD, 8-decimal USD, bps); `key` `bytes32`; times in seconds. Rounding: the cap step compares exact products (`new × 10⁴` against `old × 15,000`), so it never rounds in the loosener's favour; the slice base `⌊marketUsdCap / 10⌋` rounds down. No overflow: caps are at most `uint128` values and `× 15,000` fits in `uint256`. (Source: Q-114, Q-139, Q-228, Q-246, Q-398)
  - Why: tighten selectors are the only parameter setters with the short delay, so they must be unable to loosen anything; evaluating at execution means two tighten operations queued in a different order, or a loosening that executed meanwhile, can never turn a short-delay operation into a loosening. Each loosening can look safe on its own while risk accumulates through many small ones; one bounded step per parameter per normal delay keeps every step reviewable and gives borrowers and cancellers the whole window for each, while tightening stays fast for incidents. The slice base lets a slice tightened to 0 in an incident return to a tenth of its market cap in one step.
  - Example: with `hardHealthFloor(m) = 1.10`, set after a stress test justified it (safer = higher, bounds 0 or 1.00–1.25, launch 0), `tightenHardHealthFloor(m, 1.12)` succeeds, `(m, 1.08)` reverts `NotStricter` and `(m, 1.30)` reverts `OutOfBounds`; `loosenHardHealthFloor(m, 1.07)` succeeds and `(m, 1.06)` reverts `LoosenStepTooLarge`; from 1.03 one loosening may switch it off (`(m, 0)`, counted as 1.00). For a market listed with `slippageAllowanceMinBps = 40` and `slippageAllowanceBps = 100`, `tightenSlippageAllowanceBps(m, 60)` succeeds and `(m, 30)` reverts `LivenessBound`; `loosenSlippageAllowanceBps(m, 125)` succeeds and `(m, 126)` reverts `LoosenStepTooLarge`. With `marketUsdCap = 4,000,000e8`, `loosenMarketUsdCap(m, 6,000,000e8)` executed at 1,780,432,000 succeeds; a second loosening executed at 1,780,700,000 with `normalDelay = 432,000` reverts `LoosenTooSoon(key, 1,780,864,000)`, while `tightenMarketUsdCap(m, 5,000,000e8)` at that time succeeds. A slice tightened to 0 in a market with a 4,000,000e8 cap can be loosened to at most `400,000e8 × 1.5 = 600,000e8`. With `minPledgeUsd = 10e8`, `tightenMinPledgeUsd(m, 25e8)` succeeds at the short delay and `loosenMinPledgeUsd(m, 10e8)` waits the normal delay.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-11, TEST-225.
- `SPEC-GOV-012` — **Liveness and governance bounds.** Every setter below checks its new value `v` against fixed bounds and reverts otherwise: `graceHealthFactorMin ≤ graceHealthFactor < 1e18` and `graceDuration ≤ graceDurationMax` (`LivenessBound`, ParameterStore); `consistencyToleranceBps ≥ consistencyToleranceMinBps` (`LivenessBound`); `600 ≤ gateRecoverySeconds ≤ 7,200` (`OutOfBounds`, ParameterStore); `MIN_NORMAL_DELAY ≤ normalDelay ≤ MAX_DELAY` (`DelayOutOfBounds`, AvanaTimelock). Values (`Q-210`, `Q-228`, `Q-262`): `graceHealthFactorMin = 0.90` WAD, `graceDurationMax = 7,200` s, `consistencyToleranceMinBps = 10`, `SHORT_DELAY = 43,200` s, `MIN_NORMAL_DELAY = 345,600` s, `MAX_DELAY = 432,000` s. Derived bounds: after each price recovery an account with health factor in `[graceHealthFactor, 1)` waits at most `graceDuration ≤ 7,200` s, while an account below `graceHealthFactor` (never below 0.90) stays liquidatable throughout; a temporary breaker gate stays closed at most 2 hours after its condition clears; no operation that needs the normal delay waits less than 4 days or more than 5 days to become ready unless its proposer chose a longer delay. Units: WAD, seconds, bps (for concentrated liquidity one bp of `consistencyToleranceBps` is one tick of tolerance). No rounding. No overflow (comparisons only). (Source: Q-139, Q-163, Q-209, Q-210, Q-228, Q-262, Q-399, Q-401)
  - Why: these are the settings a timelock operation could use to stop liquidation or keep it stopped, or to stretch governance beyond what borrowers can plan for. A grace floor of 0.90 means grace protects only accounts still close to healthy, and the 2-hour cap ends that protection quickly; a 10-tick floor (about 0.1 % price difference, since `1.0001^10 ≈ 1.001`) keeps honest TWAP and Chainlink prices in agreement; the recovery bound keeps a temporary gate from holding new activity closed long after a price or depth breach ends, and its 600-second floor stops a gate flickering open between breaches; the delay bounds keep every short review window at 12 hours and every normal one between 4 and 5 days.
  - Example: at launch values (`graceHealthFactor = 0.95`, `graceDuration = 1,800`, `consistencyToleranceBps = 50`, `gateRecoverySeconds = 1,800`, `normalDelay = 432,000`) every bound holds. `setGraceHealthFactor(0.88e18)` reverts `LivenessBound`; `setGraceDuration(7,200)` succeeds and `setGraceDuration(7,201)` reverts `LivenessBound`; `tightenConsistencyToleranceBps(m, 9)` reverts `LivenessBound`; `loosenGateRecoverySeconds(300)` and `tightenGateRecoverySeconds(7,201)` revert `OutOfBounds`; `setNormalDelay(864,000)` and `setNormalDelay(300,000)` revert `DelayOutOfBounds`.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-14, MATH-GOV-03, TEST-244.

## Multisig requirements

- `REQ-ACCESS-102` — Each chain's governance multisig MUST be a Safe with seven signers from Avana Labs and external parties and a threshold of four (`governanceMultisigThreshold` 4-of-7). (Source: Q-114, Q-228)
- `REQ-ACCESS-103` — Each chain's Emergency Guardian MUST be an independent Safe with five signers and a threshold of three (`emergencyGuardianThreshold` 3-of-5). (Source: Q-228)
- `REQ-ACCESS-104` — On each chain, the signer sets of the four Safes (governance multisig, Emergency Guardian, Aave Protocol Guardian, Joint Configuration Safe) MUST be pairwise disjoint: no signer key is shared; there is no separate LPVaultSwap operations multisig. The live-config check reads `getOwners()` and `getThreshold()` of each Safe before `deployments/verified/` and after every owner change, and monitoring alerts on any owner or threshold change of the four Safes. Every signer of the governance multisig and the Emergency Guardian follows the key-security standard of `Q-245` (governance/README): a dedicated hardware wallet with on-device typed-data display; at most 3 of the 7 governance signers from Avana Labs, spanning at least 3 independent organisations and 3 jurisdictions; 5 different Emergency Guardian signers across at least 3 time zones; calldata decoded from the raw transaction, the Safe transaction hash recomputed against the device, a fork simulation and a check of `requiredDelay` against the intended class for every transaction; a monthly signed liveness message; replacement within 72 hours of departure or suspected compromise; and rotation at least every 24 months. A Safe guard limits the governance multisig to calls to the AvanaTimelock and to itself, and a delay module makes its owner and threshold changes wait 48 hours. (Source: Q-014, Q-090, Q-114, Q-228, Q-245)
- `REQ-ACCESS-173` — Governance MUST be per chain (`Q-285`). Each chain where Avana runs (Ethereum and Base at launch) has its own AvanaTimelock, governance Safe 4-of-7 and Emergency Guardian 3-of-5, each meeting `REQ-ACCESS-102`…`REQ-ACCESS-104` on that chain; the signer sets of two chains' Safes may be the same people or different ones, and the key-security standard applies to each. No AvanaTimelock, Safe or guardian role on one chain has any power on another, no Avana contract accepts a cross-chain message, and an operation that must apply on two chains is scheduled separately on each, with its own delay and cancellers. The Aave DAO acts on each chain through its executor there: on Base, through the Aave DAO's Base executor, which receives payloads over Aave's cross-chain governance; the emergency runbook ([3.6](../03-operations/06-emergency-operations.md)) states that path's delivery time and that Aave-side actions on Base (reserve freeze, Hub halt, `drawCap` changes) can lag Ethereum's. Why: one chain's incident or compromised key must not reach the other chain's contracts, and a cross-chain message path would add a bridge to the trust base of every upgrade. Example: an AaveAdapter fix is scheduled on the Ethereum timelock and, separately, on the Base timelock; the Base Emergency Guardian can cancel the Base operation without affecting the Ethereum one, and each executes after its own chain's normal delay. (Source: Q-228, Q-279, Q-285)
- `REQ-ACCESS-160` — The Aave governance executor of each chain MUST hold `PROPOSER_ROLE` on that chain's AvanaTimelock beside its governance multisig, granted at deployment (`REQ-ACCESS-027`). `PROPOSER_ROLE` MUST never have fewer than two holders: the live-config check fails, and monitoring pages, when it does, and every role-change operation that would leave fewer than two proposers is a configuration error that the Aave governance executor, the only canceller of role-change operations, cancels. The constructor refuses fewer than two proposers (`REQ-ACCESS-183`). The executor's operations wait the same delays and are cancellable by the same cancellers as the multisig's. With the governance multisig as the only proposer, losing four of its keys or renouncing the role by mistake would stop every repair for good; the DAO as second proposer restores that path through Aave governance, which every canceller still watches. Checked by: TEST-224. (Source: Q-245)

- `REQ-ACCESS-105` — Handover of administration to the Aave DAO MUST follow the pre-agreed plan on each chain: that chain's governance multisig schedules one operation on its timelock, every call of which is `grantRole` or `revokeRole` (so it is a role-change operation: normal delay, DAO-only cancel), that grants `EXECUTOR_ROLE` to the Aave governance executor and `PROPOSER_ROLE` and `EXECUTOR_ROLE` to a second DAO-named holder, and revokes both roles from the governance multisig, revoking last in the batch, so `PROPOSER_ROLE` keeps at least two holders throughout (`REQ-ACCESS-160`). `APPLICATION_ADMIN` and `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` stay with the timelock; the guardian, canceller and DAO canceller roles are unchanged by the handover. (Source: Q-114, Q-228, Q-245)
- `REQ-ACCESS-106` — Wind-down MUST follow the pre-agreed offboarding plan on each chain: `setMarketOffboarding(marketId)` for every market of the chain (short delay) with an Aave freeze of each vault-token reserve; keepers keep liquidating; repay, fee collection and withdrawal stay open; no force-close power exists; Avana holds no settlement fund to return (`Q-397`), and payouts held in the PayoutEscrow stay claimable by their payees. (Source: Q-052, Q-114, Q-167, Q-228, Q-397, Q-401)
- `REQ-ACCESS-107` — If the governance multisig stops acting, every Avana contract keeps running on its current configuration, operations already scheduled stay executable by anyone within their grace window and cancellable by the cancellers, the guardians keep their immediate powers (`REQ-ACCESS-089`), and the Aave DAO keeps its Aave-side controls (drawCaps, reserve freeze and pause, Hub halt), its cancel rights and its proposer role: through Aave governance the executor schedules any repair, including the revocation of the multisig's roles, at the same delays (`REQ-ACCESS-160`). (Source: Q-032, Q-092, Q-114, Q-228, Q-245)

## Risk-parameter change process

- `REQ-ACCESS-161` — Every Avana-side risk parameter MUST change through one process: (1) the risk service providers publish the change with the cap-sizing calculation of `Q-249`; (2) the governance multisig builds the operation and every signer follows the key-security standard of `REQ-ACCESS-104`; (3) it is scheduled with `delay ≥ requiredDelay` (`SPEC-GOV-001`), the short delay only when every call is a tightening on the short-delay list, so one loosening call makes the whole batch wait the normal delay; (4) monitoring alerts on its `CallScheduled` and any canceller may cancel during the delay; (5) anyone executes it from its ready time until `GRACE_PERIOD` later, and each setter re-checks its hard, liveness, step and spacing bounds at execution (`SPEC-GOV-011`); (6) the live-configuration check and the Lens cap headroom confirm the result. Every economic or risk value Avana publishes is a proposed default whose final value is set in the listing or funding ARFC (`Q-394`). Aave-side values (collateral factor, bonus curve, target health factor, Hub and Spoke caps, credit lines, reserve minimums) change through Aave governance; Avana's `aaveTargetHealthFactor` 1.10 and `minLiquidationBonus` ≥ 2 % are its recommended guidance for the listing AIP, and the Aave DAO decides them (`Q-393`); for a collateral-factor cut Avana first tightens `poolRiskFactor` at the short delay, so the drift check (`REQ-RISK-069`) stops new borrowing while Aave's factor is above the new expected one, and the Aave payload then lowers it; for a raise the Avana change at the normal delay executes first. Checked by: TEST-225. (Source: Q-110, Q-196, Q-228, Q-246, Q-270, Q-393, Q-394, Q-398)

## Restrictions

What these contracts must never allow. The global invariants these rules preserve (governance cannot move user collateral outside the stated custody trust, exits and liquidation stay open, the timelock cannot be bypassed) are stated in [4.1 Global Invariants](../04-security/01-global-invariants.md).

- `REQ-ACCESS-111` — No account MAY, except through a timelock operation that waited its required delay, upgrade an Avana contract, replace a flash source, change a role or a delay, register a CDP Spoke, list a market, enable a debt reserve, enable cross-Hub lines, add a price source, swap route or keeper, or change any parameter. PositionProxies have no code to upgrade. (Source: Q-228)
- `REQ-ACCESS-112` — The Emergency Guardian and the Aave Protocol Guardian MUST NOT be able to upgrade, change parameters, propose, move funds, choose an unreviewed price source, unfreeze an address, cancel role-change or restoring operations, block an exit or liquidation, or keep Avana paused, an address frozen or a breaker tripped beyond the normal delay of a role-change operation that removes them (`SPEC-GOV-006`). (Source: Q-136, Q-227, Q-228, Q-241, Q-242, Q-248, Q-263)
- `REQ-ACCESS-113` — No role MAY move user collateral, vault tokens or borrowed assets directly; the only fund-moving privileged selector is each LPVault's `returnStray`, a timelock call at the normal delay; no SettlementBudget exists (`Q-397`). An upgrade of the AaveAdapter changes the only code with custody power over proxy positions (`Q-223`), because every PositionProxy executes only the Adapter's calls; it waits the normal delay, every canceller can stop it, and its blast radius is bounded as in `REQ-ACCESS-083`. (Source: Q-104, Q-167, Q-223, Q-228, Q-397)
- `REQ-ACCESS-114` — The AvanaTimelock MUST NOT be upgradeable; `SHORT_DELAY`, `MIN_NORMAL_DELAY`, `MAX_DELAY` and `GRACE_PERIOD` are constants, `SHORT_ELIGIBLE` and the DAO-only cancel set are fixed by its constructor, and `getMinDelay()` always returns `SHORT_DELAY`. A new timelock can take over only through an upgrade of each Avana contract to an implementation whose `TIMELOCK` constant names it (`REQ-ACCESS-153`), at the normal delay and cancellable by every canceller. (Source: Q-226, Q-228, Q-259, Q-262)

## Timelock rules

- `REQ-ACCESS-140` — The AvanaTimelock MUST define `SHORT_DELAY = 43,200`, `MIN_NORMAL_DELAY = 345,600`, `MAX_DELAY = 432,000` and `GRACE_PERIOD = 1,209,600` seconds as constants and keep `normalDelay` within `[MIN_NORMAL_DELAY, MAX_DELAY]` (launch 432,000). These are the values of `Q-228` and `Q-262`: a normal delay of at least 4 and at most 5 days, a short delay of 12 hours, and a 14-day grace window. (Source: Q-228, Q-262)
- `REQ-ACCESS-141` — `schedule` and `scheduleBatch` MUST compute `requiredDelay(targets, payloads)` per `SPEC-GOV-001` from the stored short-delay list and revert `DelayBelowRequired` when `delay` is lower. The launch list is exactly the table in "Delay classes and timing", passed to the constructor with the deterministic deployment addresses of its targets, and equal at construction to `SHORT_ELIGIBLE` (`REQ-ACCESS-155`); it changes only through `setShortDelaySelector`, a normal-delay operation, can only remove pairs and add back pairs of `SHORT_ELIGIBLE`, and each launch entry's target is checked by the live-config check to hold deployed code exposing that selector. Checked by: MATH-GOV-01. (Source: Q-228, Q-262)

- `SPEC-GOV-013` — **Expiry and clean-up.** For a scheduled operation with `readyAt = getTimestamp(id) > 1` that is not done, `isOperationExpired(id) ⇔ block.timestamp ≥ readyAt + GRACE_PERIOD`. An operation is executable iff `readyAt ≤ t < readyAt + GRACE_PERIOD`, so at every `t ≥ readyAt` it is, as far as time is concerned, exactly one of executable or expired (an unexecuted predecessor or a caller without the executor right can still stop an execution inside the window). `cleanupExpiredOperation(id)` by anyone succeeds iff `isOperationExpired(id)`, makes `getTimestamp(id)` return 0 (state `Unset`, through `_cleaned[id]`), clears the role-change and restoring marks and emits `ExpiredOperationCleaned(id)`; otherwise it reverts `OperationNotExpired(id)`. A canceller may still `cancel` an expired operation. Units: seconds. No rounding. No overflow: the check is computed as `t ≥ readyAt ∧ t − readyAt ≥ GRACE_PERIOD`, so a `readyAt` near `2^256` (a proposer may choose any delay) never overflows and `isOperationExpired` never reverts. (Source: Q-228)
  - Why: the grace window stops a stale approval being used long after its context changed, and clean-up frees the identifier so the same call can be scheduled again in public, with a fresh delay, without waiting for a canceller.
  - Example: an operation ready at 1,780,432,000 is executable until 1,781,641,599. At 1,781,641,600 `execute` reverts `OperationExpired` and `isOperationExpired` returns true; anyone then calls `cleanupExpiredOperation(id)`, and the governance multisig may schedule the same calls again, ready no earlier than 432,000 s later. `cleanupExpiredOperation` at 1,781,000,000 reverts `OperationNotExpired`.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-04.
- `SPEC-GOV-014` — **Exit window before a change.** For an operation scheduled on chain `c` at `t_s` with `requiredDelay = normalDelay`, the exit window is `W = readyAt − t_s ≥ normalDelay`. A borrower's accounts on chain `c` are one PositionProxy per (CDP Spoke, market) it uses (`Q-280`); with `k` pledged positions spread over `m` markets and any number of debt reserves, it leaves with `k` transactions: per market one `repayAllAndWithdraw` that repays every debt reserve of that account and withdraws its first position, then one debt-free `withdraw` per remaining position, none of which reads the pause state or a RiskModule gate. The bound counts `k + m` transactions, one spare per market (a separate `repay` where a borrower prefers it). With block time `b_c` (12 s on Ethereum, 2 s on Base, one transaction per block), reaction time `r` and the chain's worst-case inclusion delay `q_c` while its sequencer is down (0 on Ethereum; on Base the parameter assumption `q_Base = 43,200` s, taken from the OP Stack sequencing window of Base's rollup configuration, 3,600 L1 blocks × 12 s, after which an L1 deposit transaction is forced into Base; [1.5](../01-architecture/05-parameters.md) lists it as an assumption and it is re-checked against Base's published configuration at each deployment and before any `normalDelay` change), leaving needs at most `r + q_c + b_c·(k + m)` seconds. Exit is guaranteed iff `r + q_c + b_c·(k + m) ≤ W`. Units: seconds; `k`, `m` counts. No rounding. No overflow. (Source: Q-227, Q-228, Q-280, Q-285, Q-287)
  - Why: the AaveAdapter holds custody power over proxy positions (every PositionProxy executes only its calls), so the protection against a harmful upgrade is that every borrower can leave first and every canceller can stop it. Each chain's timelock delays only that chain's upgrades, so the window is checked per chain with that chain's block time; on Base a sequencer outage can delay a borrower's transactions until forced inclusion, so the assumed forced-inclusion delay `q_Base` is part of the time to leave. The normal delay of 5 days covers a 3-day reaction time with room for thousands of withdrawals; the short delay of 12 hours would not, which is why upgrades and role changes can never be short (`REQ-ACCESS-142`), and why the normal delay never falls below 4 days (`SPEC-GOV-015`).
  - Example: with `normalDelay = 432,000` and a 3-day reaction (`r = 259,200`), an Ethereum borrower with 3 positions in 1 market needs `259,200 + 0 + 12 × 4 = 259,248 ≤ 432,000` seconds, and the bound holds for up to `k = ⌊172,800 / 12⌋ − 1 = 14,399` positions in one market. The same borrower on Base needs `259,200 + 43,200 + 2 × 4 = 302,408 ≤ 432,000`, and the bound holds for up to `k = ⌊129,600 / 2⌋ − 1 = 64,799`. At `W = 43,200` the 3-day reaction alone exceeds the window, so even one position could not leave first.
  - Checked by: MATH-GOV-05, MATH-GOV-06.
- `SPEC-GOV-015` — **Delay floor for the exit window.** Every normal delay a chain's timelock can reach satisfies `normalDelay ≥ MIN_NORMAL_DELAY = 345,600` s (`REQ-ACCESS-154`), and no upgrade or role selector can take the short delay (`REQ-ACCESS-142`). With the exit bound of `SPEC-GOV-014`, a borrower on chain `c` with `k` positions over `m` markets and reaction time `r` can leave before any normal-delay operation executes, at every reachable delay, iff `r + q_c + b_c × (k + m) ≤ MIN_NORMAL_DELAY`; for the design reaction `r = 259,200` s (3 days) and `m = 1` this holds for every `k ≤ k_max(c) = ⌊(MIN_NORMAL_DELAY − r − q_c) / b_c⌋ − m`: 7,199 on Ethereum (`b = 12`, `q = 0`) and 21,599 on Base (`b = 2`, `q = 43,200`). A cut of the delay is itself announced one old delay ahead (`SPEC-GOV-003`), so a borrower sees it at least 4 days before it applies. Units: seconds; `k`, `m` counts; `b_c` seconds per transaction (one per block). Rounding: `k_max` rounds down, so the guarantee is never overstated. No overflow (`k ≤ 2^64` keeps `b_c × (k + m)` far below `2^256`). (Source: Q-262, Q-285, Q-287)
  - Why: the exit guarantee rests on the normal delay exceeding a borrower's time to leave plus the cancellers' reaction. Without a floor one normal-delay operation could set the delay to 12 hours, after which a hostile upgrade would be ready before a borrower with a 3-day reaction could act. A 3-day floor would leave zero transactions of room for a 3-day reaction on Ethereum and a negative margin on Base once the assumed forced-inclusion delay `q_Base = 43,200` s is counted; 4 days leaves room for 7,200 transactions on Ethereum and 21,600 on Base.
  - Example: Ethereum, `r = 259,200`, `k = 1`, `m = 1`: `259,200 + 12 × 2 = 259,224 ≤ 345,600`, so even at the floor the borrower leaves with 86,376 seconds to spare. `k = 7,199`: `259,200 + 12 × 7,200 = 345,600 ≤ 345,600` holds; `k = 7,200` needs 345,612 s, which the floor no longer covers, although the launch delay of 432,000 s still does (up to `k = 14,399`). Base, `k = 21,599`, `m = 1`: `259,200 + 43,200 + 2 × 21,600 = 345,600` holds; `k = 21,600` needs 345,602 s. `setNormalDelay(259,200)` reverts `DelayOutOfBounds` on either chain.
  - Checked by: TEST-244, PROP-073.
