# 4.4 Design Decisions

> **Status:** In review · **Owner:** Avana Labs · **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-15

Every design decision behind Avana, each with its final resolution and the reasoning for it: the risk it controls or the failure it prevents. Decisions are grouped by topic. Each row states the decision as it stands now, citing the later decisions that shaped it; a decision that a later one replaced keeps its row as one line naming its replacement, so every `Q-NNN` referenced from the spec pages and the verification models resolves here. Q-IDs are stable. Where a decision states a parameter value, [1.5 Protocol Parameters](../01-architecture/05-parameters.md) holds the full table.

| ID | Decision | Resolution | Reasoning | Folds in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Q-279` | Chains, Hubs and instance scope | Avana runs on several chains, Hubs and AMM protocols. Launch: Ethereum and Base together, each with an Avana Stable LP Hub; the Correlated LP and Volatile LP Hub tiers can be added on any chain. Contract instances are scoped as follows. Per chain: ProxyManager, VaultTokenFactory, AvanaTimelock, AaveAdapter, LPVaultSwap, Collateralization (one AMM-neutral ledger), MarketRegistry, ParameterStore, RiskModule, PayoutEscrow (payout escrow only, `Q-401`), Lock, each DebtOracle, the `IFlashSource` implementation, and the Lens as a periphery contract; no SettlementBudget is deployed (`Q-397`). Per AMM protocol per chain: one immutable LPVault and one pricing library (or oracle implementation). Per market: one VaultToken and one LPOracle instance. Per (wallet, CDP Spoke, market): one PositionProxy. Every cap, budget, pause, breaker, route permission and emergency control is keyed by Hub, and by Spoke and market where it applies. The Lock has exactly two members, the AaveAdapter and LPVaultSwap; every LPVault and Collateralization entry point that moves value is reachable only through them. Hub-wide caps (token and pool share; there is no per-wallet cap or counter, `Q-412`) live in the one ledger keyed by Hub, so adding a Spoke never multiplies them. Supersedes `Q-005` (Ethereum only) and `Q-032` (one Hub); amends `Q-225`, `Q-240`. | Per-Hub copies of the Adapter and LPVaultSwap run the same code under the same timelock and Guardian, so they add deployments and upgrade calls without shrinking the blast radius. A vault and ledger per Spoke cannot join an immutable Lock deployed earlier. One ledger keyed by Hub keeps Hub-wide caps from multiplying with the number of Spokes. One Adapter per chain also removes cross-Hub proxy squatting, because only one contract can ask the ProxyManager for a proxy. | `Q-005`, `Q-032`. Resolves AMMN-ARCH-03, AMMN-FLOW-17, AMMN-RT-10, AMMN-FLOW-18, AMMN-RT-05, AMMN-ARCH-01, AMMN-FLOW-01, AMMN-GAS-09 |
| `Q-285` | Per-chain governance and pause scope | Each chain has its own AvanaTimelock, 4-of-7 governance Safe and 3-of-5 Emergency Guardian Safe (distinct signer sets per chain are allowed; the same rules apply). Cross-chain governance does not exist: an operation on one chain never changes another. The pause state, breakers and address freeze are held per Hub in the chain's MarketRegistry; `pauseAll(reason)` and `freezeAll()` change every Hub of the chain in one guardian call, and the address-freeze set is per chain, read by every Hub. Aave-side actions on Base go through the Aave DAO's Base executor, whose latency the runbook states. Amends `Q-114`, `Q-227`, `Q-228`, `Q-242`. | One code base serves every Hub on a chain, so a code incident must be containable in one call; per-Hub pause keeps a local incident local. A freeze that covered only one Hub would let a frozen address keep acting on others. | Resolves AMMN-FLOW-19 |
| `Q-287` | Per-chain parameters and minimum debt | Parameters that depend on the chain are set per chain and stated in 1.5: block time (Ethereum 12 s, Base 2 s), the sequencer-uptime feed and its grace period (Base feed `0xBCF85224fc0756B9Fa45aA7892530B47e10b6433`, grace 3,600 s; none on Ethereum), feed heartbeats (each feed's own, for example 86,400 s for Chainlink stablecoin feeds) and gas assumptions. At most `MAX_RECORDS_PER_ACCOUNT_MARKET` = 16 open records per account per market. | An uncapped record list lets the dust rule force a seizure of 125 records, about 31M gas, above the per-transaction gas limit; with at most 16 records, one liquidation of the largest account fits under that limit (`Q-381`). | `Q-169`, `Q-215`, `Q-277`. Resolves AMMN-ARCH-05, AMMN-GAS-01, AMMN-GAS-02, AMMN-FLOW-03 (scope) |
| `Q-311` | Minimum debt enforced at borrow | Superseded by `Q-381`. | — | Resolves AMMN-GAS-02 |
| `Q-324` | L2 sequencer-uptime gate with a grace period | Per chain `SequencerConfig {feed, gracePeriod}` (Base `0xBCF85224fc0756B9Fa45aA7892530B47e10b6433`, 3,600 s; Ethereum none). Down (`answer == 1`): seizure paused (`liquidationPaused`) and borrowing blocked. Restart: during `[startedAt, startedAt + gracePeriod)` borrowing is blocked and `inGrace` is true, so LPVaultSwap liquidates only below `graceHealthFactor`. Unreadable or unexpected feed: borrowing blocked, seizure not paused. The sequencer status never changes state, served price, accepted price or timers. The DebtOracle applies the same gate. | While the sequencer is down only L1-forced transactions run; borrowers cannot top up or repay, so seizing them is unfair and new debt would be priced on stale data. After restart borrowers need time to act. A broken uptime feed must never stop liquidation. | Resolves AMMN-FLOW-03 (1, 2), AMMN-INT-06 |
| `Q-326` | TWAP observation buffer per chain block time | `twapMinObservations = ⌈twapWindow / CHAIN_BLOCK_TIME⌉ + 1`, with `CHAIN_BLOCK_TIME` an implementation constant per chain (Ethereum 12 s → 151, Base 2 s → 901 for a 1,800 s window); `checkConfig` requires the cardinality and a successful `observe`; cardinality growth may be split over several transactions. The consistency check reads `observe` through the pricing module, never `slot0`. | k observations written one per block span (k − 1) × block time; a buffer sized for 12 s blocks covers 300 s on Base, so about $2 of dust swaps forces `OLD` and keeps the market Drifting. | Resolves AMMN-RT-08 |
| `Q-381` | No minimum debt; Avana's keepers pay liquidation gas | Avana has no minimum debt on any chain: a borrow of any amount above 0 that passes the other checks is accepted. `minDebtUsd`, `minDebtSafetyBps`, `BelowMinDebt`, `tightenMinDebtUsd` / `loosenMinDebtUsd`, the minimum-debt step of `preBorrowCheck`, the break-even sizing and every keeper-profitability claim are removed. Avana's own keepers, registered as committed operators (`Q-404`), liquidate every account below health factor 1 whatever its size, and Avana pays their gas as its own operating cost. Every address that is not frozen can also liquidate through the one `liquidate` entry point (`Q-404`). Liquidation never depends on a keeper making a profit. `SPEC-CHAIN-001` now states only the per-chain gas model `G(R) = G_base + R × g_record` (launch 1,310,000 + 240,000 × R gas on Ethereum and Base), which sizes the record cap and Avana's keeper gas budget; the gas price is a budget input, never a protocol value. The cap of 16 records per account per market stays because one liquidation of the largest account must fit the per-transaction gas limit: `G(16) = 1,310,000 + 240,000 × 16 = 5,150,000 ≤ 16,777,216`. Keeper gas is Avana's keeper cost; there is no SettlementBudget, and the caller pays any rounding gap at final settlement through the shortfall top-up (`Q-397`). Supersedes the minimum-debt parts of `Q-287`, `Q-311`, `Q-317`, `Q-362` and `Q-380`, and restores `Q-169` on minimum debt. Amends FINDING/AMMN-GAS-02 to: resolved by decision, no minimum, Avana's keeper pays gas. | A minimum debt only stops borrowers opening small loans: small debts still arise from partial repays, partial liquidations and price moves, so the gas cost it addressed exists either way. With Avana's own keepers liquidating every account, a small liquidation is a bounded operating cost of at most `G(16)` gas, not a reason for an account to go unliquidated. The one gas limit that can make an account unliquidatable is the per-transaction gas limit, and the record cap keeps the largest liquidation under half of it. | `Q-287`, `Q-311`, `Q-317`, `Q-362`, `Q-380`. Resolves AMMN-GAS-02 |
| `Q-357` | Chain-specific gates in every flow | Each chain's flows read the chain's sequencer gate (`Up`, `Down`, `Grace`) from its sequencer-uptime feed (Base `0xBCF85224fc0756B9Fa45aA7892530B47e10b6433`; Ethereum has none, so its gate is always `Up`). `Down`: borrow, withdraw-with-debt, new-account pledge, repay with collateral and liquidation revert `SequencerDown`; top-ups, repay, debt-free withdrawal and fee collection run. `Grace` (`gracePeriod` after restart, launch 3,600 s): borrow, withdraw-with-debt and new-account pledges revert `SequencerGrace`; liquidation runs only for accounts with health factor below `graceHealthFactor` (0.95); everything else runs. Every feed is fresh while its age is at most its own heartbeat plus `heartbeatMargin` (1,800 s), so a stablecoin feed with an 86,400 s heartbeat is fresh for 88,200 s (`Q-322`); borrow and withdraw-with-debt also require the independent second source to agree (`Q-323`). Time windows are in seconds, never blocks; on Base (2 s blocks) the TWAP buffer is sized for `twapWindow / 2 s` observations (`Q-326`) and the per-block quote-change cap applies per 2 s block. There is no minimum debt on any chain (`Q-381`). | After an outage, borrowers had no access while prices moved; liquidating at once would be unfair, and transactions forced in from L1 during the outage would act on stale prices. Day-long stablecoin heartbeats make a 3,600 s age bound unlistable, so freshness follows each feed, with a second source covering the long silent periods. | Resolves AMMN-FLOW-03, AMMN-INT-01 (flow level), AMMN-RT-08 (flow timing) |
